## What is known, and what is not

The designer found weather more alive than exposed geography, cloud separation above ground promising, and small settlement lights in need of exaggeration. These are observations, not an accumulating feature specification. Detailed imagery failed to belong; vector drawings also failed. This does not establish that fewer details, flatness, procedural generation or any particular rendering technique will solve the problem.

No whole appearance was accepted. Gravity is established danger. Atmospheric heating was exploratory; its existence, extent, damage, drag and speed dependence are not settled. Earth recognition, habitation, atmosphere and flight clarity remain connected uncertainties.

The designer stopped the study as looping, describing apparent model collapse through oversteering. The previous agent interpreted its repeated pivots as insufficiently grounded. Treat that interpretation as a hypothesis, not the diagnosis you are asked to confirm.

## Deliverable and stopping point

Return a concise, evidence-backed diagnosis with one direct visual comparison. Describe observable differences in material, light, edge treatment, spatial depth, scale or motion only where the examples support them. Identify which differences plausibly explain the mismatch and which remain uncertain. Include counterevidence: Mercury's composed material richness does not justify a blanket low-detail rule, and Venus's quiet body does not prescribe Earth's appearance.

Separate recorded designer decisions, direct observations and your interpretation. State what static examples cannot establish about motion. If the available evidence cannot support a diagnosis, say precisely what is missing; do not manufacture a visual rule or build a demo to compensate.

Finish by proposing at most one bounded decision worth investigating next, with why it follows from the diagnosis and the minimum evidence that could resolve it. Do not undertake that investigation. The designer will judge the diagnosis before authorizing any further design work.
