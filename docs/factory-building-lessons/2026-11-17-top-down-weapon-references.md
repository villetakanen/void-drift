# Keep weapon references in the game's viewing direction

Recorded 17 November 2026, from the Plasma Projector fitting study. The designer
identified the side-profile drift and explicitly supplied the intended basis:
top-down, fighter-jet- and tank-inspired space weapons.

## Evidence and finding

[Round 02.11](../../apps/web/src/content/lab/studies/plasma-projector-fitting-2026-11-17.mdx)
placed a long exposed insert and a protective cheek on a flat gun body. The
[retained SVG](../../apps/web/public/studies/plasma-projector-fitting-2026-11-17/031-open-throat.svg)
and its capture show the proposal that prompted the observation: it read as a
handheld rifle seen from the side, despite being mounted on a top-down ship.
The reference family had carried over a viewing angle and handheld anatomy,
beyond the intended inspiration for character and assembly.

The lesson is to distinguish a reference's useful qualities from its projection
and mounting context. Turning a side-profile composition upright does not make
it an overhead machine. Separate parts and correct colours alone did not resolve
that problem.

## Application in this study

Draft the weapon from above: establish its upper surfaces, lateral thickness,
overlaps, attachment and forward opening together. Use fighter-jet and tank
weapon construction as the vehicle context. Keep the Plasma Projector's own
space-weapon role; those references do not establish terrestrial weapon mechanics,
a new turret, or a mandatory silhouette. Add exposed internals and light within
that overhead construction.

Round 02.12 applies the clarification in a new SVG proposal. Whether that drawing
succeeds remains a design judgment. This record logs the requested lesson; it
does not claim that the correction has already produced a successful weapon or
change production artwork, living specifications or skills.
