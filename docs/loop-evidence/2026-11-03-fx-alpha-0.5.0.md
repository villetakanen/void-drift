# Arcade FX and alpha 0.5.0

## Intent and owner decisions

Investigate sluggish play and broken-looking engines after the FX change, implement
the shared effects framework and an agent skill, and release the whole reviewed
checkout as 0.5.0. The owner explicitly authorized tests, verification, commit,
push, publication and a ready-for-playtest notification, while preserving concurrent
work on studies, design-system pages and Lab layouts.

The owner prioritised sharp, sparkly, electric 90s rendering and the expressive
quirks of cheap methods. Whole-scene FX targets p95 under 3 ms, with 60 Hz preferred
and 30 Hz acceptable under heavier load. Technology is replaceable. This was a
bug/architecture/development task, not a new design study. Appearance equality is
not the effects contract. The owner accepted the written direction; that is not
acceptance of the finished effects' feel.

## Checks and observations

- Targeted checks exposed and repaired a component props type error, a stale Lab
  counter and a historical shadow painter running through the new blend path.
  Historical comparison painting retains its compatibility path and loads when visible.
- First full run failed three browser cases: old saves omitted the optional linked
  setting and Switch rejected an undefined binding; a label-text locator mismatched
  the shared control; the Lab pane could receive a click before hydration. The
  saved-value adapter, accessible-role locator and readiness state fixed those cases.
  All three then passed in a targeted run. No assertion timeout was increased.
- Failed full report:
  `artifacts/verification/2026-11-03T12-14-05.598Z-MmG7UW/report.json`.
  Its other gates passed; failure traces were copied into
  `artifacts/release-0.5.0/first-browser-results/` before subsequent runs.
- Final full report:
  `artifacts/verification/2026-11-03T12-18-54.764Z-ILQGBW/report.json`.
  All gates passed, source unchanged: 144 unit tests, 36 browser tests, 10 rules
  checks, one multi-player competition test, five factory checks, contracts,
  commit validation, lint, types and build. It tested a dirty working candidate;
  the release record distinguishes its fingerprint from the later commit identity.
- Two corrected sustained profiles each ran for 60 seconds after warmup on an
  Apple M4 Max, Chromium 156.0.8078.4, 1920×1080 backing canvas. Desktop/mobile-emulated
  rolling p95 update-plus-submission ranged 1.1–1.5 / 1.1–1.8 ms. Largest recorded
  window maxima were 7.8 / 7.1 ms; p95/max frame intervals were 16.8 ms. Samples and
  trace categories/limitations are in the release record and
  `artifacts/fx-profile/2026-11-03T12-10-23.645Z/report.json`.
- Earlier profiling was rejected as final evidence after a development reload and
  offscreen ships were observed. The corrected fixture keeps six ships visible.
  No physical phone or isolated GPU/compositor timing was measured. Individual
  raster/paint trace events cannot be summed as whole-pipeline FX cost.
- Live smoke initially failed to click a Toggle radio under its visible label.
  The probe was corrected to use the visible label and assert the resulting state;
  production source was unchanged. The completed smoke passed with no page errors.

## Evidence gaps

Physical-device performance, attributable whole-pipeline FX cost and the owner's
judgment of layering, sparkle, sharpness, readability and fun remain open. Station,
missile and fire activity in the Lab is a workload fixture, not implemented combat.
The initial feasibility benchmark is not physical-device acceptance. Local ignored
traces/reports/screenshots and the temporary release checkout are not durable
backups; this record retains their essential results. No general lesson or standing
process change is inferred by this closure.
