# Plasma vent-area damage · 18 November 2026

**Disposition.** The designer stopped this loop during geometry review, saying “we are in an over-correction loop.” Terra implementation and Sol review were interrupted. The vent-area draft remains uncommitted in the shared checkout. No vent-area commit, push, deployment or live verification took place.

**Starting point.** Commit `424fefb7` on `feat/0-7-0` had already been pushed. It unified paired vent emission and backward thrust after a shot or a lost charging target. The designer had accepted the visible effect with “now it looks as it should.” That feedback concerned the vents before area damage was added.

**Scope.** The requested addition was 3 hull damage per entry into either spray, counting uninterrupted contact across overlapping sprays once, with equal behavior after either trigger. The designer ruled out one gameplay object per visual bubble and later asked to revisit geometry. The separate firing kick was outside this loop. See [study section 02.9](../../apps/web/src/pages/lab/studies/plasma-rifle-operation-2026-11-18.mdx) and the [draft contract](../specs/game/plasma-rifle/spec.md).

**Local work.** The draft added moving spray envelopes, contact history, combat integration and tests. The implementation is visible in the working-tree changes to the rifle spec, salvage schema and simulation, targeting bench and plasma tests, including the untracked plasma-vent-area module and its test. There is no immutable revision of this draft.

**Checks and review.** On the dirty checkout above, the observed command `pnpm exec vitest run packages/mode-a/src/lib/plasma-vent-area.test.ts` passed six geometry cases. Terra separately reported 11 passing vent tests, 41 passing Gauntlet and plasma stream/cycle/acquisition tests, and a passing TypeScript check during development. Sol identified a case the six geometry tests missed: merging consecutive emission spans can erase an intermediate change in outlet velocity and move the inferred damage area away from already emitted gas. Terra also reported that fragments created by vent damage were first eligible for a vent hit on a later substep. No final review approval, complete-suite result or before/after damage-performance measurement was recorded. The earlier vent-rendering benchmark measured a different change.

**Resumed at the designer’s request.** The stop instruction was intended for another chat. On the resumed working tree, the damage envelope retains bends and detaches from the turning emitter. Merging adjacent trajectory spans allows at most 0.25 u of estimated centreline deviation. A geometry probe on a turning Wasp found six paired spans halfway through emission, with nine still active 150 ms after closure. Damage uses the same outlet motion for either trigger. Sol reproduced a queued hit against a newly removed wreck shard. The corrected sweep rechecks changed wreck material and evaluates newborn fragments from their time of birth. A production combat regression covers this case.

**Resumed verification.** On the uncommitted checkout based on `424fefb7`, `pnpm exec vitest run` passed 366 tests in 71 files and `pnpm exec tsc -p tsconfig.check.json --noEmit` passed. A local simulation-only A/B used the sustained mixed three-loop bench, two plasma slots, six measured orbits after two warmups, and alternating baseline/candidate order. Across 294 simultaneous-vent ticks per build, mean simulation time per 60 Hz tick went from 0.161 to 0.224 ms and p95 from 0.189 to 0.272 ms. These timings exclude drawing. No commit, push or deployment was requested in the resumed work.
