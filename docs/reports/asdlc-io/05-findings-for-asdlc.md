# Findings for developing ASDLC.io

These are analyst findings for review, not adopted framework policy. Confidence describes the supported case claim; it does not imply universal generalisation. Each finding separates evidence from the proposed documentary implication. Case identifiers link to [the case studies](04-case-studies.md); guidance and evidence IDs resolve through [the register](01-method-and-sources.md).

## F-01 — A design-led factory needs an explicit inquiry mode

**Claim.** In this case, a study's decisive output was often a property or alternative made judgeable through a comparison. A correct artifact or polished problem statement could still leave the question unanswered. C-02–04 and C-08; E-D03–07, E-L01–03.

**Guidance relation.** Double Diamond and Requirement Review address framing; Learning Loop legitimises discovery; Experience Modeling protects a defined visual vocabulary. The case connects these activities through a concrete inquiry instrument rather than a simple document handoff. G-D05/07/08/10.

**Counterevidence and alternative explanations.** The button pilot and later derelict comparisons show useful inquiry and decisions. Task scope, presentation capability, context retrieval and interaction interpretation may all matter; internal model causes are unknown. The records do not establish that a new phase label improves outcomes.

**Rationale and confidence.** The designer explicitly identified scope and unanswered questions in selected episodes. Confidence is high that these distinctions mattered in those records; transfer to other domains is tentative.

**Framework implication.** Add a bounded inquiry example that identifies the decision, accepted constraints, remaining alternatives, what observation could distinguish them, and legitimate stop/rule-out outcomes. Avoid turning inquiry into another mandatory questionnaire. P-02.

## F-02 — Shared definitions and exploratory invention have different permissions

**Claim.** The project combines shared visual/behavior definitions with deliberate exploration that can extend them. Not every composed value is a global token, and using a shared renderer does not make an instrument identical to the game. C-01/04/08; E-D01–02 and E-R05.

**Guidance relation.** Experience Modeling's strict consumption boundary explains part of this architecture but under-describes how a new vocabulary is investigated and promoted. Its separate model-change route already acknowledges change. G-D05.

**Counterevidence.** The records also describe bypassing existing shared styles and inventing components unnecessarily; exploration cannot be used to rationalise every local variant. The present source sample establishes specific reuse, not universal conformity.

**Rationale and confidence.** Token ownership and accepted promotions are recorded designer choices. Confidence is high for the local distinction, moderate for the proposed framework extension.

**Framework implication.** Document exploration, approved definition change, consumer integration and retired apparatus as separate states; include procedural/Canvas visuals alongside conventional UI components. P-03.

## F-03 — Gate evidence needs a claim, candidate and scope

**Claim.** The runner/release history demonstrates why pass/fail alone is insufficient: partial runs, source mutation, process timeout semantics and deployment versus live checks materially change what can be asserted. C-05/06; E-G02–04 and E-R03–04.

**Guidance relation.** Context Gates gives the taxonomy; Provenance already includes intent. The case supplies an implementable evidence shape and a distinction between integrity enforcement and procedural permission. G-G05/08/12.

**Counterevidence.** Hashes do not prove tests are adequate, and durable summaries cannot recover expired raw traces. The runner itself previously misreported a timeout. No new full verification was performed for this assessment.

**Rationale and confidence.** Code and retained run records support the mechanism and selected historical failures. High confidence within this case; no universal minimum gate set follows.

**Framework implication.** Add a worked evidence envelope and a claim-state example separating partial/full, prepared/deployed/live, plus retention availability. P-04.

## F-04 — Human authority should be described by decision, not only by position in a pipeline

**Claim.** Human direction occurs during exploration, scope changes, lesson promotion and publication decisions. A single final acceptance box does not express those different authorities. Current repository rules explicitly keep people's playtesting outside its process. E-R02/04, E-D01/05, E-L06–07.

**Guidance relation.** Autonomy and Context Gates describe human governance, while assembly diagrams often place acceptance near merge. These are compatible only if the particular decision and artifact are stated. G-G05/09–11.

**Counterevidence.** The current guidance already gives humans strategic and architectural roles. This finding is about operational precision, not absence of human governance in ASDLC. No claim about the suitability of this project's publication policy is made.

**Rationale and confidence.** Explicit instructions and recorded decisions support the authority boundaries. High case confidence; organisation-specific allocation remains contextual.

**Framework implication.** Show an authority table: decision, actor, scope, evidence and enforcement mechanism. Distinguish subjective judgment from a mechanically enforced transition. P-05.

## F-05 — Evidence collection, interpretation, adoption and efficacy are separate lifecycle states

**Claim.** The case repeatedly preserves approved procedure changes while saying effectiveness remains untested. Closure records deliberately collect observations without automatically producing lessons. Later failures coexist with adopted guidance. C-05/07/09; E-L04/06/07 and E-D05.

**Guidance relation.** Compound Loop already requires discrimination and human approval; Agent Optimization already discusses held-out validation. The case offers a practical intermediate lifecycle that avoids calling approved instructions validated learning. G-C01–03.

**Counterevidence.** Repeated failure after adoption does not prove the amendment was ineffective: scopes, agents and contexts differ. Absence of a comparative trial also does not imply the change was unjustified.

**Rationale and confidence.** The separation is explicit in current procedure and episode records. High confidence in the distinction; effectiveness of individual amendments remains unresolved.

**Framework implication.** Add a small lesson record with observation, interpretation, alternatives, approval, implementation and validation status. Permit useful closure with no lesson candidate. P-06.

## F-06 — Delegation and critique need fidelity checks as well as bounded context

**Claim.** Selected reviewer and digest outputs required factual or interpretive correction. A compact answer can retain the wrong framing while appearing decisive. C-07–09; E-D07–08, E-L06–07 and E-R07–08.

**Guidance relation.** External Attention focuses on isolation and bounded answers; review guidance already warns about capability and shared blind spots. A provenance-bearing extraction contract makes those cautions operational. G-C04–05, G-G06–07.

**Counterevidence.** Delegation also supplied useful evidence and bounded reading. There is no complete retained transcript set or direct-reading baseline, so neither error rates nor comparative cost/quality can be inferred.

**Rationale and confidence.** Corrections are explicit in records; broader causes are unknown. Moderate confidence in generalisability of the reporting safeguard, not a model ranking.

**Framework implication.** Require packet-level source anchors, coverage, observation/interpretation labels, contrary evidence and unknowns in an example workflow. Reserve material interpretation for synthesis and review. P-07.

## F-07 — Guidance consistency and applicability matter more than template conformity

**Claim.** Current guidance has an observable tension between source-code metaphors and explicit rejection of spec-as-source. It also combines broad onboarding and draft assembly instructions with detailed proportionality caveats. G-G01–03/09/14, G-D07. The project itself uses functional equivalents such as plans and recorded decisions rather than proving adoption of every named artifact.

**Counterevidence.** Live detailed guidance is often already nuanced: living specs are hypotheses, templates are adaptable, and several review/workflow practices say when to skip them. The critique should strengthen consistency, not add those same ideas as if missing.

**Rationale and confidence.** High confidence in the textual tension, moderate in the reader-confusion hypothesis. No historical failure is causally attributed to the wording.

**Framework implication.** Reconcile onboarding/experimental metaphors with the current spec-anchored position and link applicability before prescriptive examples. P-01 and P-08.

## F-08 — Retained evidence and retained machinery have different lifespans

**Claim.** The writing/closure decision preserves useful records while allowing temporary study components to retire. Elsewhere, summaries outlive ignored or temporary logs, limiting reconstruction. Shared-tree commits also show that commit identity and session ownership are different. C-06/09; E-L07, E-G/C-G05, G-C07/10.

**Counterevidence.** Some instruments remain useful and are explicitly retained; deleting them would lose capability. No maintenance savings were measured. Micro-Commits already recommends isolated worktrees, so this is an example and clarification, not a newly discovered omission.

**Rationale and confidence.** Recorded retention decisions and provenance limits support the distinction. High case confidence; retention policy is context-dependent.

**Framework implication.** Add a lifecycle table for temporary state, durable evidence, reusable tools and standing instructions, with provenance and scope carried across transitions. P-09.

## What this case cannot establish

It cannot establish a productivity multiplier, a model-tier optimum, the causal effect of ASDLC adoption, enterprise-scale safety, a universal factory architecture, or the effectiveness of each adopted skill. Shared authorship and possible co-evolution reduce independence. Missing runtime prompt composition, transcripts and some historical artifacts limit causal reconstruction. The final proposals are review inputs supported by a rich single case, not validated universal rules.
