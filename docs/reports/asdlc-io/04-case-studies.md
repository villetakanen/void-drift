# Case studies: outcomes and their limits

These are deliberately selected episodes, not a representative rate sample. The [method](01-method-and-sources.md) records the full 69-record metadata inventory and extraction scope. Cases reuse evidence IDs from the three project packets; overlapping records are one event, not multiple confirmations. A source's historical delivery status is preserved as a statement about that time.

## C-01 — The button pilot: shared vocabulary is useful, but must be discovered

The early button study used concrete compositions and designer corrections, then promoted an accepted baseline into shared styles and application consumers. The initial examples recreated rectangular buttons despite existing angled styles; category framing and spacing also changed after inspection. The retrospective records a useful method and integration, while explicitly limiting generalisation from one pilot. [Pilot retrospective](../../factory-experiment-design-studies.md), E-D02.

**Framework implication:** Experience Modeling describes the value of a common vocabulary. This case adds the work of discovering, correcting and extending that vocabulary, including the distinction between global tokens and component-private values. It does not justify making every visual number a token. The designer's recorded decisions explain the choices; claims about lasting productivity would be inference.

## C-02 — Planetary inquiry: local usefulness did not settle the umbrella question

Two early cycles failed in different ways: premature invented situations and a restart that clarified intent without producing a useful inquiry. A subsequent planet-purpose cycle produced useful comparisons and partial decisions but was still judged unsuccessful overall because the subject was too broad. The records preserve both judgments. [Planetary closure](../../loop-evidence/2026-10-29-planetary-design-study.md), [purpose/scope account](../../factory-experiment-planet-purpose-scope.md), E-D03–05 and E-L01.

**Framework implication:** Discover/Define requires a question whose alternatives can actually be distinguished by the proposed examples. More questions, a correctly structured document and technically valid demonstrations are not interchangeable with that condition. The recorded scope diagnosis is stronger evidence than an invented model-internal explanation. No controlled comparison establishes that a particular wording change would have prevented failure.

## C-03 — Ion-stream and utilities failures: technical validity and inquiry outcome diverged

The ion-stream record reports repaired hydration, passing intermediate checks and a bounded positive response, followed by an overall failure judgment. The utilities record retains some construction preferences but says only part of the intended scope was explored; causal explanations remain hypotheses. [Ion-stream record](../../loop-evidence/2026-10-31-ion-stream-failure.md), [utilities record](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-02-utilities-failed-study.md), E-L02–03.

**Framework implication:** A useful outcome vocabulary needs to preserve unsuccessful, stopped and inconclusive inquiries without converting a local preference or passing test into acceptance. These cases do not show that automated checks were useless: they established narrower technical properties. Nor do they demonstrate that persistence would eventually have resolved the design question.

## C-04 — Derelict correction and acceptance: restoring evidence mattered

The correction analysis describes a lost ability to compare live game-state changes; discussion shifted to presentation mechanics and explanations. It explicitly notes that the existing procedure already required concrete examples. A later study records useful fixed-scale comparisons and bounded visual choices, while preserving earlier negative feedback. [Correction analysis](../../factory-building-lessons/2026-11-06-derelict-correction-loop.md), [third closure](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-06-derelict-three-closure.md), E-R06 and E-D06.

**Framework implication:** Distinguish missing guidance, failure to apply guidance, unsuitable comparison instruments and unsuitable inquiry scope before prescribing a new agent rule. The later acceptance is a useful counterexample to an account of uniform failure; it does not isolate why recovery occurred or validate every adopted procedure.

## C-05 — Factory amendment: a working mechanism is a narrower result than a better method

The 3 November amendment delivered release-preparation machinery and clarified instruction/evidence ownership. Its first smoke assertion expected a stale control label; the record retains the failure and correction. The checks establish bounded mechanisms and actual preparation evidence. The record explicitly does not claim a fresh study established less repetition or better reporting. [Harness amendment](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-03-factory-harness-amendments.md), E-G/C-G01 and E-L04.

**Framework implication:** Agent Optimization and Compound Loop examples benefit from separate states for authorized, implemented, mechanically checked and empirically evaluated changes. This is an extension of the guidance's existing validation cautions, not a claim that it currently endorses unreviewed writeback.

## C-06 — Verification and release: identity is part of the claim

The 0.5.4 record preserves fixture failures and later stable-candidate verification, preparation, publication and live evidence. The 0.5.6 record describes a timeout that the runner had misreported, followed by a runner correction and later full pass. The 0.6.1 record distinguishes sandbox/environment failures and shared-tree mutation from a later isolated clean candidate. [0.5.4](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-05-interstellar-arrival-release.md), [0.5.6](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-08-release-0.5.6.md), [0.6.1](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-11-gauntlet-two-pirates-release.md), C-G03–05.

**Framework implication:** A gate verdict needs candidate identity, stage scope, completion semantics and evidence retention. The current runner and release functions embody these distinctions (E-R03–04). This provides a concrete worked example for Context Gates and Provenance. Historical passes do not certify the current repository or complete correctness.

## C-07 — Retrospective: extraction and interpretation can redirect the work

The 8 November record distinguishes subagent digests from directly checked facts. The designer corrected a guessed tooling cause to the scope of design questions and stated that the retrospective's purpose was understanding. It also records deliberate CI removal after investigation and discussion; the stronger claim that it was uncommissioned remains attributed rather than independently reconstructed. [Retrospective actions](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-08-retro-and-actions.md), E-L06 and E-R08.

**Framework implication:** External Attention needs traceable extracts and an interpretation boundary. A digest should not silently choose the research agenda or turn an observation into remediation. This case does not establish that delegation is inferior to direct reading; its retained gaps limit causal attribution.

## C-08 — Point defence and ship slots: review is evidence, not an oracle

The accepted point-defence study still received later factual/context corrections and did not itself integrate production behavior. The ship-slot study ended inconclusive after a rubric review and feedback-handling problems; the record includes reviewer factual mistakes. [Point defence](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-09-ion-point-defence-study.md), [ship slots](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-11-ship-slots-study.md), E-D07–08.

**Framework implication:** Reviewer output should preserve source references, checked properties, uncertainty and disputed claims. These examples support ASDLC's existing warning that critic capability needs validation; they do not quantify error rates or invalidate adversarial review generally.

## C-09 — Writing and study closure: preserving evidence need not preserve every tool

The 11 November amendment was reviewed item by item. The designer redirected the last proposal toward retiring temporary study apparatus while preserving durable prose/static evidence. The record distinguishes approval, guidance edits and structural validation from migration or a trial proving effectiveness. It also records correction of a subagent pass that treated stopped work as a quality defect. [Writing/closure record](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-11-design-study-writing-and-closure.md), E-L07 and E-R07.

**Framework implication:** Context Offloading and Compound Loop can distinguish temporary execution state, durable evidence, current reusable instruments and standing guidance. This is a documented retention decision, not measured evidence that maintenance cost fell.

## C-10 — The latest star-family endpoints: decisions do not erase scope boundaries

The two 12 November records separate a fixed Practice star decision from a wider seeded-family trial. They state what was inspected, which checks were reported, and which revisions or final views were not established. Their then-uncommitted status is historical: both records now exist in the assessment's baseline commit. [Practice G7](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-12-practice-g7-sun.md), [yellow family](../../loop-evidence/2026-11-12-yellow-family-flight-trial.md), E-L08 and E-L10.

**Framework implication:** Acceptance and evidence should carry the properties and modes they cover. A decision in one mode is not general acceptance of related modes. This recent case checks that the earlier outcome distinctions remain relevant near the cutoff; it does not constitute a new gameplay evaluation.
