# Challenge pass — design, specification, and context claims

Scope: challenge reports 02–06 for design/spec guidance fit, evidence traceability, factual scope and causal language. Project claims below were checked against baseline `49336a6e5675da1b50b7179f3baaf28f6d3a7bb4`; ASDLC propositions were checked against the 12 November capture set and cited page status. This packet records review issues and supporting counterevidence; it does not edit the assessment reports.

## Findings

### CH-D01 — “Spec as source code” is a real wording tension, but not evidence of operational contradiction

Reports 03 and P-01 describe an “inconsistency”; report 05 more carefully calls it a “tension.” The captured **Live** pages distinguish the spec as source of truth for architectural intent/contracts from deterministic code as runtime authority (`patterns/the-spec/`, “Definition”; `concepts/spec-driven-development/`, “Levels of SDD Adoption” and “Spec-as-Source”). The **Experimental** Agentic Double Diamond says “the Spec becomes the primary source code” and “Spec Writing IS Coding,” but its Phase 4 describes agents assembling code/tests from the spec. Getting Started repeats the metaphor. This supports a reader-facing ambiguity if “source code” is read literally; it does not establish that the framework's intended runtime authority is inconsistent. Preserve the clearer term **textual tension/ambiguity** and its experimental maturity rather than treating it as a proven contradiction or project-caused failure. Report 05 already handles this fairly; 03's “directly inspectable guidance inconsistency” and 06's “inconsistency” are stronger than the underlying conclusion.

### CH-D02 — Design-inquiry conclusion is bounded by a deliberately selected sample

F-01 says a study's decisive output was “often” a property or alternative made judgeable through comparison. The case file explicitly says episodes are selected and not a representative rate sample (`04-case-studies.md:3`). The button, planetary-purpose, and derelict episodes do support that comparison-based decisions occurred (`04-case-studies.md:7,13,25`; primary/retrospective sources below). “Often” reads as a frequency claim across studies; the inspected subset and lack of denominator do not support it. Read as “in several sampled studies,” the finding is supported. Report 04's process implication—that examples should distinguish alternatives—remains an interpretation for framework authors, not proof that every inquiry requires a controlled visual comparison.

### CH-D03 — P-02 is a cross-case teaching composite, not one recorded episode

P-02 labels its table “Example drawn from the case, simplified for teaching,” but fields combine different episodes: the derelict closure names supplied artwork, production ship painter, and fixed-scale comparisons (`loop-evidence/2026-11-06-derelict-three-closure.md:8-26,46-64`); planetary sources concern gravity, orbit and body scale (`factory-experiment-planet-purpose-scope.md:26-32,39-63`); ship slots concern markers for parts outside scored rules (`loop-evidence/2026-11-11-ship-slots-study.md:14-22,46-89`). The table's accepted art, “real ship/renderer,” uncertain size/state transition and “intended object-state change” therefore should not be attributed to one study. Its proposed use is plausible, but mark it as a cross-case composite/illustrative synthesis and identify which episode supports each field. That keeps the worked example from implying more settled intent or common instrumentation than the sources record.

### CH-D04 — Primary evidence is strong for recent studies, thinner for the button pilot

The planetary records retain direct sequence and closure evidence, including the failed restart (`loop-evidence/2026-10-29-planetary-design-study.md:14-45,110-158`) and planet-purpose decisions plus cancelled integration (`loop-evidence/2026-10-29-planet-purpose-scope.md:9-30`; `factory-experiment-planet-purpose-scope.md:20-37,39-63`). Derelict, ship-slot and point-defence closure records also preserve dated decisions, limits, and working-tree provenance (`loop-evidence/2026-11-06-derelict-three-closure.md:1-64`; `loop-evidence/2026-11-11-ship-slots-study.md:1-10,24-89`; `loop-evidence/2026-11-09-ion-point-defence-study.md:1-8,21-37,89-99`). By contrast, E-D02/C-01 relies chiefly on `docs/factory-experiment-design-studies.md`, a retrospective. It records designer approval and described integration (`:12-29`) and concrete corrections (`:51-93,107-118`), but no independent transcript or separate contemporaneous loop record was identified in the cited evidence. The button page/code may corroborate the resulting artifact; it cannot independently establish the conversation's sequence or the retrospective's causal interpretation. The report appropriately treats it as one pilot and rejects general efficacy, but should keep that provenance distinction explicit.

### CH-D05 — The Experience Modeling comparison is fair when kept narrow

F-02/P-03 say the page under-describes vocabulary creation and promotion. The captured Live page already gives a separate human-governed model-change route and offers hard isolation versus toolchain enforcement, so it would be inaccurate to characterize the page as permitting unrestricted feature-local invention (`patterns/experience-modeling/`, “3. Read-Only Enforcement (The Governance)”). The project sources likewise route definition changes to separate design work and require canonical Lab demonstration (`docs/specs/factory/design-system/spec.md:21-35`; `AGENTS.md`, shared-component guidance). The narrower claim is supportable: the page explains consumption and enforcement more concretely than discovery, revision, approval and promotion of a new shared visual/behavior vocabulary. Treat this as a possible example gap, not proof that experience modeling excludes design exploration or that every Canvas system needs the project's architecture.

### CH-D06 — The design-study episodes support distinctions, not attribution to current ASDLC review patterns

The reports mostly preserve this limit. The planetary restart record says the agent was following explicit instructions, records the designer's suspected prompt/skill cause, and says exact prompt composition was unavailable (`docs/factory-experiment-planetary-study.md:110-158`). The planet-purpose record says the designer judged the method useful but the subject too broad, while the retrospective disclaims a controlled test (`docs/factory-experiment-planet-purpose-scope.md:18-37,65-85,99-108`). Recent derelict acceptance retains earlier negative feedback (`docs/loop-evidence/2026-11-06-derelict-three-closure.md:1-6,19-44,98-107`). Ship slots and point defence show reviewer/prose factual errors and later corrections, but closure snapshots were dirty shared trees and several checks were not run (`loop-evidence/2026-11-11-ship-slots-study.md:3-6,30-45,108-128`; `loop-evidence/2026-11-09-ion-point-defence-study.md:39-87,89-145`). These are good counterexamples to treating rubric passes or acceptance as design truth; they do not estimate reviewer error rates, prove the guidance caused the failures, or evaluate the experimental Requirement Review page, which was not shown to have been used as a procedure in those studies.

## Disposition

No unsupported project remediation or general causal claim was found in the design-focused synthesis. The material wording cautions are CH-D01–03; CH-D04–06 are provenance and transfer limits already substantially acknowledged in reports 02–06. The strongest synthesis holds if it distinguishes a literal-reading ambiguity in experimental guidance from an intended contradiction, describes sampled episodes without frequency implication, and identifies P-02 as a cross-case example.
