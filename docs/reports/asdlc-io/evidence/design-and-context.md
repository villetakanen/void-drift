### E-D01 — Design intent, ownership, and current study contract

- **Sources:** `docs/design.md:6-12,14-35`; `docs/architecture.md:6-11,24-43,81-100`; `docs/specs/lab/spec.md:5-24,27-44`; `docs/specs/lab/design-studies/spec.md:5-23,25-55,57-97`; `.agents/skills/design-study/SKILL.md:8-18,21-39,58-91,94-125`; `.agents/skills/shared-components/SKILL.md:8-37,39-57`; `.agents/skills/spec/SKILL.md:21-46,84-98`.
- **Type:** declared intent, architecture/contract, and operating procedure.
- **Factual extraction:** Experience principles state desired play qualities and distinguish them from exact component behavior; material principle changes require explicit agreement. Architecture assigns physics, camera, input, procedural drawing and shared styles to Core; Mode A owns the simulation; Lab and game use the same simulation, camera, configuration and drawing code. The Lab spec characterizes it as designer tooling that displays/reuses systems and does not fork game behavior. The design-study contract separates problem, exploration, readiness and scope; treats uncertainty as a normal input; distinguishes desired intent from feasibility evidence; states that acceptance does not itself authorize implementation or publication; and separates rendered correctness from human usefulness. The skills assign workflow ownership and direct inspection of current relevant code, production/Lab consumers, accepted constraints, readiness and slice sizing.
- **Limits:** These sources show the intended context routes and authority boundaries, not that an agent loaded the right context in a given cycle. They also do not establish that the shared systems actually answer every design question. The spec skill has a suggested generic outline including “Definition of Done” checkboxes at lines 48-82, while current project instructions require acceptance criteria to be plain intent statements; this is a cross-document format tension, not evidence of a particular spec being authored incorrectly.

### E-D02 — Early button study: accepted design translated into shared UI

- **Sources:** `docs/factory-experiment-design-studies.md:12-29,31-47,51-82,107-118`; `.agents/skills/shared-components/SKILL.md:13-37,49-57`; `docs/specs/factory/design-system/spec.md:7-35`.
- **Type:** retrospective with recorded designer decisions and described delivered implementation; current shared-system contract.
- **Factual extraction:** The retrospective records a conversation-driven button-hierarchy pilot that progressed through explanation, interactive comparison, corrections, token audit, acceptance and integration. It states the accepted outcome became the canonical `/lab/buttons` page and shared styles used by the application. The designer endorsed the situation-first explanation and approved the baseline and promotion. Recorded corrections changed the initial category framing, restored the existing angled styles, shifted the comparison to realistic menu groups versus action strips, reduced spacing from 16px to 8px, and kept some component-specific values private rather than global tokens. Integration addressed shared focus/disabled/selected/motion behavior and settings persistence.
- **Limits:** This is one successful pilot and its retrospective. The account itself says acceptance is not proof of fit for every problem. It reports verification costs and some candidate lessons but does not independently measure general workflow effectiveness. Existing shared-system contract documents where the current design-system home and consumers belong; it does not independently verify this historical episode.

### E-D03 — Two planetary inquiry cycles: context and method mismatch

- **Sources:** `docs/factory-experiment-planetary-study.md:11-49,79-108,110-158`; `docs/loop-evidence/2026-10-29-planetary-design-study.md:9-20,22-45` (baseline source; episode sequence and check record); `.agents/skills/design-study/SKILL.md:12-39,45-56`.
- **Type:** retrospective interpretation plus retained episode record; rationale provenance is mixed.
- **Factual extraction:** The retrospective records two cycles closed as unsuccessful. In the first, a topic about planetary bodies was developed into agent-supplied situations, presets, and a page before the designer considered the inquiry's purpose shared. In the restart, the prompt explicitly asked for grounded questions, reflected understanding, and no presets/page before a shared problem statement. The designer supplied intended roles (ship-dependent slingshots, scale/effort to reach, collision and atmosphere hazards) and an unknown about survival; the record says the agent continued asking questions, delayed page work until asked, then documented intent without starting an investigation. The designer said current-game appearance did not answer the normative design question. Retrospective interpretation: the workflow left discovery less concrete than artifact production. The record explicitly says no single prompt/skill cause is proved; the designer's attribution to prompt/skill shaping is a hypothesis, and no exact runtime prompt composition was captured. The historical page/presets were subsequently removed; they are not current design artifacts.
- **Limits:** The detailed causal framing is retrospective, not an independent experiment. The counterevidence is explicit: the prior skill already called for clarification of consequential ambiguity. The retrospective states that no wording change had yet been tested against a fresh cycle. Later lessons should not count the two cycles as independent causal proof of one mechanism.

### E-D04 — Planet-purpose cycle: useful method, overbroad scope

- **Sources:** `docs/factory-experiment-planet-purpose-scope.md:3-37,39-63,65-85,87-108,110-122`; `docs/loop-evidence/2026-10-29-planet-purpose-scope.md:9-30` (episode record); `docs/factory-building-lessons/002-phased-design-studies.md:37-77`.
- **Type:** designer's recorded closure judgment and retained examples; retrospective interpretation.
- **Factual extraction:** The designer explicitly judged the whole study unsuccessful but the method useful; diagnosed the subject as too large for one study. The cycle produced a framing page, numbered sections, concrete examples, corrections and retained comparisons. Recorded partial outcomes include a selected gravity profile candidate, entry-momentum comparisons, nested planet/moon motion, and corrected separation of body size from gravity reach. The retrospective lists successive topics (encounter reward, gravity steepness, momentum, hierarchy, appearance) and notes that the study still did not settle its umbrella question. It preserves unselected and provisional items and states no production physics/art/moon hierarchy was integrated. The phased-studies lesson links this episode and the prior failures to the later purpose → exploration → integration workflow.
- **Limits:** “Method worked” is the designer's assessment of this cycle, not a controlled efficacy result. The broad scope and partial outputs support that diagnosis but do not prove all broad design subjects fail. An independent review found force-composition and fixture concerns, and initially assumed a separate moon force law; the designer corrected that to a shared law, showing review can expose as well as introduce framing errors.

### E-D05 — Factory amendment: phased study method and its validation boundary

- **Sources:** `docs/factory-building-lessons/002-phased-design-studies.md:6-35,37-77,79-109,129-140`; `docs/specs/lab/design-studies/spec.md:25-55,99-134`; `.agents/skills/design-study/SKILL.md:21-39,51-56,78-114`.
- **Type:** recorded designer-approved factory decisions; implemented contract/procedure; explicit evaluation plan.
- **Factual extraction:** The lesson attributes the amendment to three attempts: one button study with purpose, comparison and integration; two planetary attempts described as failing for different reasons. It records designer-identified phasing (establish why, explore how, then determine design-system/application change), with human participation across phases. Adopted changes bound studies to one decision, preserve accepted variants/conditions, assess readiness against the intended delivery, make study-quality evaluation optional for ordinary studies, and let unsuccessful work end usefully while preserving the study's failure judgment. The current contract represents four parts and says acceptance does not grant integration authority. The optional replay protocol records a future evaluation design and states no replay/model comparison had been performed in that amendment record.
- **Limits / rationale provenance:** The designer's approval and identified rationale are recorded; the lesson itself cautions that this is not an independent causal experiment. Implemented instructions and repository validation can show the workflow surface exists, but the lesson explicitly says only a fresh study plus human assessment could establish improved operation. At this baseline, the packet does not establish a fresh controlled replay of the amended workflow.

- **Sources:** `docs/loop-evidence/2026-11-06-derelict-three-closure.md:1-44,46-64,66-107`.

No full verification, gameplay collision validation or release occurred. The loop ran in a shared dirty tree at a different HEAD than this packet's baseline.

- **Factual extraction:** The designer stopped the study as inconclusive during exploration. A first section was shortened after feedback and a rubric; it was not retested. ## Cross-packet boundary

These episodes show both shared-system/design-spec translation and cases where purpose, scope, factual grounding, or feedback handling remained unsettled. They do not establish project-wide rates. A finding that a written method is aligned with an ASDLC proposition, or that it should be amended, requires comparison with the separately registered ASDLC guidance and a broader source/episode denominator; this packet makes neither assessment.
