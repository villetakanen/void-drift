all failed study application payloads. Their pages, dedicated demos, catalogue
entries, and obsolete test were removed. Historical artifact paths below identify
removed files, not retained deliverables. This later instruction supersedes earlier

Closed on 29 October 2026 by the designer as an unsuccessful study. **Nothing

## Outcome and the designer’s diagnosis

The designer’s closing distinction was explicit: “this study in its entirety
failed” but “in this case, the method worked.” They judged the skills and agent
work useful. Their diagnosis was that the presented design problem was too large
for one study—potentially too large for twenty studies—not that the current skill
needed another repair.

The initiating topic was the role of planets in the game and how they work. Its
three purposes were solar-system spectacle, exciting gravity-assisted movement,
and collision hazards. It also included planets orbiting the sun and moons orbiting
planets. These are related subjects, but together they encompass substantial game
design: force behavior, flight, hierarchy, scale, camera, visual identity, body
types, and difficulty. Agreement on those purposes did not make the combined
problem small enough to settle.

The designer stopped the work after a proposed integration review and an independent
agent assessment. That later instruction supersedes the earlier direction to carry
accepted mechanics into the game. The readiness reviews remain historical assessments;
neither is an authorization to resume integration.

## What the method made possible

This cycle produced a short framing page, progressively developed numbered sections,
concrete examples, corrections recorded on the page, and retained comparisons.
The designer initially called the framing good work. Later examples made specific
judgments possible:

- The first encounter replay was judged not very useful for the actual concern.
  Discussion narrowed to how gravitational pull grows with proximity.
- A comparison of gravity profiles led to a selected middle candidate: power 2.5
  between powers 2 and 3. The designer later clarified that a shared gravity law
  means **every gravity well**, including the sun’s—not a separate law per body.
- Entry-momentum comparisons showed clean flybys at faster starts. The designer
  accepted that example for the time being; it did not establish an extraordinary
  retained speed reward.
- A planet–moon composition made nested motion inspectable. Restoring the actual
  sun pulses, gravity markers, and scene context made it more useful for judgment.
- Repeated corrections separated moon body size from gravity reach. The working
  example ended with body radius 8, gravity reach 100, orbital radius 110, and
  parent reach 200. The agreed relationships were moon reach < moon orbit radius
  < parent gravity reach. Only the orbit had to fit inside the parent’s well.

These are useful partial findings and decisions from this cycle. They are not
proof that the original planetary design problem was answered, universal tuning
rules, or integrated production behavior.

## Where the inquiry exceeded a single study

The examples successively addressed encounter reward, gravity steepness, entry
momentum, orbital hierarchy, visual context, moon size, moon reach, and planet
appearance. The later request concerned how different planet types, sizes, rings,
and solar proportions could belong to the game’s sharp neon visual language.
At that point the designer explicitly stopped further visual work.

The visible sequence supports the designer’s scope diagnosis: the work contained
several individually answerable investigations, while the umbrella question kept
inviting another. A numbered page and incremental agreement helped retain progress;
they could not by themselves turn the whole subject into one resolvable decision.
This is consistent with the designer’s assessment, not an independent causal test
of the method or proof that every broad inquiry must fail.

The independent agent review was useful within the then-proposed bounded integration.
It identified the untested combined moon/parent forces and the risk that changing
live production dependencies would alter historical fixtures. Its suggestion that
the moon needed an explicit force-law choice also exposed a framing error: the
designer intended one shared law for all gravity wells. The lead corrected and
recorded that scope. The review did not resolve the overall study’s breadth.

## The narrower studies the designer named

The designer identified three examples of questions that could fit a single study:

1. How do gravity wells operate?
2. How do planets look?
3. Can we add a moon to a planet?

These examples name the intended granularity. They are not twenty new assignments,
a scheduled roadmap, or automatic authorization to start another cycle. Each can
reuse relevant evidence while establishing its own question and acceptance boundary.

## Learning recorded, not promoted

The designer’s conclusion is that this was a scope failure with useful partial
results and a working method. The retrospective implication is to distinguish an
umbrella design subject from an answerable study question. Receiving a broad topic
need not mean keeping all of it inside one inquiry.

This document records that interpretation for review. It does not add a mandatory
scope checklist, rewrite the design-study skill, or claim another instruction can
mechanically solve the judgment involved. No standing rules were changed.

## Closure

The study is closed as unsuccessful in its entirety. Preserve the artifacts,
partial decisions, and independent review. Stop integration and further exploration
in this cycle. Planet artwork, type treatment, general sizes, rings, zoom,
extraordinary retained slingshot speed, and broader difficulty remain undecided.
No production physics, artwork, or moon hierarchy was integrated from this study;
no commit, push, or publication was performed by this cycle.

Checks of the artifacts establish implementation correctness only within their
recorded limits. They do not overturn the designer’s outcome or prove visual
quality, gameplay feel, or general effectiveness of the method. Temporary browser
captures and logs may not survive; the evidence record states that limitation.
