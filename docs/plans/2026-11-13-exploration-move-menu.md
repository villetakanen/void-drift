# Exploration move menu: draft addition to the exploration guide

Status: draft for review, 2026-11-13. Not applied. The proposed text below would be a
new section in `.agents/skills/design-study/references/exploration.md`, placed after
"Make the question inspectable". The factory spec allows only approved lessons to
become standing guidance, so the guide is unchanged until this is reviewed.

## Evidence

- 284 part-02 steps from 44 study pages, indexed and labelled by move and result
  (Haiku, one pass, not audited). 262 statements from 12 reviews and 70 loop-evidence
  records on moves that helped or hurt (Sonnet, five quotes spot-checked).
- The pages keep only retained steps, so every rate below understates failure.
- Share of steps that ended with something selected or fixed: hand over the control
  75% (24 steps), ladder 73% (23), foundation in words 70% (30), put it where it lives
  59% (22), spread 58% (74), narrow 57% (57), run the real system 54% (22), single
  first example 42% (14). Assemble (6) and start from an accepted thing (7) are too
  few for a rate.
- Accepted studies settle something in 71% of steps, stopped studies in 33%. Half of
  the accepted studies end on handing over the control or putting the result where it
  lives. These are associations; a study with an unanswerable question produces the
  same pattern whatever moves are chosen.

## Proposed text

```markdown
## Choose the move

Each round makes one move. Choose it from the state of the question, and name the
move to yourself before building. The designer sees the example, never the move name.

The question is open and no answer has been picked:

- Spread: show several whole answers that differ from each other in kind. Prefer this
  to a single first example, which gives the designer nothing to compare.
- Start from an accepted thing: copy real geometry, a production effect or today's
  component, and change one property. Use it when earlier proposals did not look
  like they belong to the game.
- Settle the foundation: when the examples cannot be judged because a name, promise,
  term or content list is undefined, decide that one thing in a few sentences, then
  return to examples in the same round.

The designer picked an answer and named what is wrong with it:

- Narrow: keep the pick intact and vary only the named part.
- Combine: when two retained variants each hold a wanted part, draw the combinations.

The form is accepted and a value or position is open:

- Ladder: step one value through three to five settings with everything else fixed.
- Hand over the control: give the designer sliders for the open values on the real
  object, with a reset to the starting values, and record what they set.

Something is settled in isolation:

- Put it where it lives: show it on the real hulls, at flight scale, in the real tool
  or in a real flight. Do this only with a form the designer has accepted.
- Next dependent decision: take the choice the settled one unlocks, such as the next
  state of a control or the next part of an assembly.
- Assemble: when three or more decisions stand apart, show them together on one
  sheet and mark anything on it the designer has not judged.

The question is about how the game behaves:

- Run the real system: run production simulation on one fixed input sequence and
  starting condition, and keep that fixture unchanged across the steps that compare
  against it.

## Change the move when a round fails

- Every variant is judged worse than the pick it came from: the answer is not known
  yet. Return to Spread with whole answers.
- The designer says the examples are flat, icon-like or foreign to the game: Start
  from an accepted thing, or Settle the foundation. Do not draw another set in the
  same manner.
- The designer cannot judge the example: keep its content and change its medium or
  scale, for instance fixed-scale stills in place of a flight, or one component in
  place of a whole screen.
- A result that was accepted stops working in a later step: compare the fixtures of
  the two steps and restore the earlier one before changing anything else.
- A step has just been accepted: recommend readiness for it or take the next
  dependent decision. Do not refine it further unless the designer names a fault.
- The next step would be a written plan, a proposal or a question about what to try:
  build the example the text describes.
```

## Open points for review

- Whether "Combine" and "Next dependent decision" stay as named moves. Each rests on
  a handful of steps and was identified by reading, without a label of its own.
- Whether the guide should carry the rates. The draft leaves them out because they
  are from retained steps only and would read as stronger than they are.
- The last pivot overlaps the existing "Described alternatives ... are insufficient"
  rule. It is repeated here because written recovery proposals preceded three
  stopped studies.
