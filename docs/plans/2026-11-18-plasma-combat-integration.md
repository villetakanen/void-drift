# Plasma rifle: combat integration and performance assessment

18 November 2026. Local implementation experiment for the 0.7.0 release direction. Prepared from the operation study, living specs and current code. Recheck owners against the completed RC1 snapshot before starting.

## Scope

Commit and push the authorized RC1 snapshot to `feat/0-7-0` before starting the experiment; do not deploy. Then use a local branch for the experiment. Run its Fires in the Sky challenge offline, with remote leaderboard access disabled. An emulator may be used to verify the score path. The experiment has no push or deployment.

Move automatic target acquisition and firing decisions for all weapon families through the same Mode A runtime in Practice, Derelicts, Gauntlet and Survival, including the ranked rules path. Every mode supplies its own target population and encounter policy. Practice runs the mounted weapons with an empty combat target set and keeps its existing flight purpose. Gauntlet enemies use the same authoritative execution as the player. Individual profiles own acquisition geometry, eligible target classes, retention, obstruction and timing.

Plasma engines, the functional Plasma rifle and new star systems are intended for ranked play before final 0.7. The local candidate exercises the existing Plasma engine, rifle and seed-selected yellow-family star through Survival with Playground Off. This uses the currently implemented stellar system work as the experiment baseline. Additional system layouts and final ranked balance remain release decisions. Keep other catalog eligibility and the default enemy fleet at their existing settings.

The main implementation is a shared combat query and mounted weapon runtime in Mode A. Give each mount its own cycle. Use bounded analytical contact and fume volumes, broad-phase rejection, and the existing shared FX layers. Keep AI prediction focused on firing opportunity. Reuse production geometry and ownership for gameplay, and draw through the production render model.

The study and code inspection establish candidate mechanics and likely costs. Measure frame pacing and multi-rifle load in the local experiment.

## What the evidence actually establishes

The [operation study](../../apps/web/src/pages/lab/studies/plasma-rifle-operation-2026-11-18.mdx) is accepted as a design proposal. Its last operating proposal is a 130 u, 30° full-angle muzzle sector; 0.7 s charge; a firing/venting outcome followed by 2.1 s recovery; mass-dependent kick and vent force; first-contact eruption; and two moving fume envelopes. Earlier exploration values such as 0.5 s charge and 70 damage are not the final proposed defaults. Final bands are 75 through 10 u, 50 through 26 u and 25 through 42 u, with 3 damage per entry into the combined fumes. Visual radius and damage radius are explicitly different.

The study demonstrates wreck acquisition, mount views, recoil and competition with ion fire. It does not establish a production implementation of live-ship material collision, every energy exit, or multi-rifle performance. Its scope table is useful design context, not the project's implementation order.

## Proposed implementation boundaries

### 1. Shared queries, distinct profiles

Create a small Mode A combat module rather than moving game policy into core. Proposed files are `combat-world.ts`, `combat-queries.ts`, `weapon-runtime.ts` and `schemas/weapons.ts`. Implementation may follow the existing module conventions when naming files.

A combat entity has a stable ID with generation, kind, owner/team, life state, current/previous transform and a geometry reference. References adapt existing salvage and ship owners rather than duplicate their health or movement. Entity IDs must not collide across actors or be reused while effects still refer to them. A world-query view exposes eligible candidates, nearest material, first swept contact and area overlap. Damage goes through one authoritative resolver preserving breakup, attribution, scoring and same-tick encounter results.

A profile selects supported policies: acquisition shape/reference point, eligible kinds, ranking/retention, obstruction mask, contact primitive, timing/energy model and damage recipe. Use a discriminated union of implemented weapon families, not an unrestricted configuration language or a large set of mutually optional fields. Keep current ion, rail and laser numbers and firing semantics. Mount execution must also work with no encounters or salvage owner. Validate definitions and imported tuning once with Zod; hot geometry loops use typed validated data rather than repeated parsing.

Runtime is actor + mount state, not profile data: idle, charging, charged/waiting, firing, venting and cooling, target identity, charge amount, phase deadline, release sequence, stream state and fume occupancy. Rendering receives a compact current-state view and timestamped presentation events. It never advances a weapon or determines a hit.

Extract current weapons through adapters first. Leave salvage spawning, collection and breakup in their existing owner. Avoid a broad file move before behavior parity is established.

### 2. Broad phase before material work

Start with reusable compact candidate storage, squared-distance/bounding-circle rejection and allocation-free selection. A cone-centre test needs only forward/lateral dot products and precomputed angle constants; it does not need to intersect every hull polygon. Blast queries reject bodies beyond outer radius plus body bound before asking for nearest material once per candidate. Evaluate three bands from that one distance, not three independent scans.

For larger workloads, place the query implementation behind a shared uniform-grid broad phase. Compare it with the bounded scan in the baseline spike: small current encounters may be faster with a scan. Build/update once per world substep, not per gun. Use swept bounds for moving streams and bodies, include every touched cell, deduplicate candidates with reusable stamps, and preserve deterministic hit/tie order independently of cell iteration. Star/planet circles can stay in a small separate list.

Transform queries into cached local material coordinates. Reuse existing mask caches; avoid creating world polygon arrays per rifle per substep. Cache only within a valid transform/material revision. Breakup, death and newly exposed material must be visible to later weapon actions according to the scheduler's ordering. Patch affected index entries immediately or merge a deterministic mutation list into queries; a stale spatial index must not make a newly exposed shard intangible.

Expected cost is O(B) world maintenance plus local query candidates and exact geometry, rather than repeated O(W × B × material complexity) scans. A grid does not remove worst-case dense overlap: include that case in measurement. Do not silently truncate authoritative candidate sets to meet a frame budget.

### 3. Time, contact and damage

Advance at the existing simulation cadence, including Gauntlet's 1/240 s combat steps. Consume elapsed time across phase boundaries so a step that finishes charging can spend its remainder in firing. Integrate charge debit and vent impulse over actual phase duration. Preserve defence-first and slot-order resource competition and the cannon's captured volley. Cooling/venting continue without requiring new firing power.

Sweep the stream head from previous to next position relative to moving bodies; select earliest physical contact, using shared material casts for wrecks and shared circles for ships. Handle initial overlap and stable equal-contact blocker precedence. If angular motion requires smaller contact intervals, bound them from a documented motion-error tolerance, not visual particle count. Expire the stream once it reaches its maximum travel without contact. Arena wraps are discontinuities, never a sweep through the entire world.

On eruption, gather recipients and distances from one pre-damage snapshot, then apply damage deterministically. Carry a release ID and per-body primary-hit identity. Damage attribution includes attacker and effect kind so enemy damage cannot accidentally count as player weapon kills. Commit deaths/results at the established encounter boundary, preserving mutual destruction.

Each rifle has two analytical fume envelopes, independent of visual particles. Sample emission at the fixed combat cadence and retain the outlet position and velocity at emission in a bounded history. Use swept segments between adjacent live samples to cover continuous emission. The 0.2 s lifetime bounds the history without using visual particle count. Expire old sections and break continuity at wraps.

Track entry into the **union** of both outlets per release/body generation. Moving from one overlapping spray to the other does not retrigger; exit then re-entry does. Swept overlap must detect a fast pass that begins and ends outside. Define temporal union handling so an apparent gap between discrete samples cannot invent another hit. Prune destroyed/exited entities and expire all membership with the effect. Separate rifles remain independent damage sources unless the product explicitly chooses a global fume immunity rule.

Physics continues off camera. Only presentation work is visibility-culled; changing zoom or camera cannot alter damage, recoil or AI.

### 4. Enemy aiming without expensive combat rollouts

Weapon execution uses the same profile and world queries for both sides. Enemy steering consumes a cheap profile-derived aiming evaluator: effective range, alignment error, muzzle offset, readiness and approximate time needed to hold acquisition. Player controls continue supplying movement normally.

Keep the existing bounded command prediction. Score supported weapon opportunity at a small fixed set of predicted samples, using simple profile geometry and target motion. Do not add real damage, breakup, FX, spatial-index rebuilds or fume membership to its 3,456 propulsion substeps. Prediction may approximate opportunity; the real weapon runtime always makes the authoritative acquisition/release decision. Scratch prediction must never mutate shared combat state.

Initially keep the stock Scout loadout. Add deterministic diagnostic fixtures with plasma-equipped enemies to prove execution parity and sustained alignment, without silently changing the encounter contract. Any change to planning frequency, scoring weights or staggered planning must be deterministic and separately measured against existing pilot behavior.

### 5. Flight presentation

Reuse existing rifle art, `weaponPose`, build transforms and production `FlightFx` composition through `flight-frame.ts`. Charge bead and fresh stream cues belong to the agreed live-light passes; drifting fumes and eruption light stamp into shared persistent FX. Preserve the Wasp/Fighter mounting and occlusion distinction through shared geometry, not a study-only painter.

Cache a small finite set of source marks by style/phase, prepared before drawing. Avoid per-frame pixel work, per-mount full-screen canvases, per-puff gradients and repeated filter setup. Animated turbulence should use bounded cached variants/native layer treatment rather than a fluid simulation. Final visual identity remains provisional where the study defers it.

Cull complete visual bounds before contact queries or stamp preparation. Maintain current active cues per mount instead of searching copied event histories. Cosmetic events can expire/drop decoration; gameplay effects and their lifetime must not live in the existing 0.2 s, 128-event presentation ring. A 0.7 s firing cycle cannot depend on that ring retaining its start event.

Use the existing scene-wide source/decorative allowances, shared spark pool and pause/reset/wrap/seek lifecycle. Prioritize charge/release/contact readability when reducing decoration. No emission backlog after skipped frames; respect interval coverage through the shared presentation path. FX quality changes must yield identical gameplay state.

## Performance evidence and acceptance

Measure a production build before extraction, after behavior-preserving extraction, and after rifle integration using identical seeds and workloads. Keep AI, physics/contact, weapon scheduling, FX update/submission and browser raster/compositing distinguishable. Collect counts of candidates, exact casts, blast recipients, active envelopes, events and allocations as well as time. Instrumentation must be disabled or quantified in final timing runs.

Use normal play and a diagnostic matrix: one rifle; maximum legal rifles per build; player plus both enemies with mixed guns; six mixed-effects ships; breakup-heavy wreck/fragment populations; simultaneous eruptions; overlapping fumes; fast crossing bodies; moving camera; fully offscreen effects; and repeated cycles until memory reaches steady state. Keep counts explicit. Six ships is a stress fixture, not a new gameplay population rule. Combine AI replanning and releases to expose burst contention rather than only testing staggered easy frames.

Proposed sampling: 10 s warm-up and at least 60 s measurement, three repeats per scenario, plus a longer repeated-cycle memory run. Name hardware, browser/version, acceleration status, CSS and backing resolutions, device pixel ratio and thermal conditions. Cover hardware-accelerated desktop Chromium, a less powerful desktop profile and physical iPad landscape Safari; mobile emulation is supplementary. Run Lab fixtures at ≥1280 px; test smaller devices through play.

Existing acceptance remains whole-scene FX p95 **below 3 ms**, including attributable raster/compositor cost; normal play targets 60 fps, heavy fixture p95 frame interval ≤33.34 ms. Report p50/p95/max and stalls. CPU callback duration alone cannot prove this budget. If GPU attribution is unavailable, explicitly mark the evidence incomplete.

Proposed engineering allocation for review: whole-scene combat/query work p95 ≤2 ms per 60 Hz tick, aggregating all four substeps where used; report planning separately and include it in total frame pacing. This is a planning target, not an established project requirement or a measured result. Calibrate against named hardware in the first spike. Reject material regressions in existing weapons and investigate periodic long frames even if median performance is good.

If over budget: first remove duplicate queries/allocation, then improve broad-phase selectivity and cache geometry, then reduce cosmetic stamp density. Optimize AI evaluation separately if it dominates. Do not reduce damage candidates, skip offscreen simulation, change collision precision or slow acquisition checks as an invisible performance fix. Such gameplay changes require an explicit spec decision. Web Workers, OffscreenCanvas and a new rendering backend are later options only if measured remaining cost warrants their synchronization and lifecycle complexity.

## Verification that protects behavior

Focused tests should cover meaningful regressions rather than mirror internal function calls:

- Same target/acquisition result for player and enemy under the same policy; pirate acquisition restricted to its intended opponent while friendly bodies still block damage.
- Cone edge/range and stable target selection; a fragment alone cannot charge plasma; existing defence fragment targeting remains intact.
- Charge loss, target return, insufficient power at start/during charge/at release, competing guns, multiple capacities, independent mounts and repeated recovery. Equal elapsed simulated time gives equivalent energy, charge and impulse under supported subdivisions.
- Swept fast contact, nearest material around holes, equal-contact blockers, one primary damage application, surviving wreck breakup and fresh fragments, overlapping fumes and exit/re-entry. Deletion and identity reuse cannot retain false membership.
- Mutual destruction, no self damage, correct kill attribution, no damage after destruction and no double world stepping with several actors.
- Identical gameplay with full/reduced/no rendering; pause, reset, wrap and seek clear or preserve the correct state; long runs bound gameplay effect storage and cosmetic pools.
- Saved Plasma rifle and engine fittings can launch candidate Survival with Playground Off, fire automatically at eligible targets and return. Playground Survival remains unranked. Other unsanctioned fittings still require Playground. Production score access is unavailable in the experiment.
- The same dispatch path runs in Practice, Derelicts, Gauntlet and Survival. Practice's empty target set keeps weapons idle and its flight sandbox has no new encounters. Candidate ranked Survival uses the same seed-selection rule for its star as Playground Survival.

Run targeted tests during each commit and repository-required verification on the stable integration candidate per [development](../development.md). Report automated correctness, measured performance and visual inspection separately. Human playtesting stays outside the repository and is not a gate or proof request. This plan records local implementation scope. Record actual validation results after the experiment runs.
