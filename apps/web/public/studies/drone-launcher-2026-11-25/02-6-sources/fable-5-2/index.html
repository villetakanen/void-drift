A fission family for Void Drift

Four proposals that differ in idea: a timing, a shadow, a branching rule and a counted footprint. Every image below is drawn live by this page with Canvas 2D through a copy of your World / FX / FX glow / LFX stack.

Pick

A, Double flash. Every fission event blinks white, then black, then green, and the length of the black gap tells the yield. It is the only one of the four whose identity survives at 6 px, it costs three draws per tick, and it keeps the saturated green out of the frame for all but the decay. Reasons and open questions are in section 5.

The four loops (one per proposal) are paused on a mid-detonation frame until you press this. They flash at about 2 Hz.

Master of Orion (1993) Tactical grid. Beams are straight lines in one palette-cycled hue per weapon. A destroyed stack plays one explosion animation on its tile. No engine light in combat. VGA sprite frames, dithered ramps. One hue per technology, and cycling means energy is flowing. recalled. Where to check: manual scan.
Master of Orion 2 (1996) Explosion sprite sized to the hull class. A ship whose drive is destroyed blows up and damages neighbours, scaled by class. Engines show a small flicker at the stern. Pre-rendered fireball sprites. Danger comes in a discrete ladder of sizes tied to a known class, so players learn four blasts instead of a continuum. recalled, low confidence on the drive rule. Where to check: manual.
Missile Command (1980) Eight palette entries. Explosions are drawn in one entry whose colour is rewritten every frame, visible at about 30 Hz. Smart bombs avoid blasts by reading screen pixels for that flashing entry. Blasts grow and then shrink. 3-bit RGB, full-palette strobing. The drawn flash is the hitbox: what you see is exactly what kills. Flicker comes from cycling a flat shape, and the light retreats after it grows. checked for palette, cycling rate and pixel-reading: video memory notes, disassembly. Grow-then-shrink: recalled.
Defender (1981) A particle routine makes each explosion from the destroyed object, so no two look alike. The player's death is far longer and denser than an enemy's. Chunky scaled pixels. Build the blast from the victim's own shape. Importance is told by duration and count. checked that Sam Dicker wrote a particle algorithm for unique explosions: Wikipedia. The rest: recalled.
Elite (1984) An explosion cloud is random particles seeded around some of the ship's vertices. Each ship blueprint stores how many vertices to use, and a cloud counter drives growth. 1-bit dots. One routine, one integer per object for scale. The cloud keeps the skeleton of what died. checked: DOEXP, annotated source.
Fire effect (demos from about 1993; PSX Doom, 1995) A buffer of heat values 0 to 36. Each frame copies heat upward with a random loss. A 37-step palette from white through yellow, orange and red to black turns heat into colour at the end. Low resolution, orange fire ramp. Store intensity, apply the ramp last, and let decay plus feedback animate. Your FX layer is this without the final ramp, which has a consequence for colour (section 2). checked: Sanglard, How DOOM fire was done. Demo-scene dating: recalled.
Physical reference: the double flash An atmospheric nuclear burst emits two light peaks. For 19 kt: first maximum near 0.3 ms, minimum at 12 ms, second maximum near 130 ms. The shock front is opaque and hides the fireball between them. The minimum and second-maximum times grow with yield, and satellites read yield from them. None. A nuclear event has a time signature that no chemical or plasma effect shares, and its clock is its size. checked: LANL review (PDF), Bhangmeter.

2. Three findings that apply to all four

Stacked yellow-green turns into your brand lime

FX adds towards white with no ramp at the end. #c6ff32 stamped twice clips to rgb(255,255,100), which is L 97.9 C 72.6 h 100.8. Brand lime is L 96.4 C 84.2 h 104.9. Green clips first, red catches up, and every overlap passes through the interface colour on its way to white.

So the FX stamps here are authored greener and bluer than the identity: rgb(96,224,72) (h 133.7). Twice gives rgb(192,255,144) (h 126.6, C 57.6), three times is near white. Hot stamp rgb(200,255,190), deep stamp rgb(30,150,48).

Rules that follow: no fission colour with chroma above 60 sits below hue 120 or above L 88. Nothing between hue 138 and 160 is lighter than L 55. Fission light never holds a level for more than 0.3 s, and the interface lime never glows or fades. Mint at hue 178 and cyan beyond it are untouched. Amber and violet are on other sides of the wheel.

Hard-light over the void shows 2s − 1

With a backdrop near black, an LFX source channel s above 50% displays as 2s − 1, and below 50% it displays nothing while darkening whatever light is there. A deep green you want to see as rgb(45,148,52) must be painted as pale sage rgb(150,202,154). Proposals B and D author their LFX colours this way. LFX is also the only layer that can remove light, and A and D use that on purpose.

What the images assume

One world unit is one pixel at flight scale, drawn here at 2 px per unit with the glow blur doubled to match. The fighter is 38 u long with a pusher plate at the stern. Its geometry is invented, the facet colours are yours. The burst point sits 6 u behind the plate. Speeds are 1 to 3.5 u per tick. The FX fade is a black fill at 10.9% per tick.

3A. Double flash

Every fission event blinks twice: a hard white flash, a black gap with a bright rim, then a slower green swell. Yield sets the clock, so a larger event has a longer gap and its size is read from timing before radius.

Engine pulseTicks 0–1 (33 ms): white disc r 6 u at the burst point, soft white r 16 u. Ticks 2–4 (50 ms): black disc r 8 → 12 u with a 1.1 u pale rim. Ticks 5–10 (100 ms): green swell, soft stamp r 12 → 25 u, peak on tick 7, drifting aft 0.6 u per tick. The ship is 38 u, so the swell peaks at two thirds of a ship length and sits behind the plate.
Drone detonationTicks 0–2 (50 ms): white disc r 11 u, soft white r 30 u, LFX white r 22 u on tick 0 then 15 u. Ticks 3–9 (117 ms): black disc r 16 → 34 u, rim 1.5 u. Ticks 10–26 (283 ms): swell r 34 → 50 u, peak on tick 14, hot to green to deep. Ticks 27–45: decay only.
FXFlash, swell and a faint print of the rim each tick. Accumulation makes the swell's white core and stretches it along the ship's path. The rim prints stack into nested echo rings inside the swell.
LFXThe flash disc, because hard-light white is white on the first frame with no bloom lag. The gap disc, because only LFX can subtract: its dark source takes the flash afterglow and the glow layer beneath it to near zero. Over a hull it dims the hull to about a tenth, for at most 7 ticks.
Shapes and countsHard disc, ring stroke, one cached soft radial stamp scaled as needed. At most 3 draws per event per tick. No particles.
ColourFlash neutral white, gap black. Green appears only in the swell, as FX stamps rgb(200,255,190) → rgb(96,224,72) → rgb(30,150,48). Of the four this puts the least saturated green on screen, and none of it is flat or steady, which is the separation from the lime.
ScalingGap of 3 ticks for the engine, 7 for a drone, about 16 for a reactor, about 40 for a capital event. Radius grows more slowly than the gap. Constant: the order white, black, green, and a gap that is never skipped.
The waitNothing new is drawn. The last swell decays at its world point, falling to an eighth by tick 28, so a thrusting ship leaves green beads whose spacing is its speed. The plate accent steps hot, mid, deep, dark at 4 ticks each.
Cost and riskNegligible cost. Risk: a white flash and a blackout at 2.2 Hz is a strobe, and if the gap reads as "off" instead of as a shape you are back to an engine that flickers. The rim prevents that, and at 1× the engine's rim is about one pixel wide. Over empty void the black disc is invisible except for the rim.

3B. Plate shadow

The blast is drawn by what it lights: a flat pale wash that appears at full radius and contracts in steps, while every solid cuts a hard shadow wedge out of it. The engine's own plate shades its ship, so the ship sits unlit in a notch of its own explosion.

Engine pulseTick 0: wash at R 32 u (0.85 ship length) in two flat tones, the inner at 58% of R. It steps every 2 ticks: 32, 26, 20, 15, 10, 6 u, each step one tone deeper. The plate's notch is ±53° at ignition and narrows as the ship leaves. Core: white r 2.2 u and soft r 7 u on ticks 0–3.
Drone detonationTick 0: wash at R 78 u. Steps every 3 ticks: 78, 66, 53, 41, 29, 18, 9 u, gone at tick 21. Any ship inside is overexposed to a white silhouette on ticks 0–1 and throws a wedge to the rim for as long as the wash reaches it. Core: white r 5 u, soft r 16 u, ticks 0–2.
FXThe core, and a 0.7 to 1 u ring printed at the wash edge each time it steps. FX therefore remembers where the light has been: nested rings that outlast the wash by about 15 ticks.
LFXThe wash and its wedges. No history means hard steps and shadow edges that follow moving ships exactly. Wedges are cut from the LFX canvas with destination-out, so a shadow is the untouched scene and a shaded ship keeps its own colours. Over a lit hull the pale wash screens and lifts it.
Shapes and countsTwo discs per event, one wedge polygon per ship in range (five silhouette points scanned for the two tangent angles), one ring per step.
ColourLarge areas stay low in chroma: displayed rgb(205,228,190) → rgb(150,200,132) → rgb(96,166,92) → rgb(56,126,64) → rgb(30,90,46), at 78% falling to 54%. Saturated green is confined to the 7 u core. It separates from the lime by chroma and by never being the same size twice.
ScalingR and step period: 32 u and 2 ticks, 78 u and 3 ticks, about 160 u and 5 ticks. Constant: light that retreats to its point, two flat tones, wedges.
The waitThe ring prints and core fade. The plate accent steps down as in A.
Cost and riskCheap, but the wedge scan runs per ship per event per tick. Risks: the engine frame reads as Pac-Man with the ship in its mouth. A flat disc wider than the ship every 0.45 s is the largest lit area of the four. Overlapping washes from two engines replace each other on LFX and do not add.

3C. Chain

Light is straight tracks that appear at full length, never move, and end by splitting in two. The picture grows by generations, and yield is the number of generations.

Drone detonation40, 20, 10 and 5 tracks on ticks 0–3, all round, 14 to 76 u. First forks at +3 ticks. Second forks at +6: four twigs per track, 18% length, deep green. LFX white core r 7 u on ticks 0–1. Peak density ticks 5–9, gone near tick 36.
FXEvery track. Overlap near the origin adds to white, so the core comes free. The glow layer turns the dense centre into a body and leaves the tips crisp. Because the ship moves during the push, successive origins comb the fan along its path.
LFXOnly the two-tick core, to give tick 0 a solid.
Shapes and countsLine strokes and 1 u dots. Engine: 30 tracks and 60 branches per push. Drone: 75 tracks, 150 branches, 300 twigs, about 200 strokes on the busiest tick. Tracks live in a small list per event for 8 ticks.
Colourrgb(200,255,190) on the birth tick, rgb(96,224,72) after, twigs rgb(30,150,48). Thin strokes never fill an area, so there is no flat lime-like patch anywhere.
ScalingGenerations: 1 for the engine, 2 for a drone, 3 for a reactor, capped near 1,200 strokes. Constant: the fork, its angle range and the 40% ratio.
The waitForks of the last tracks finish on tick 14, then the comb decays in place.
Cost and riskThe most strokes of the four, still small. Risks: it reads as lightning, frost or a plant. Lines of 1 u disappear when the camera zooms out. It has the least mass, so the weakest push.

3D. Standing shells

The event shows its whole footprint on tick 0 as counted rings 6 u wide. A hot band then walks outward one ring every 3 ticks and leaves burnt rings behind. Size is the ring count and time is where the hot band stands.

Engine pulseThree bands from r 3 to 21 u in a 120° sector behind the plate, fixed in the world at ignition. Hot band on ring 1 for ticks 0–2, ring 2 for 3–5, ring 3 for 6–8. Burnt rings fade over ticks 9–14.
Drone detonationSix bands from r 3 to 39 u, full circle. The walk takes 18 ticks (300 ms) and the burnt fade 6 more. Pending rings are visible from tick 0, so the kill radius is announced before the heat arrives.
FXThe hot band only, at 55%. Over its 3 ticks it adds up to white and lingers as glow.
LFXAll bands. Pending rings are pale sage sources that display as deep green. The hot ring is near white. Burnt rings are dark sources that remove the FX afterglow beneath them, which leaves bright 0.9 u hairlines in the gaps between rings.
Shapes and countsAnnular sectors: 3 per engine event, 6 per detonation, plus one FX sector and a 2-tick centre flash.
ColourFour flat steps, displayed: rgb(240,255,215) hot, then rgb(70,190,60), rgb(45,148,52), rgb(20,105,45) outward. Banding is the style.
Scaling3, 6, 12, 24 rings. Constant: 6 u per ring and 3 ticks per ring, so the hot band walks at 2 u per tick at every size and a bigger event simply takes longer.
The waitThe burnt sector for 6 ticks, then nothing.
Cost and riskCheapest after A. Risks: concentric green rings read as radar, sonar or a targeting widget, and the outer rings sit closest of any proposal to the countdown green. It is orderly, so the least violent. The engine version is small.

5. Pick: A, Double flash

  • It solves the plume problem at its root. Today's plume reads as an ion engine that flickers because on and off is all it says. A says three things per push in a fixed order, and no ion, plasma or gravity effect in the game has a black phase.
  • It survives scale. A timing reads the same at 6 px and at 300 px. B, C and D each depend on geometry that needs pixels.
  • It uses each layer for the thing only that layer can do. LFX subtracts, FX remembers. The gap disc eating the afterglow is the one image here that cannot be made on a single layer.
  • The family scales with one number. Gap length. A player who has seen an engine pulse can read a reactor loss without being taught.
  • It is the furthest from the interface lime, because two of its three beats have no hue.
  • The push comes from the bead left behind. The swell is painted at a world point and the ship leaves it. At 2 u per tick the ship is a full length away before the swell peaks.

What I would test next on top of A, as an experiment and not as part of the pick: B's shadow wedge on detonation ticks 0–2 only. It answers "who was hit" in one frame and costs one polygon.

Questions I would need answered

  1. How many pixels is one unit at flight zoom, what is the zoom range, and does the 12 px glow blur scale with zoom? The engine rim is about 1 px at 1×.
  2. Typical and top ship speeds in units per tick. Bead spacing and the "ship has left" read depend on it. Does the camera follow the thrusting ship?
  3. Does the detonation have a gameplay radius, and should the drawn rim equal it, as in Missile Command?
  4. Is there a photosensitivity limit on flash area and rate? Several fission engines on screen at 2.2 Hz each will need one.
  5. May the first flash be neutral white, or must every frame carry the yellow-green?
  6. Is the FX fade done in gamma space as a black fill, as here, or some other way? It changes how long the deep end lingers.
  7. Within LFX, do effects draw over each other with normal compositing? A's gap disc must draw after other LFX light, or it cannot darken it.
  8. What happens when thrust is released mid-push, and how should the 0.9 s rhythm differ from the 0.45 s one besides spacing?
  9. What is the budget for simultaneous drones and detonations?
  10. Where do the sound transients fall? A wants two hits per event, with the second one longer.
