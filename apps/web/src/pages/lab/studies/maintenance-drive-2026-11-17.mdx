## 01 / The smallest ion engine

Playtests and design studies often need a ship that moves slowly enough to
watch what happens around it: gravity bending a path, a weapon finding its
target, a planet passing under the hull. The maintenance drive is a weak ion
engine for that work. Its thrust is one and a half times one stabilizer
outlet's, about a third of a light ion's.

The drive belongs to the ion family and sits with the light and heavy ion,
which share one form language and one beam. The heavy ion already shows its
strength inside that envelope: its form is longer and wider, its plume longer,
wider and bluer. Borrowing the light ion's form and plume, the drive would look
exactly like a light ion, because an ion engine's plume size follows how much
of its push is delivered, not its strength; only stabilizer outlets are drawn
smaller. The drive has to show its weakness honestly while still reading as a
main engine rather than a stabilizer.

Open: what can change within the ion engines' form language and within their
plume, and which of those changes make the drive read as the smallest ion
engine. Its name is open too, judged on its part card beside the flight.
Mass, energy draw and cruise contribution are catalog values for readiness to
propose. Sanctioning it for ranked play in 0.7.0 is a separate decision.

Held fixed:

- **The ion family.** Stabilizers share the plume and part of the form
  language. The light and heavy ion share the form language and the plume. The
  ion cannon and point defence share the effects language and part of the form
  language.
- **The envelope.** Nothing outside it: no new form language, no new kind of
  plume.
- **The drive.** Thrust one and a half stabilizer outlets, steady ion response,
  engine category, unsanctioned, so Playground makes it fittable in 0.6.x.

## 02.1 / The plume: the light ion's beam at 0.7

**Accepted within the study:** the drive's plume is the light ion's beam at
0.7 of its length and 0.7 of its width, in the light ion's mint, at full band
strength, sized by the game's plume rule. Of the three answers it came closest
to the intended look and feel in flight. The heavy ion shows strength by a
larger, bluer beam; this shows weakness by a smaller one in the light ion's
colour.

The forms flown with it are not accepted; the form remains open, with
reservations about all three. The names were not judged. Two other plume
answers were set aside: a full-length plume at half strength, and the heavy
ion's difference inverted (shorter, narrower, greener). The half-strength
plume barely read as weaker in flight, because the persistent light surface
builds its core back up to near full brightness.

## 02.2 / The form beside its family

**Accepted within the study:** form A, the light ion's faces at 0.7 by 0.7,
the same proportions as the accepted plume. The lesson of this step is to keep
the main form simple: plume and size make the drive identifiable among the ion
engines, and its nature as a maintenance part has to come from something added
to that form. Form A is kept for its simplicity; on its own it still reads as
a production engine. The name was not part of this step.

## 02.3 / Something that says tool

**Accepted within the study:** a part added to form A can communicate the
drive's nature without losing the ion family's feeling, as long as it is built
the way the engines are: faceted planes, lit on one side and shaded on the
other, with the blue accent. None of the three answers is there yet. **Side
rails** is the idea to carry forward. On the Wasp it does not fit yet: the dark
rails sit on top of the white stern and do not relate to it. The clamps had
something promising, and so did the cradle's long recessed lights; the cradle
plate itself did not belong.

## 02.4 / Rails that fit the ship

**Accepted within the study:** the side rails at length 8.4 u, centred 0.8 u
forward of the drive's origin, their inner edge 2.5 u from the axis, 0.85 u
thick, in engine units: shorter, closer and slimmer than in 02.3. The whole
drive, form and rails, sits 1 u aft of the middle of the Wasp’s engine slot.
Plume, form, addition, size and position are settled; the name and the
drive's mass, energy draw and cruise contribution are not.

## 02.5 / The name on its card

17 November: readiness showed that the name was never explored, and the
drive's mass, energy draw and cruise contribution are design questions too,
not catalog values for readiness to propose. Section 01 is to be amended to
include them once this is settled. This step takes the name; the values follow
in their own step.

Four names, each a different kind, on sketch cards laid out like the Hangar's,
beside the game's own light and heavy ion cards. **Feather ion** continues the
family's weight classes below light. **Service ion** says what the drive is for.
**Maintenance ion** makes the working title an ion. **Tug ion** borrows pilot
slang for a work engine. Each carries a line of card prose in the catalog's
voice. The drawing is the accepted drive in catalog lighting.

## 03 / What the catalog needs

On hold since 17 November: the name and the drive's values went back to
exploration (02.5 onwards). The assessment below stands for everything else.

The accepted drive is a catalog engine, unsanctioned, with the light ion's
response at one and a half stabilizer outlets of thrust (0.33 of a light ion).
It draws the light ion's faces at 0.7 × 0.7 with two side rails, and the light
ion's beam at 0.7 × 0.7 in its mint. Nothing found here needs more design
evidence for the Wasp. Two things are open before it can ship on every ship:
its name, and its look on the other chassis.

**Reusable as it is.** The catalog validates parts (`PARTS`, `PartSchema`,
`PartIdSchema` and `EngineIdSchema` in `loadout.ts`); `releaseClass: "preview"`
makes the drive unsanctioned, so Playground lets it be fitted and runs with it
stay unscored. Score records store fitted ids through `PartIdSchema`, and the
Firestore rules do not list part ids. The Hangar card draws any engine from
`ENGINE_ART` with the ion accent, and `partFigures` computes its figures from
the catalog. In flight, the ion path in `build-flight.ts` takes any strength
from `ionStrength`, and the plume is sized by the square root of output from
cached stamps.

**Adaptations.**

- **Thrust.** `ionStrength` in `schemas/ion.ts` returns 1 and 1.75 for the
  light and heavy ion and 0 for anything else; it needs the drive's 0.33, from
  `STABILIZER_STRENGTH`, and the catalog `thrust` follows it.
- **Plume.** `PlumeStyleSchema` in core `ion-beam.ts` has two ion styles, and
  its stamp writes the heavy ion's multipliers inline. A third style needs the
  stamp to read length, width and ink from a table; `buildPlumeEmitters` maps
  the part to it.
- **Art.** `ENGINE_ART` needs an entry with the four poses the renderer uses:
  catalog, two side lightings and aft. Each is the light ion's pose at
  0.7 × 0.7 plus the rails.
- **Placement.** The 1 u aft offset has no home yet: `engineOffset` in
  `build-renderer.ts` adjusts only the Scout. Baked into the art, it would also
  move the drive on the Hangar card.
- **Lists that name engines.** The Hangar's side-mount fitting in
  `ShipBuilder.svelte` lists the four engine ids, the Lab instrument's
  `config.ts` maps ion ids, and the Lab's ship propulsion page lists engines.
  The light ion's energy scale in `buildDrain` applies to `"ion"` only.

**Unresolved choices.**

- **Name and part id.** The name was never judged; "Maintenance drive" is the
  working title. Part ids must stay stable once stored fittings use them, so
  the id is chosen at delivery even if the display name stays provisional in
  Playground.
- **Mass, energy draw and cruise contribution.** Returned to exploration as
  design questions. For reference the light ion is 10, 1.75/s and +8 u/s, the
  heavy ion 20, 3.5/s and +11 u/s.
- **Other chassis.** The drive was judged only on the Wasp's rear-centre mount.
  The Scout, Fighter and Corvette carry mirrored, angled side engines drawn in
  side lighting, and the Fiddler matching rear engines. Whether the rails and
  the 1 u aft offset belong to the part on every chassis, or only to the Wasp,
  is the project's decision.
- **Sanctioning.** Ranked play from 0.7.0 is a separate decision.

**Specs that would change, as proposals.** No living spec defines the ion
family that section 01 holds fixed, or the ion engines' plume styles. The
parts specification could gain a short rule for ion engines: they share one
form and one beam, and differ by proportions and ink, with the drive as the
smallest member. No change is proposed to the ships, Playground or render
specifications, or to `docs/design.md`.
