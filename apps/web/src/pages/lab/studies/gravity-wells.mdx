## Question

A close pass of a sun should be a temptation. Cutting closer should bend the
ship’s path, gather speed and let it escape with a useful advantage. Cut too
close, and the same pull can make escape difficult or impossible before heat
destroys the ship. A planet poses the same temptation, with impact replacing
solar burn. Escape also depends on approach speed, direction and the ship’s
thrust, so no drawn ring can mark a distance that is always safe.

An unpowered pass around a stationary body gains speed on approach and gives it
back on departure at equal distance. Real gravity assists exchange energy with a
moving body ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/gravity-assists/)).
The study therefore asked what useful speed the player carries away.

The study wanted useful close passes, readable danger and shared rules for suns
and planets. It set the force curve and an initial strength range. Physical
sizing was outside the study.

## What the study proposed

The study says three changes belong together, because resistance masked the
well’s influence and removed speed gained during a pass.

- **Bounded inverse-square gravity for suns and planets.** Inside the well radius
  R the pull is a(r) = μ × (1/r² − 1/R²), with r measured from the body’s
  center, and zero outside. It is continuous at the border, though its slope
  changes there. The body’s surface ends a pass before the formula’s central
  singularity. It was chosen for the close-pass shapes it produces: weaker
  than the original linear pull outside half the well radius and stronger
  inside.
- **Solar strength 4×–10×; the game starts at 4×.** At the original strength
  the close-in pull was too weak.
- **No passive drag and no hard speed cap.** Gravity-earned speed should not be
  clipped by an arbitrary cap. Removing the cap does not make a stationary well
  a source of unlimited net energy.
- **Engines limit thrust, not momentum.** Each of the three ships has two
  engines whose effectiveness depends on push direction relative to motion.
  Initial estimate: half-effectiveness at 600 units/s along the push; opposing
  and perpendicular thrust remain fully effective.
- **Planets:** strength and reach are independent of physical size. Initial
  planetary acceleration is 300 units/s² at half reach.
- Existing body sizes and well reaches are kept.

## What the comparisons showed

With the original rules, the nearby acceleration was real but the pass did not
retain a speed reward. At entry speed 500 and aim offset 80 the solar pass
peaked at 539 and exited at 317 units/s; the same launch with no sun exited at
346. A direct approach at offset 0 ended in sun contact. A faster exit than the
no-sun reference can still be slower than entry. These are observations of the
fixture, not a verdict on how the game feels.

The designer’s review found appealing loops at low speed, but little visible
influence at entry speed 325 with the original tuning. The aim offset was not
recorded, so this is an experience judgment, not a numerical threshold. It
identified a prerequisite for the slingshot: the well must bend a ship moving at
gameplay speeds. A faster ship spends less time under the pull, and
increasing strength could make the slow loops lethal. The propulsion change
then took priority over tuning well strength, reach and curve shape.

The curve comparison matched both curves at half the well radius, 300 units/s²
at 157.5 units. That match was an experimental control, not a strength rule.

## Ruled out

- The original linear pull, replaced by the bounded inverse-square curve.
- Passive drag and the hard speed cap.

## What remained uncertain

- Retained exit speed was unresolved.
- The comparison slider reached 8×, so 8×–10× was not verified.
- A powered escape and a moving planet needed separate comparisons before the
  coasting result could be generalized to all wells.
- The planet question stayed open: which approaches produce the useful turn and
  exit-speed reward. The hypothesis was that planetary motion can make the
  reward depend on approach side. The trial fixed a small rock planet at radius
  20 and compared a stationary planet with an orbiting one.
- Engine effectiveness and planet strength were initial estimates. Steering and
  braking at high speed still needed to work. The comparisons show trajectory
  behavior, not how the combined game feels.
- New sizing rules, refined planet ranges and visual danger cues were deferred.
- Playtesting was to happen after integration, on the built alpha. It was not a
  prerequisite, and no human playtest result is claimed.

## Where the work went

The game and [Flight Lab](/hangar) use bounded inverse-square wells, no passive
drag or speed cap, and two directional engines per ship. Four physics substeps
per replay tick resolve close passes. Automated checks cover coasting, braking
and outward escape across all three ships, differential steering, contact and
deterministic replay. New scores are isolated in the gravity-1 challenge.
Score-rule and two-player leaderboard checks passed for release 0.3.4. The
gravity field spec, `docs/specs/game/world/gravity-field/spec.md`, states the
intent.

The full study page before conversion is in commit `830d145f`, at `apps/web/src/pages/lab/studies/gravity-wells.astro`.
