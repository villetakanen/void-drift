## 01 / Develop the problem

The drone launcher turns potential into small autonomous craft carrying fission
payloads. A drone pursues an enemy and explodes nearby, or returns to the
launcher to be converted back into energy. Manufacturing takes time, and only
a few drones can be in play at once. The weapon's character comes from this
whole cycle: preparation aboard the ship, independent flight, and either an
explosion or recovery.

The launcher and drone already have their visual direction and approximate
proportions. The study brings those forms into working detail and explores how
the cycle should move and read in flight. How does the launcher communicate its
preparation? What makes the drone's pursuit and return convincing? How do the
pilot's movement and distance from the enemy shape the encounter?

### Form and equipment family

The [original SVG](/studies/drone-launcher-2026-11-25/01-ion-weapons-source.svg)
and reference image preserve the artwork. The open rails, cyan planes, lime
fittings and pointed drone silhouette carry its identity. Mastering needs to
resolve construction, attachment, the drone's position in the launcher, and
release geometry while retaining that character. Exact world dimensions and
how much of the fitting remains visible on a hull need examination at flight scale.

The launcher belongs to the same equipment family as the fission engine. Their
explosions share the nuclear color: in the drone's close attack and in the
engine's plume. The [fission light study](/lab/studies/fission-light-2026-11-11)
provides a related visual reference. The drone itself has an ion drive with a
small plume, giving its travel a different light from its detonation.

### Manufacture, attack and recovery

The launcher acquires targets like other weapons. When it has a target within
range, it starts manufacturing a drone from potential. After a manufacturing
delay, it launches the drone with its fission payload. Deployment is limited
by the number of drones already in play: three is the starting point, with a
higher limit still possible.

An airborne drone heads towards an enemy while that enemy is in range. It
explodes when close enough. Without an enemy in range, it heads back towards
the firing ship if that ship is in range. At the launcher, the returning drone
is converted back into energy. This recovery is what “reload” means in the cycle.
The amount of potential used to manufacture a drone and the energy recovered
on return remain to be established.

These range conditions describe different moments: acquiring a launch target,
pursuing an enemy, returning to the ship, and detonating nearby. Their distances,
measurement origins and relationship need definition. The ship-range condition
also needs clarification: does it govern starting the return or beginning
recovery on arrival? The behaviour when neither destination is in range is open.

### Flight and machinery

The ion drive supplies thrust; turning and rotation control are still unresolved.
A small rotation stabilizer is one possibility. The movement needs to establish
how the drone points towards its destination, accelerates, handles drift and
overshoot, and meets a launcher that is itself moving. Departure velocity,
turning authority, braking and gravity all affect that character.

The launcher and drone must make the cycle understandable at game scale.
Manufacturing, release and recovery need a visible relationship to the machinery.
The small ion plume should help explain flight, while the nuclear explosion
marks the attack. Several drones must remain readable alongside their carrier,
targets and other weapon effects.

### Open design questions

- **Timing and commitment.** How long does manufacture take, when is potential
  spent, and what happens if the target is lost before launch? What pace do
  successive launches give the weapon?
- **Capacity and recovery.** Is the active limit per launcher or per ship? How
  do manufacturing, returning and conversion count towards it? How much energy
  returns, and how quickly can that potential become another drone?
- **Pursuit and return.** What do the range conditions measure? How does a drone
  choose or change its target, and when does it commit to returning? What happens
  when neither an enemy nor its firing ship is in range?
- **Attack and interruption.** What proximity triggers detonation, what does the
  explosion affect, and can a drone be intercepted? What happens if its firing
  ship is lost?
- **Pilot control.** How do Auto/Off and emergency energy affect manufacturing
  and drones already airborne? Which states need explicit feedback beyond the
  machinery, movement and light?

The inquiry centres on one complete cycle, from target acquisition through
manufacture and flight to detonation or recovered energy. Its timing, motion
and visual expression should be considered together: the launcher's form needs
to explain what it does, and the drone's behaviour needs to make sense from the
moving ship that sent it.

### 02.3 / Eject, coast, ignite

The launch presentation and starting values were accepted as reasonable for
continuing the study: 17 u launcher length, 68% attachment, unchanged master
proportions, +70 u/s ejection and 32 u unpowered distance. Launches occur at
1, 3, 5 s and so on. The next drone appears only during the final 0.10 s.

The drone starts at the yellow receiver and follows the moving, turning rails
until its centre reaches the muzzle. At that crossing it inherits both the
carrier’s translation and the muzzle’s sideways velocity from rotation. Coast
distance includes rail travel and is measured relative to that inherited drift;
ignition cannot precede muzzle clearance. With these values, ignition is at
approximately 1.46 s. The original blue gradient remains World artwork; a

### 02.4 / Accepted Fighter motion and plume

The designer selected **Fighter** motion for continuing the study: the production
Fighter’s stabilized turn response, with its 180°/s ceiling and canted thrust.
Releasing a turn counter-burns the remaining rotation without reversing it;
lateral velocity remains. The comparison used repeating left, forward, right,
forward commands, each for 0.5 s relative to ignition. Wasp cut its main drive
while turning; Scout doubled the turn response. Fighter was selected from these
three study adaptations; no production drone handling was adopted.

The retained values are **60 u/s²**, the **271 u/s engine envelope**, and the
**50% Service Ion → Light Ion plume**. The engine envelope tapers acceleration
along world velocity rather than clamping inherited speed; braking and transverse
thrust remain available. The midpoint plume has a 40.8 u full-output source length,
shrinking with the square root of delivered output through the shared FX surface.
The original blue gradient remains separate World artwork, including when the
main drive is off.

### 02.5 / Pursuit, return and drift

One drone now flies autonomously against one moving Scout target. Launch is
allowed only while an enemy is **within 450 u of the carrier**. In flight,
an enemy strictly inside **500 u of the drone** takes priority: pursue it.
Otherwise return towards the carrier if it is strictly inside **500 u of the
drone**. Outside both airborne ranges, switch off the main drive and drift
while the stabilizers settle rotation.

These distances remain suspect and are deliberately adjustable: the menu has
separate launch, pursuit and return ranges. Centre-to-centre measurement is this
step’s working interpretation. Launch includes its boundary (≤450 u); airborne
checks exclude theirs (less than 500 u). Neither measurement origin nor these values
are production targeting decisions.

Each encounter substitutes one launch for the earlier repeating salvo so the
drone’s decisions remain readable. The carrier coasts at 40 u/s. The target
follows a staged smooth route with continuous position and velocity, using
production Scout artwork. Its route is an experiment condition, not enemy AI;
the drone chooses its own commands throughout.

The earliest release is 1 s. If the target is already in launch range, the
accepted seated preview begins at 0.90 s and release is at 1 s. If it enters
range later, the drone appears for 0.10 s before release. Losing range cancels
that preview; release checks range again. There is no manufacturing simulation
or queued launch after a target has left range.

At the default ranges, both encounters launch legitimately at 1 s and ignite
at about 1.47 s. **Long pursuit** pursues until about 8.83 s, then drifts through
14 s: the enemy is about 501 u away and the carrier 726 u away at the transition.
The main plume extinguishes; a remaining −30°/s rotation settles while the
roughly 81 u/s drift continues. **Early departure** switches from Pursuit to
Return at about 3.73 s. It approaches the moving carrier over the remaining
course, reaching about 3 u separation and 41 u/s against the carrier’s 40 u/s
at 14 s. This demonstrates approach and settling, not recovery.

Use 1× to inspect the whole encounter. Higher magnifications are close inspections
and can take objects outside the carrier’s view. The blue dashed circle shows
pursuit range; if return range differs, a yellow circle shows that second
radius. Distances, current command and spin distinguish the decision from the
inertia that follows it. Changing a range or encounter reconstructs the same
selected instant, so the new conditions are applied from the start.

The controller uses the pirate pilot’s bounded planning approach: eight
Off/Left/Right/Forward command sequences, a 1.2 s prediction, and a 0.3 s first
command followed by coasting or forward thrust. It looks again every eight
60 Hz ticks, immediately on destination changes. Its cached response table is
built from the accepted drone’s Fighter steering including starting angular
velocity. Each predicted impulse applies the drone’s actual 271 u/s envelope
to its current velocity. It borrows no pirate hull, standoff distance, hazard
avoidance or energy assumptions. Pursuit aims for the moving target’s centre;
Return also values matching carrier velocity. These are study choices to judge,
not settled drone intelligence.

The accepted launcher, rail attachment, ejection, coast, ignition, blue World
indicator and ion plume remain. The smallest yellow-family star is present,
with the carrier’s course outside its gravity field and no planets. The drone
has no gravity, drag, collision or attack in this step. Approaching the carrier
does not trigger recovery or energy conversion. Manufacture, capacity, target
selection among several enemies and detonation remain later questions.

the fission engine's push. Before a detonation is built into the bench, three
models were asked for a look for that family: Gemini Neon, GPT Astra 6.2 and

| Idea | Proposals | What it claims |
| --- | --- | --- |

| A fixed order in time | Fable, Double flash | Every fission event goes white, then dark, then green. A larger event has a longer dark gap. |

Under the looks, the three agree on several points.

- **The blast belongs to a point in the world.** On a push, the ship leaves
  the light. On a detonation, the light leaves the point where the drone was.
  Fission light 02.1 has the same premise.
- **Stamping has to stop early.** GPT's arithmetic: at a 0.1 second half-life a
  stamp keeps half its light after six ticks and a quarter after twelve. An
  engine that stamps through all eleven ticks of a push is still lit well into
  the wait.
- **Size can be told by something other than radius:** the length of a gap, the
  number of forks, a ring count, one more fracture.
- **A drawn extent promises a danger radius.** GPT and Fable both ask whether
  the detonation damages once at its trigger or holds a radius for several
  ticks. 01 leaves that open.

All three open with a period study. GPT and Fable mark each claim as checked
against a linked source or recalled from memory; Gemini's checks link no
source. This step has not re-checked those sources. The links are in the
original files.

#### Three findings about the layers

These hold for any look and were checked against the code and tokens.

**FX builds light up over ticks.** The surface integrates emission over game
time. At 60 Hz a stamp painted on one tick enters at about a quarter of its
strength, and a source held in one place settles at about 2.2 times its stamp.
None of the three knew this. A flash that must be white on its first frame has
to be live light on LFX, which is how the plasma rifle's eruption gets its
core. A mark stamped once at low opacity, as several of the proposals specify,
would barely show.

**Stacked yellow-green turns towards the interface lime.** Stamps add inside
the FX surface. The engine body's accent `#c6ff32` at twice its strength clips
to `rgb(255,255,100)`: lightness 98, chroma 73, hue 101. Brand lime is
lightness 96, chroma 84, hue 105. Fable found this and its numbers agree with
the tokens; a held source passes that point on its way to 2.2 times. Its
answer is to paint FX stamps greener, around `rgb(96,224,72)`, which goes pale
when stacked. The other interface green is the Android green of the countdown
numerals and the arrival speed line, at hue 151; both of those are FX light
already. To stay apart from both, fission's stacked light would sit between
those hues. Whether fission's light may move away from the engine body's
accent has not been decided.

**LFX can remove light.** Under hard-light over the void, a source channel `s`
above one half displays as `2s − 1`. Below one half it displays nothing and
darkens whatever is beneath it. A deep green has to be painted pale to show at
all, and a dark mark on LFX takes light out of FX and its glow. Production
already uses the darkening for the night side of world bodies. Double flash,
Standing shells and Carbon Paper all depend on it.

#### Against the project's record

The design principles in `docs/design.md` give hulls, natural bodies and light
their own drawing languages. Hulls are
flat planes. Light can swell, overlap and dissolve. The game is not retro and
does not imitate a period look.

The [plasma light study](/lab/studies/plasma-light-2026-11-11) is the nearest
worked case of light being judged here. Its 02.5 removed three geometric marks,
a chain of diamonds, thrown rings and a rake of parallel strokes, as far from
the game, and found a pixel-cell fireball to belong to another genre. Its 02.4
settled that the body of plasma's light is stamped into FX, with live light on
LFX kept for the charge at the nozzle. Its 02.6 carried forward a cached
luminous stamp built as ion's is: a white-hot core, a coloured body and a
transparent edge, with lobes that drift between frames and two of the game's
small sparks on each tick. The plasma rifle's eruption in
production has the same construction: a lobed fireball stamp, drawn live as a
hot core and into FX as a dimmer copy, growing to 58 u in 0.26 seconds. These
are plasma's findings and they do not bind fission.

Most of the ten proposals draw light as flat shapes with hard edges: plates,
discs, bands, wedges and thin lines.

#### Options to carry into the bench

The carried ideas are restated here in the construction plasma arrived at:
cached luminous stamps in FX, a live core on LFX and the game's sparks. They
combine, and each is one change that can be judged on its own. None is built.

1. **A body left behind.** One lumpy green stamp at the point of ignition,
   stamped for the first few ticks and then stopped. The drone's is round and
   sits at the drone's last position, with its ion plume ending on the same
   tick. The engine's is broader than it is long. Sources: all three, fission
   light 02.1, and Spall Press for the proportions and the early stop.
2. **Two beats.** A live white core on LFX for one or two ticks, a dip, then the
   green swell building in FX and peaking later. The dip is longer for a larger
   event. Source: Double flash. The plasma charge bead is the precedent for a
   cached stamp drawn live from engine state.
3. **Arrives whole, cools inward.** The detonation appears at its full extent on
   the first tick and retreats to its point. Plasma's eruption grows from a
   point, so the two families would differ in motion as well as colour.
   Sources: Plate shadow, Standing shells and the Missile Command notes. It
   depends on the detonation having a radius.
4. **Sparks that split.** The game's hard sparks, each dividing once into
   unequal parts. Sources: Daughter Tracks and Chain.
5. **The blast lights its target.** The live core is large enough to wash a
   nearby hull pale for a tick or two. Sources: Carbon Paper's facet tick and
   Plate shadow's overexposed silhouette.

A lobed stamp in green may read as green plasma. Options 2 and 3 are the
candidates for separating the families by time and motion.

This study owns the drone's detonation. The engine's push belongs to the open
[fission light study](/lab/studies/fission-light-2026-11-11); the engine ideas
recorded here are material for it.

#### Their questions

The project can answer some of what the three asked.

- **Sizes.** The drone is about 7.2 by 4.2 u beside a 17 u launcher; a Wasp is
  about 32 u long. The bench's fixed views are 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 CSS px per world
  unit.
- **Glow.** The 12 px blur is CSS presentation on a stage-wide canvas. It does
  not scale with camera zoom.
- **Fade.** A translucent black fill on every timestep, half-life 0.1 seconds,
  with no periodic cleanup.
- **Camera.** The standard camera keeps the ship and its star in view together
  and changes zoom in a few steps. How far a blast left in the world travels
  across the screen depends on the view.
- **LFX order.** Its marks share one canvas and are drawn in pass order,
  world-body light first. The hard-light blend applies to the finished layer.

These remain open.

- Whether the detonation has a damage radius, and for how many ticks.
- Whether fission's light may be greener than the engine body's accent, and
  whether its first frame may be neutral white.
- How much flash, how large and how often, is acceptable with several fission
  engines and drones in view.
- How the nozzle's travel over a push compares with the size of the light left
  behind. It has not been computed for any hull.

### 02.7 / Fission light: what it says, then how it is painted

At 02.6 the study turned from the drone to a question underneath it: the game
has no language for fission light. The drone's detonation and the fission
engine's push will both use that language. Neither is the subject of this step.

The first version of this step, built on 26 November 2026, was a bench that
offered 02.6's marks as controls: a core, leaf shards, branching tracks and
rings, each with its counts, sizes and durations. It was removed the same day.
Nothing on its screen belonged to the game's light, because it began from the
marks. The plasma studies had already found the order that works: settle what
the light has to express, then paint that onto the layers.

#### What fission light says

The model is fission in space, told in arcade language.

1. **Nothing, then everything.** There is no gathering and no warning.
2. **A flash frame, kept local.** At the source is a point too bright to have a
   shape. For a frame or two everything within its reach is caught in that
   light, as an arcade bomb flashes the whole screen, but only around the
   event.
3. **Matter answers.** What faced the flash keeps showing it after the flash is
   gone. The event appears on the things it reached.
4. **The remains leave.** A thin shell moves outward at a speed the eye can
   follow. It is the only part that travels.
5. **The place stays wrong** for as long as the light surface holds it.

It should feel frightening, fatal, final and immoral. In the designer's words,
these are the weapons of a lunatic. Fission light does not aim: it reaches the
target, a bystander and its sender alike.

The physics is mocked from the real thing. Without air there is no fireball, no
blast wave and no sound. Nearly all the energy leaves as one burst of radiation
in every direction, faster than a tick. That is the primary effect. The
secondary effects are what matter does when the burst arrives, and the bomb's
own remains leaving as a shell. There is no third stage, because blast, fire
and fallout need an atmosphere. The double flash recorded in 02.6 also needs
air, so it is not part of this language.

Ion is a line from one place to another, aimed and clean. Plasma is a body of
flame, gathered and thrown. Fission has neither a line nor a body.

Two things are left open in this statement: what the source looks like beyond
its flash, and which part does the harm.

#### How the layers say it

LFX is live light from current state, and the game already uses it to light
solids. FX is what stays in the world and fades. Each part of the statement is
painted with a kind of mark the game already has.

| Part | Layer | Painted as | The game's precedent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The flash | LFX, with a copy in FX | A soft cached stamp, white well past its centre, for two ticks. | The plasma rifle's eruption: a live core and a copy that stays. |
| The flash frame | LFX | Even pale light across the whole reach for one tick, weaker on the next, then gone. | None in the game. It is the arcade screen flash kept local. |
| Lit faces | LFX, with a faint copy in FX | A glow on the side of each ship that faces the charge: white during the flash, then green and cooling. | The glow on a ship's planet-facing side in an atmosphere, and the lit side of a world body. |
| Sparks thrown back | FX | Hard sparks leaving a lit face toward the source. | Impact sparks. |
| The shell | FX for the band and its wake, LFX for its bright front | A coloured band with a narrow bright core, growing from the source to the limit of the reach. | The impact plume: a wide coloured stroke under a narrow pale one. |
| Sparks leaving with the shell | FX | Streaks that follow the shell outward. | Impact sparks; their trails are the surface's memory. |
| The afterglow | FX | The source keeps feeding a cooling glow into the surface. | The trace a plasma charge leaves. |

How much of the flash a ship takes follows one rule. Inside the fatal radius it
takes all of it. Beyond that the share falls with the square of the distance,
and at the reach limit it is nothing. When the shell passes a ship, the ship
answers a second time with a short glow and a few sparks; that answer is strong
only close to the source.

#### Where 02.6 comes in

02.6 read its ideas from pictures. Here each one either found a job inside the
statement or has not been tried yet.

| Idea in 02.6 | In this step |
| --- | --- |
| The white first frame and the arcade flash, from the period notes | The flash and the local flash frame. |

A bare charge goes off at a fixed point in space every 2.5 seconds, the first
at 1.00 s. Three ships are at rest around it: a Scout at 35 u, inside the fatal

- The sparks are study sparks in fission ink, drawn in the form of the game's
  impact sparks. The game's shared spark pool draws only in fire colour.
- One charge costs fewer than ten draw calls a tick on each layer, with all

- The starting values are a first guess. They stay in this browser, and Export

- The flight bench of 02.5 stays live above for the unsettled ranges.

#### What this step leaves open

- Whether this reads as frightening and final, or as a pretty burst.
- What the source is beyond its flash.
- Which part harms: the instant flash, the shell, or both.
- Whether the flash frame over empty space belongs, or only the lit solids.
- How it reads on moving ships and with several charges at once.
- The ideas from 02.6 that are not tried here: shards from a struck face, and
  shadows.
