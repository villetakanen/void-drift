## 01 / Charging commits; cooling still escapes

A ship closes on a target while the Plasma Projector gathers energy. Starting
that charge spends energy: commitment precedes impact. Charging produces timed,
staghorn-like exhaust plumes that arc to the sides, reaching farther than the
main strike with weaker, less reliable damage. That venting happens even if the
main strike never fires. When charged with a target in its close hit zone, the
weapon erupts in a massive plasma explosion immediately ahead of the ship. A
successful strike still vents the staghorns: the weapon cools in either case.
This fictional mechanism connects preparation, destruction and the residual
opportunity after a missed approach.

That sequence is the agreed foundation for this study, not an existing
production weapon or a project decision. The question is how its charge,
concentrated eruption and branching cooling exhaust should look and affect
flight so the weapon feels visceral, overwhelmingly powerful and sharply limited.
The delay comes from preparation; the primary is a close eruption, not a volley
of travelling projectiles. The ship, target and continuing flight must remain
readable. A backward response that checks the approach is a promising observation
from earlier work; its strength and timing remain provisional.

The unresolved work is convincing plasma in the actual game: its substance,
light, motion and connection to the firing ship. The central eruption and timed
staghorn exhaust supply structure, not vector outlines to reproduce. Exact
energy costs, charge and vent timings, reach, damage, recovery and ship response
remain provisional. Existing engine behaviour and shared flight laws stay fixed.
Weapon response acts on the current production flight model, without retuning
gravity, friction or engines; the Flight scene instrument contract
(`docs/specs/lab/flight-scene-instrument/spec.md`) owns that baseline.

### What the example must make visible

- Show approach, energy-spending charge, the target-conditioned main eruption,
  cooling exhaust and continuing flight in the shared Flight scene.
- Show both a successful close strike and loss of the main opportunity after
  charging begins. Exhaust survives either outcome because it vents the charged
  weapon; it is not an unrelated secondary gun.
- Examine damage to targets, debris and newly exposed fragments. Separate actual
  contact and breakup from the appearance of contact.
- Use fixed burns, the timeline and burger-pane study options. No manual piloting,
  schematic, graph or calculation substitutes for a firing example.

Energy spending and target presence are part of the mechanism, not adjacent
scope. Their exact thresholds and balance, target prioritisation, fitting artwork
and production delivery are not settled here. Each round must state any staged
condition or omitted behaviour so a timer cannot silently stand in for the
weapon's target condition. No new manual firing control is implied.

### 02.3 / Gather, erupt, vent

One paid charge gathers at the nose while cooling plasma curls away to the sides.
The main volume ignites immediately ahead of the ship only if the charged weapon
finds a body in its close zone. A brief backward force checks the approach; the
engines and the shared flight laws continue unchanged.

The proposal uses a dense, short eruption and thinner branching exhaust. Their
fresh marks overlap in the shared FX surface, whose decay, screen blend and
Turbulent bloom supply the visible light. No travelling primary front is emitted.
Appearance and response tuning remain proposals awaiting designer feedback.
