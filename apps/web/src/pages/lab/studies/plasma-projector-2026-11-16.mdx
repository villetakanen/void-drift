## 01 / Commit before it lands

A ship closes on a target with momentum still carrying it forward. Using the
projector must be a commitment made before the hit: its effect does not arrive
instantly, and the interval should make anticipation and exposure tangible. The
release should feel extremely visceral, powerful and impactful, yet sharply
limited. The tension is overwhelming force within a narrow opportunity: getting
close and committing should matter, rather than making the weapon feel effortlessly
available whenever a target appears.

These are design requirements, not claims about an existing weapon. Close range,
a perceptible interval before the main impact, and constrained power are fixed intent. How the discharge gathers, erupts, reaches
its target and dies away must be seen alongside what firing does to the ship's
movement. Exact timing, reach, duration and visible recovery remain provisional.
A backward push that checks the approach is one hypothesis, not a requirement.
The hull, target and continuing flight must stay readable through the violence.
The result is one proposed firing event whose light, rhythm and ship response
make both its power and its limits felt.

Existing ship flight, engine behaviour and rendering remain the baseline; shared
motion laws are not reopened. Every round shows one proposal firing in the real
Flight scene, using fixed burns, its timeline and burger-pane options, without
manual piloting. Approach, commitment, release and aftermath belong in the same
example. Damage belongs in this inquiry: main discharge and secondary exhaust must be
examined against targets, debris and newly exposed fragments. Damage strength,
reach and persistence are open design choices; final balance numbers remain
provisional. Energy funding, target-selection rules and fitting artwork remain
adjacent unless the firing event requires them to be reconsidered.

### Exhaust beyond the main strike

The firing event includes secondary exhaust plumes that arc outward to the sides,
reach farther than the main discharge and can cause weaker, much less reliable
damage. They remain an opportunity even when the main attack misses or its
commitment is not completed. This is retained design intent for this cycle, not
proof that the previous study demonstrated the behaviour or a decision for the
project. The secondary reach should make a near miss interesting without erasing
the main strike’s demand for a committed close approach.

Their shape, timing, spread and relationship to an incomplete main discharge
remain open. Each round must show the main strike and side plumes as one firing
event, including a missed or incomplete main strike where their independent
opportunity can be judged. Whether the plumes actually intercept a target must be
checked separately from how convincing their light looks; damage amounts remain
provisional.
