## 01 / A heavy discharge with a continuing consequence

The Plasma Projector should be a heavy weapon whose charge commits the pilot
before impact. Its concentrated close eruption and paired residual exhausts
need a recognizable identity beside the production plasma engine. The ship,
target and continuing flight must remain readable through the release.

The working action gathers energy, delivers a brief destructive explosion and
strong backward kick when a target is close, then vents at lower output. A lost
opportunity leaves loading and venting without the main strike. **Venting applies
continuous backward thrust:** its continuing effect on velocity was accepted in
02.2. One weapon occupies one Wasp equipment slot. Both its kick and venting push
backward without adding rotation, making the off-axis weapon predictable to fly.
Magnitude, duration and decay remain provisional. The stag-beetle concept
supplies the central-strike / paired-exhaust relationship; its schematic contours
and initial plume directions do not prescribe the finished light in motion.

The open question is the visual identity of this whole action: preparation,
release and cooling should belong to one machine, remain distinct from propulsion,
and share the engine's plasma material. Whole visual proposals use the same
weapon response and production flight/rendering. Explicitly labelled engine
conditions may isolate weapon motion; they do not propose changes to game engines.

Energy spending, recovery and weaker residual damage remain required mechanism
work beyond this appearance trial. The retired flight example did not implement those
costs. Target prioritisation, fitting artwork and production delivery remain
outside this visual inquiry. Decisions here apply only within the study.

## 02.1 / The stag-beetle sequence in flight

Putting the projector beside production plasma engines made their family
relationship judgeable. That comparison is retained. The contours, initial
exhaust direction, timing and force model were not settled by this example.

The example used a short primary between long, curved shoulders, with a gathering
bead and a 70 u/s release kick. Its material comparison was useful; its inherited
composition did not establish a model for the weapon. In particular, it did not
show cooling as a smaller, longer backward push. Plasma's accumulated FX and the
ship's movement need to be judged together.

## 02.2 / Venting continues to move the ship

17 November: this round replaced fixed plume contours with an action built from
loading, a brief eruption and residual venting. Its accepted finding is that
**venting must apply continuous backward thrust**, including after a lost target.
The sustained effect on velocity gives the aftermath a continuing mechanical
consequence. This holds inside the study; the exact thrust curve, duration and
visual treatment remain provisional.

The force comparison was useful once its purpose was clear. Its controls have
been retired. The plasma-family comparison from 02.1 and this continuous-thrust
finding both support the current question; neither accepts an entire appearance.

## 02.3 / The effects need a weapon to belong to

Isolating the antler-like vents and removing main-engine propulsion made their
form and continuous backward push easier to distinguish. Twin jets and Open bell
both remained promising; neither was rejected or selected as the final appearance.
The useful result is the dependency revealed by the comparison: **the weapon's
physical form, its mount position and the origins of its emissions must be judged
together.** Detached effects cannot establish that relationship.

One projector occupies one left or right Wasp equipment slot. Both vents belong
to that one weapon. Its main kick and continuous venting thrust act backward
without adding rotation; this deliberate simplification makes the weapon more
predictable to fly. The left slot was used in the example.

<summary>Retired experiment and remaining uncertainty</summary>

The prototype gathered light from 1.00 to 1.68 s. A close target allowed a
0.30 s eruption and 210 u/s backward kick, followed by cooling. A miss went
directly from loading into cooling. Production simulation, target breakup and
shared FX history supplied flight and accumulated light. Fresh emission followed
the left slot; the primary was born ahead of it in world space.

Appearance switching preserved motion, and replay reconstructed weapon forces.
The dud condition exposed continuous reverse acceleration without weapon torque.
Its later B3 trajectory entered the Sun, so the retired fixture does not establish
full-flight survivability. Spending, rearming and residual damage were not implemented.

The reported above/right FX displacement remains an observation to revisit with
a visible fitting and named outlets. The mount itself lies 7.8 u above the ship's
centreline; that off-axis placement is intended. No general FX-layer correction
was established. No production weapon, renderer or flight law changed here.

used as the shared reference. Carry forward continuous venting thrust, the
