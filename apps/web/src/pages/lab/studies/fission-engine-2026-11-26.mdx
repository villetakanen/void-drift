## 01 / Develop the problem

Only the fission engine’s body is taken as given: the part keeps the look it
has in the part catalogue. Everything else about it is open.

Today the engine is an unsanctioned part that thrusts in short pushes with a
wait between them: 0.18 s of thrust in every 0.45 s, at about 1.9 times a
Light ion’s thrust. It paints the amber default beam and in flight looks like
an ion engine that flickers. The study takes that behaviour as an early
hypothesis: the engine probably thrusts in pulses, and whether it should is
part of what is studied here.

The inspiration is the Thraddash Torch from Star Control 2. Its afterburner
throws the ship forward and leaves a line of fire behind it that harms ships
that touch it. The drone launcher study gave fission a detonation light: a
white flash, then green that lingers along spark tracks. The engine has no
light of its own yet. Odd side effects are welcome.

- Does the engine thrust in pulses, and what does one burn do to the ship?
- What does the burn leave in the world, and whom does that affect?
- How does the engine’s light relate to the drone’s detonation, and stay apart
  from ion and plasma light?
- Which specs would own the result?

An [earlier study](/lab/studies/fission-light-2026-11-11) tried one light for
the pulse alone. This study takes the whole engine.

## 02 / Explore

### 02.1 / The catalogue body as an SVG master

The engine’s body is 17 flat faces in each of four lightings: the catalogue
pose, two side-lit poses and one lit from aft. Until now those faces existed
only as coordinate lists in the game’s engine art. This step copies them,
unchanged and in drawing order, into one SVG master that follows the
conventions of the service ion’s master.

<MasterSpecimen />

The master is 22 engine units long and 9.6 wide, with the nose towards +x.
The lime emitter bar at the rear takes the fission accent, `#c6ff32`, from the
file’s root colour, the way the game supplies it when drawing.

Read back through the game’s engine-master converter, the file returns the
same faces, colours and order that the game draws today, in all four poses.
The game does not read the master yet. The body’s form is unchanged.

### 02.2 / What kind of engine is fission?

This step is research on paper. It compares how each engine family answers the
pilot’s input today, then sets out options for fission. Nothing is built and
no option is selected.

The figures are the catalogue’s on 26 November 2026. Thrust is given as a
multiple of the Light ion’s.

| Engine | Mass | Thrust | Draw | Cruise | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service ion | 7 | 0.33× | 0.58 | +1 | steady |
| Light ion | 10 | 1× | 1.75 | +8 | steady |
| Heavy ion | 20 | 1.75× | 3.5 | +11 | steady |
| Plasma | 24 | 1.5× | 5 | +9 | charged, 0.2 s |
| Fission | 16 | 1.9× per push, 0.76× averaged | 3 | +17 | pulsed |

An ion engine thrusts while the input is held and stops when it is released.
The three ions are one engine at three sizes. Plasma gives no thrust or
steering until the input has been held through its charge, then burns
strongly; releasing discards the charge. The pilot chooses when to commit.

Fission pushes on its own clock while held. Releasing cuts a push short and
the wait still runs. The pilot decides nothing that an ion pilot does not, and
gets less average thrust than a Light ion. Its catalogue figures point
elsewhere: the highest cruise contribution of any engine, at middling mass and
draw.

#### How a burn answers the input

| Option | While held | On release | What the pilot decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clocked pushes, as today | Pushes repeat on the engine’s clock | The push is cut short; the wait still runs | When to hold, as with an ion |
| Whole burns | Burns repeat | A lit burn runs to its end | Whether to light the next burn |
| One burn per press | One burn; holding adds nothing | Nothing changes | Every burn |

With whole burns or one burn per press, thrust arrives in fixed amounts. Fine
corrections become hard, and a side-mounted pair turns the ship in kicks. The
three families would then differ in when thrust obeys: ion at once, plasma
after a wait, fission with no way to cancel. The flight pads are held, which
favours repeating burns over a press for each.

The Thraddash Torch suggests a fourth arrangement: an ordinary drive with the
burn as an extra on top. A fission engine could give weak steady thrust and
add burns to it. That would make it two engines in one part.

#### What a burn leaves behind

Today every engine’s light is painted where it was emitted and fades there. It
affects nothing.

| Option | Effect | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light only | None | Fission differs from ion and plasma in appearance and rhythm alone |
| A wake that harms other ships | Damage to a ship that crosses it | The engine is also a rear weapon. Pirates chase the pilot in Survival and Gauntlet, so it would work without being aimed, and outside the weapons toggle |
| A wake that harms everything | Damage to any ship or drone, the pilot’s included | A tight turn crosses the pilot’s own wake, and a returning drone flies up it to be recovered |

A burn that stays at a place has the same nature as the drone’s detonation:
one fission event at a point. The engine would lay a row of smaller ones.

#### What a burn costs

| Option | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Draw while firing, as today | Fission is priced like every other engine, at 3 |
| A price per burn | Each burn is a countable spend, and a burn the ship cannot pay for does not light |

#### Open after this step

- Does the pilot author each burn, or does holding repeat them?
- Is a wake that harms an engine’s business? The catalogue separates engines
  from weapons, and weapon rules own damage.
- If the wake harms, whom, for how long, and does it spare the ship that made
  it?
