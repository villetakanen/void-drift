## 01 / Develop the problem

Only the fission engine’s body is taken as given: the part keeps the look it
has in the part catalogue. Everything else about it is open.

Today the engine is an unsanctioned part that thrusts in short pushes with a
wait between them: 0.18 s of thrust in every 0.45 s, at about 1.9 times a
Light ion’s thrust. It paints the amber default beam and in flight looks like
an ion engine that flickers. The study takes that behaviour as an early
hypothesis: the engine probably thrusts in pulses, and whether it should is
part of what is studied here.

The inspiration is the Thraddash Torch from Star Control 2. Its afterburner
throws the ship forward and leaves a line of fire behind it that harms ships
that touch it. The drone launcher study gave fission a detonation light: a
white flash, then green that lingers along spark tracks. The engine has no
light of its own yet. Odd side effects are welcome.

- Does the engine thrust in pulses, and what does one burn do to the ship?
- What does the burn leave in the world, and whom does that affect?
- How does the engine’s light relate to the drone’s detonation, and stay apart
  from ion and plasma light?
- Which specs would own the result?

An [earlier study](/lab/studies/fission-light-2026-11-11) tried one light for
the pulse alone. This study takes the whole engine.

## 02 / Explore

### 02.1 / The catalogue body as an SVG master

The engine’s body is 17 flat faces in each of four lightings: the catalogue
pose, two side-lit poses and one lit from aft. In the game those faces exist
only as coordinate lists in its engine art. The SVG master holds them
unchanged and in drawing order, following the conventions of the service
ion’s master.

<MasterSpecimen />

The master is 22 engine units long and 9.6 wide, with the nose towards +x.
The lime emitter bar at the rear takes the fission accent, `#c6ff32`, from the
file’s root colour, the way the game supplies it when drawing.

Read back through the game’s engine-master converter, the file returns the
same faces, colours and order that the game draws today, in all four poses.
The game does not read the master yet. The body’s form is unchanged.

### 02.2 / What kind of engine is fission?

How each engine family answers the pilot’s input today, and the options that
leaves for fission.

The figures are the catalogue’s on 26 November 2026. Thrust is given as a
multiple of the Light ion’s.

| Engine | Mass | Thrust | Draw | Cruise | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service ion | 7 | 0.33× | 0.58 | +1 | steady |
| Light ion | 10 | 1× | 1.75 | +8 | steady |
| Heavy ion | 20 | 1.75× | 3.5 | +11 | steady |
| Plasma | 24 | 1.5× | 5 | +9 | charged, 0.2 s |
| Fission | 16 | 1.9× per push, 0.76× averaged | 3 | +17 | pulsed |

An ion engine thrusts while the input is held and stops when it is released.
The three ions are one engine at three sizes. Plasma gives no thrust or
steering until the input has been held through its charge, then burns
strongly; releasing discards the charge. The pilot chooses when to commit.

Fission pushes on its own clock while held. Releasing cuts a push short and
the wait still runs. The pilot decides nothing that an ion pilot does not, and
gets less average thrust than a Light ion. Its catalogue figures point
elsewhere: the highest cruise contribution of any engine, at middling mass and
draw.

#### How a burn answers the input

| Option | While held | On release | What the pilot decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clocked pushes, as today | Pushes repeat on the engine’s clock | The push is cut short; the wait still runs | When to hold, as with an ion |
| Whole burns | Burns repeat | A lit burn runs to its end | Whether to light the next burn |
| One burn per press | One burn; holding adds nothing | Nothing changes | Every burn |

With whole burns or one burn per press, thrust arrives in fixed amounts. Fine
corrections become hard, and a side-mounted pair turns the ship in kicks. The
three families would then differ in when thrust obeys: ion at once, plasma
after a wait, fission with no way to cancel. The flight pads are held, which
favours repeating burns over a press for each.

The Thraddash Torch suggests a fourth arrangement: an ordinary drive with the
burn as an extra on top. A fission engine could give weak steady thrust and
add burns to it. That would make it two engines in one part.

#### What a burn leaves behind

Today every engine’s light is painted where it was emitted and fades there. It
affects nothing.

| Option | Effect | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light only | None | Fission differs from ion and plasma in appearance and rhythm alone |
| A wake that harms other ships | Damage to a ship that crosses it | The engine is also a rear weapon. Pirates chase the pilot in Survival and Gauntlet, so it would work without being aimed, and outside the weapons toggle |
| A wake that harms everything | Damage to any ship or drone, the pilot’s included | A tight turn crosses the pilot’s own wake, and a returning drone flies up it to be recovered |

A burn that stays at a place has the same nature as the drone’s detonation:
one fission event at a point. The engine would lay a row of smaller ones.

#### What a burn costs

| Option | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Draw while firing, as today | Fission is priced like every other engine, at 3 |
| A price per burn | Each burn is a countable spend, and a burn the ship cannot pay for does not light |

#### Open after this step

- Does the pilot author each burn, or does holding repeat them?
- Is a wake that harms an engine’s business? The catalogue separates engines
  from weapons, and weapon rules own damage.
- If the wake harms, whom, for how long, and does it spare the ship that made
  it?

### 02.3 / The explosion and its plume

**Studied.** What the engine’s push looks like as a nuclear explosion. It goes
off outside the ship, or in the engine’s reaction channel, and throws the ship
violently forward. Three plumes were compared on a Fighter with Fission in its
left aft slot and Service ions on its sides. The flight was the game’s as it
stands: one explosion at the start of each push, every 0.45 s while the engine
is held.

**Known.** The drone’s detonation gives fission its light. Its flash is a
white core 14 u in radius, with pale light over everything within 180 u and
hulls lit on the side that faces the blast. It lasts two ticks and is softer
on the second. Green stays on the FX surface, pale tips ride on LFX, and
sparks 4.5 u wide travel at about 226 u/s and fork 32% of the way along. The
Fighter is about 31 u long.

Each plume opened with that flash and was built from those marks.

| Plume | Where the charge goes off | What follows the flash |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burst astern | In open space, 22 u behind the nozzle | Fourteen sparks leave in every direction the hull does not shade. Three ticks later the hull throws six of its own back at the blast. |
| **Channel blast** | Inside the reaction channel | A white lance down the exhaust’s line, 180 u long within seven ticks, that leaves its length in green. Seven sparks in a cone 9° either side, at one and a half times the drone’s speed. |
| Fire line | 10 u behind the nozzle | The place burns white for a fifth of a second. A green fire is fed there for one second, and its glow swells from 28 u to 52 u in radius as it fades. No sparks. |

Light stayed where it was made and did not carry the ship’s speed. It affected
nothing it touched.

**Learned.** Channel blast is the engine’s plume.

#### The field

The Flight scene instrument’s default scene builds its system from the ranked
challenge seed. That gives a G14 star whose gravity reaches 840 u of the
arena’s 1,190 u radius. A ship 500 u out feels a pull of 141 u/s², about two
fifths of the fission engine’s own 336 u/s² on this Fighter. Burn B3 from
there ends in the star at 7.6 s.

In the Practice system the G7 Sun’s gravity reaches 324 u. From Practice’s own
start, 500 u from the Sun, burn B1 strikes The Cradle at 2.2 s. From 90 u
further out, B1 passes The Cradle at 2.6 s with 36 u between hull and surface,
and B2 passes Venus at 8.4 s with 37 u. Both bend in those planets’ gravity
and neither enters the Sun’s. B3 strikes The Cradle at 6.3 s. The plumes were
compared from that start.

### 02.4 / The channel blast, part by part

**Studied.** The shape of the channel blast’s three parts, each on its own
and together, on 02.3’s flight. Is the blast open, or directed by a conical
chamber? How wide can the particles leave before they stop reading as thrown
through a nozzle? Which start belongs to the lance: a flame against the
nozzle, or a drop?

**Known.** 02.3’s channel blast, in the third column below.

The assumed flash is directed aft. Its core is nearly twice as wide as
02.3’s, and its light over space is dimmer. A cone leaves the hull outside the
flash, so the hull stays dark. Each tick of a flash after the first is softer.

The assumed particles leave in a wider cone and their branches stay close
together. A particle travels a first length, then splits into a long and a
short branch; the short one travels about a quarter as far. Lengths vary by a
fifth either way from one particle to the next.

The assumed lance is a little longer and narrower than 02.3’s, and its far end
is drawn out thin. Its widest point stayed near the nozzle: a flame, not a
drop. Length and width are measured to the edge of the glow; the bright part
is narrower. The white lance grows from two fifths of its length to the whole
of it and thins as it goes.

### 02.5 / The kick

**Studied.** How the engine flies, from one premise: it kicks the moment the
pads go down, and a kick cannot be taken back. How large and how sudden is a
kick? What does holding the pads do? Does the engine keep kicking at the
ceiling? A Wasp flies with Fission as its one engine, in a field with no
gravity, and each kick sets off one explosion with 02.4’s light.

**Known.** Each of the game’s five engines alone on the Wasp, with both pads
held from rest and no gravity. Speed is in u/s.

| Engine | 0.1 s | 0.2 s | 0.5 s | 1 s | 2 s | 3 s | 6 s | Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service ion | 12 | 24 | 58 | 108 | 188 | 248 | 350 | 421 |
| Light ion | 35 | 67 | 148 | 244 | 349 | 394 | 425 | 428 |
| Heavy ion | 52 | 98 | 205 | 312 | 398 | 422 | 431 | 431 |
| Plasma | 0 | 0 | 117 | 245 | 365 | 407 | 428 | 429 |
| Fission | 59 | 102 | 124 | 212 | 318 | 366 | 425 | 437 |

The engines differ in when speed arrives and hardly in how much: all but the
Service ion are within 6 u/s of each other at 6 s. Today’s fission push is a
Heavy ion’s thrust for a fifth of a second, 102 u/s against 98 at 0.2 s,
followed by 0.27 s of nothing. From 0.5 s to 3 s it trails the Light ion.
Releasing the pads cuts a push short.

Every engine shares one cruise ceiling, and thrust along the direction of
travel shrinks to nothing at it. Fission’s pushes therefore gain less each
time: 102, 77, 59, 46, 35 u/s and on down. Nothing in the game slows a ship
that is above its ceiling.

**Learned.** The values in the last column are the working assumption for how
the engine flies, as of 26 November 2026.

| Property | Range studied | Today’s engine | Assumed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed one kick adds | 20 to 300 u/s | 116 u/s | 78 u/s |
| Kick delivered over | 1 to 11 ticks | 11 ticks | 1 tick |
| Wait from one kick to the next | 0.3 to 2 s | 0.45 s | 0.30 s |
| Holding the pads | Repeats while held, or one kick per press | Repeats | Repeats |
| At the ceiling | Tapers, overshoots, or holds fire | Tapers | Holds fire |

Today’s engine written as a kick flies within 1% of the game’s fission: 59,
102, 126, 215, 322, 369 and 426 u/s at the table’s seven times.

A kick delivered over one tick leaves nothing to cut short. The wait runs from
the kick whatever the pads do, so letting go and pressing again does not bring
the next kick sooner. With one kick per press, burn B1 gives three kicks and
B3 six; repeating at today’s wait, B1 gives fifteen.

The three rules at the ceiling fly differently.

- **Tapers** is the game’s rule for every engine. A 150 u/s kick from rest
  lands 132 u/s, and later kicks land less while their explosions stay the
  same size.
- **Overshoots** lands every kick whole and lets the ship pass its ceiling.
  The excess then has to be drained by a rule of its own.
- **Holds fire** sets a kick off only when the whole of it fits under the
  ceiling. With 150 u/s kicks, two fit under 437 u/s and the engine then stays
  silent at 300 u/s for as long as the pads are held.

Holding fire sets this engine apart from every other. Held from rest on the
Wasp, the assumed engine reads:

| Engine | 0.1 s | 0.2 s | 0.5 s | 1 s | 2 s | 3 s | 6 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fission, assumed | 78 | 78 | 156 | 312 | 390 | 390 | 390 |
| Heavy ion | 52 | 98 | 205 | 312 | 398 | 422 | 431 |
| Fission today | 59 | 102 | 124 | 212 | 318 | 366 | 425 |

Five kicks fit under the 437 u/s ceiling. The fifth lands 1.2 s after the pads
go down and takes the ship to 390 u/s; a sixth would pass the ceiling, so the
engine falls silent while the pads stay down. The assumed engine is the
fastest of the five in the first tenth of a second and level with the Heavy
ion at 1 s. At 2 s only the Heavy ion is ahead of it. From 3 s on it is the
slowest but the Service ion, 47 u/s under its own ceiling. It kicks again only
once the ship has lost speed or turned its heading away from its travel.

Kicks of every size set off the same explosion; the light does not follow the
kick’s strength.

The field is the default arena with its star at the smallest of its family
and no planets. The ship starts 1,100 u from the star, whose gravity reaches
124 u.
