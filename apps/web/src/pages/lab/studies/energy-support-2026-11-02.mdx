**Stopped · 2 November 2026.** The designer closed this cycle without accepting a visual design. A revised brief went to a separate cycle; see [Ship reserves: capacity made physical](/lab/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02) for the accepted design, not a continuation of the rejected capacitor artwork.

## 01 / Extra energy needs a character

The twin-tank placeholder showed where the fitting went, not what it should be. The brief was for a static, solid, flat-plane object that could be read above or below Wasp, Scout, Fighter and Corvette without animation, sound or effects. The [utility family](/lab/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02#family-colour) supplied its established accent: **#CEDB17** on connectors, struts and attachments, without prescribing a silhouette.

A ship starts with 100 energy. The normal fitting adds 85 and the large adds 140 at twice the mass. The devices should look almost identical while the larger one looks heavier. These were givens, not balance questions; the study did not set absolute mass, charging or discharge rules.

## 02 / What could carry that reserve?

### 02.1 / One device, two densities

Layered packing and a single contained body were two ways to show about 65% more capacity for 100% more mass without simply doubling the drawing. Neither was selected. The comparison exposed a missing design decision: the numbers alone did not say what kind of object was being drawn. Without that character, the drawings were hard to judge beyond relative heft.

<h3 id="meaning">02.2 / What stores this much energy?</h3>

The naming exercise separated function from construction: an accumulator names storage, a cell names a unit, a lattice names a medium, and a capacitor names a principle. Field, phase and folded-space ideas offered other kinds of containment. None established a real mechanism, charge rate, output bonus or failure behaviour. The useful question was what the object holds and how its construction can suggest that, rather than inventing another description of the benefit of having extra capacity.

<h3 id="void-and-well">02.3 / Void capacitor and potential well</h3>

The capacitor sketch held tension across a captive void; the well sketch held energy in confined space. This round tentatively combined them under **Void capacitor**: interleaved lattices of potential held apart by void wells. The internal explanation was provisional and did not select an exterior. The name and fiction were later reopened, not carried forward as an accepted part.

![Two fictional technology sketches: a void capacitor with opposing masses held apart by restraints, and a potential well with nested construction around a dark recess.](/studies/energy-support-2026-11-02/02-3-void-and-well.png)

*Association sketches only; neither exterior is a mounting or size reference.*

<h3 id="lattice-construction">02.4 / Exposed lattices were rejected</h3>

Three broad bodies were separated by dark gaps and held by yellow-green retainers. The large version deepened the containment while keeping the same mounting centre and three-part rhythm. The review found that the bars and frame neither belonged to the support family nor read as convincing low-poly volume, enlarged or small. Mounting also exposed a separate constraint: on a hull that hides the centre, the edges have to carry the device’s identity.

<h3 id="containment-pivot">02.5 / Keep the lattices inside</h3>

The last proposal was to draw the containment instead: one low, sealed body with a raised ridge, broad sloping shoulders and two substantial structural clamps. Give the large version broader shoulders and a higher ridge while keeping the connection and clamp positions. This was a written hypothesis, never a selected drawing. The next comparison would have needed a convincing isolated pair beside an established engine and support item before returning to hull fit; the study stopped before that work.
