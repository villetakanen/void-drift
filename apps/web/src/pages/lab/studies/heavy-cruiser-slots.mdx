**Accepted · 30 October 2026.** The designer approved the layout direction and requested integration planning. The visual quality remained unsatisfactory, and acceptance did not establish flight handling.

## Question

How can eight mounts and a multi-window bridge make a slim, armored cruiser express overwhelming power?

The reference was a long, streamlined naval battleship: protected and weighty rather than agile like the Fighter. Imperial light battleships were a mood reference, without the triangular wedge.

## Accepted direction

Carry forward **08 / Long bevel**, its elongated proportions, facets and two black bow panes. The cockpit sits forward. A hull mount, H1, sits ahead of it; H2 and H3 remain on the deck. The side engines move forward, and the aft mounts use **D**: the center mount sits ahead of the outer pair. The forward engine axes point outward, away from the deck. The designer judged 08 good enough to proceed, despite its visual polish.

The eight mounts comprise two required side engines, three hull mounts for guns or utilities, and three aft mounts that also accept engines. Their spatial relationships were accepted; the screenshot-derived coordinates were starting values, not final dimensions. Across and fore–aft values use top-view drawing units.

Reference footprints were 9 × 14 for side mounts and 9 × 11 for hull and aft mounts. H1 extended beyond the narrow bow and needed an armored support or modest bow change; the final polygon treatment remained provisional. The forward engines would change steering leverage, so their spatial fit did not establish good handling.

## Ruled out

The axial arrangement was chosen over paired shoulders. A bridge behind the fittings was rejected because the cockpit needed to be clearly at the front. Of the first six hulls, only **01 / Iron keel** showed some promise. The gallery appeared too large, its mounts too small and far apart, and its cockpit and facets did not achieve the intended sleek low-poly character. Of the six compact refinements, the designer retained 08 and set the other five aside.

## What remained uncertain

Installed-part and firing-line clearance, plume clearance, bow-support finish, collision fairness, flight-scale fidelity, handling and turning response still needed examination. Dimensions and tuning remained provisional, as did the hull’s visual polish.

## Where the work went

After the designer requested local delivery, shared cruiser geometry, armored bow support, mounts, nozzle positions, collision coverage and builder selection were connected, preserving tank IDs and slot indices. Deployment was not claimed. Fidelity and handling remained to be assessed. The [builder and test flight](/hangar) show the delivered cruiser.
