## Outcome / Failed in exploration

The designer stopped the study after 02.12. In their assessment, “the loop persists. we are still over-correcting on chat discussions.” The pivot to three drawings from the vocabulary of 1990s game skies left the appearance question unresolved. No drawing was selected.

One decision held within the study. Eleven lights crowded the playable arena. Spread across the whole study sky, one orange light was visible in the opening Follow view and the designer accepted that density. The matched departures are retained in [02.3](#eleven-in-flight) and [02.4](#whole-sky). The study ended before readiness or scope.

</section>

## 01 / Drawing starlight in a handful of marks

G-class stars in ranked play are part of the intent for the final *Fires in the Sky* release. In the background, eleven close suns should bring a little warm brilliance to the existing grey starmap. During the whole-sky trial, even one orange light registered in a flight view. The family trial still read as brown smudges. Colour and spacing gave the population a place in the sky; the drawing of a single light remains open.

Use the supplied vocabulary of 1990s game skies and demoscene effects as drawing material. A pinprick can hold nearly all its light in one sample. A cross can put white at the centre and gold in the four neighbours. A starburst can spend more of its footprint on rays, with a palette change bringing them briefly into view. What does each construction convey when the pilot sees it moving among the ordinary stars?

Judge the marks at flight size. Keep the eleven positions across the whole study sky, the ordinary field’s 65% camera pan and the matched departure. The close plane travels a quarter farther than the ordinary field. The shape, distribution of light and behaviour of a gleam are open. The result would guide how the game draws these eleven background stars.

<details id="optics">
<summary>02.1 / Steady spill and passing glint</summary>

Both sketches use a warm-white pinlight. **Steady spill** gives it a gold fringe and thin, unequal rays. **Passing glint** draws those rays out briefly, then shrinks them back to the point. Each star gleams on its own beat. The demoscene idea here is economical: one little light sprite, drawn at different sizes.

<details id="eleven-in-flight">
<summary>02.3 / Eleven lights on the moving map</summary>

Watch the same departure twice. The first pass shows the ordinary points at 65% pan. The second adds eleven close stars across the arena. A few cross this flight path. They slide a quarter farther than the grey field, borrowing the relative motion of the nearer pair in the earlier photographic trial.

Each close star is a tiny three-colour sprite. A white-hot centre holds between flashes. A few times during the pass, a short amber line catches the light and draws back in steps. The stars flash out of phase. Does their presence give the moving starmap the small lift we wanted?

This extent is a study choice to try on the same B1 departure.

<summary>02.9 / Light left behind by the pan</summary>

<section id="drawing-pivot">

## 02.11 / From colouring the field to drawing starlight

19 November 2026 · The family comparison varied the brightness of soft discs. The amended question asks what an artist can do with the centre, neighbouring points and rays of a bright sky star. The supplied references give us constructions to draw and compare during flight.

**Evidence for the amended question.** The steady and passing optics in 02.1–02.2 and the drawings in 02.5–02.8 show how earlier marks read at flight scale. The family in 02.10 shows that varying the colour and size of a soft disc left its character unresolved.

**Conditions carried forward.** The density comparisons in 02.3–02.4 settled placement. The motion trail in 02.9 explored persistence; its result remains outside the immediate comparison of star drawings.

## 02.12 / Pinprick, cross, burst

Three drawings spend their light differently. The pinprick concentrates it in one sample. The cross puts a white centre between four gold neighbours and lets the outer tips catch light. The burst carries its light along four long rays and four short diagonals. For each drawing, a three-frame palette cycle lifts the gold towards cream, then settles. The stars keep individual phases.
