**Accepted · 11 November 2026** (date from Git history). The subject is the bespoke Hangar tool for choosing the part for a slot, not a reusable list pattern. The result is the card in [02.18](#empty), proposed for the Hangar. Study choices do not themselves authorize implementation.

## 01 / The question

<a id="nb"></a>

In the [Hangar](/hangar), pressing a part fits it immediately. Unsanctioned parts can be fitted with Playground on, when play is unranked. Turning Playground off unfits nothing: an unsanctioned part still fitted blocks launch until removed or replaced. The tool must distinguish the part, its fitted state and its eligibility for ranked play. The [ship-slots study](/lab/studies/ship-slots-2026-11-11) addresses the corresponding mark on the ship.

<h2 id="settled">02 / The result</h2>

<h3 id="empty">02.18 / The card and its states</h3>

- The card is always **300 pixels wide**. Where the Hangar lacks width, the estimates column gives way first; the ship chooser and card keep their sizes.
- A small drawing sits at the top left with 4 pixels around it, the name beside it, two lines of prose under the name, and figures along the bottom. The drawing helps distinguish parts, including ion from plasma by colour; the larger picture is already on the ship. Prose says what the part is about and carries no figures.
- One to four key game facts appear as icons and numbers, mass first, in four columns on one line. Figures are 12 pixels, with 16 pixels to the left and right and 8 above and below. The seven example icons were placeholders; the twelve short descriptions were unapproved drafts, not settled game copy.
- Cards have the button's cut shape and brackets but no fill of their own, on a pane at the first surface step. The gap between cards is 8 pixels. Pointer and keyboard focus light the brackets; holding tints the card. A fitted card has a black ground and lit lime brackets, without a bottom bar or a Fitted word. Its ground and brackets fade over the same short time as the game's buttons when the fitted state changes.
- A **10-pixel hatched band**, as wide as the corner cut, marks an unsanctioned part. It takes the colour of the component's border, or the colour its border would have if there is none, and follows changes to that colour. This rule was made part of the game's design language on 11 November 2026.
- An unsanctioned part still fitted with Playground off has a faint red tint, red brackets and a red band. It must be removed or replaced before launch.
- Unsanctioned parts remain listed after sanctioned parts with Playground off, but cannot be pressed. The whole unavailable card, including the band, fades to one quarter strength; a pointer does nothing and nothing is turned grey. Hiding these parts was rejected: a part vanishing when another is pressed looks wrong.
- A short **Keep empty** card starts the list, using the part-name type and no drawing. Pressing it empties the slot; it is fitted when the slot is empty. A required slot, such as an engine, has no Keep empty card; that case was not shown.

<h3 id="terms">02.5 / Terms</h3>

The terms were settled in review on 11 November and recorded in the game spec; screens and code had not then been changed to match:

- **Slot**, with slot types Engine, Weapon, Support, Hardpoint (weapon or support), and Universal (any part); Equipment is dropped as a slot type.
- **Part**, or ship part; its kinds are engine, weapon and support. A part is **fitted** to a slot and removed.
- **Ship** is the body, **fitting** the parts on it, and **build** the two together. Setup and loadout go; one part is never “a fitting”. Chassis is unwanted in code too. The ship that will fly is the **active ship**.
- **Playground** is the setting. **Sanctioned** and **unsanctioned** refer only to the rules of ranked play. An unsanctioned part fitted with Playground off gets no new name: not “forbidden”; the game's existing ranked-play wording stands. Runs are **ranked** or **unranked**.
- **Linked slots**, never mirrored, may number more than two and always hold the same part. Linked engines is a separate setting; both connections were to be drawn as lines on the ship.

A separate future axis is whether the player has a schematic: even a sanctioned part without one cannot be fitted for ranked play. Available and unavailable were working words for that axis; nothing in 0.6 built it. Whether the hatch would also cover parts flown in Playground without a schematic, or those would get another colour, remained open. Part names and modes were not otherwise reviewed.

<h3 id="band">02.6 / The band</h3>

The band worked on the existing row without moving its contents. Its faint border made the hatch barely visible at rest and clear under a pointer; the border needed amendment. The row copies reacted to a pointer but did not fit parts. The fitted and blocking states were not tested in that round.

<h3 id="content">02.7 / What a row must say</h3>

The row needs to identify a part and distinguish alternatives, its fitted and sanctioned states, its cost and what it gives. Fitting and undoing it are immediate, with flight estimates beside the list, so the idea that the row need not predict the whole ship's behaviour was a hypothesis. Existing rows were 188–274 pixels tall and showed only two or three alternatives; some descriptions repeated figures while others omitted the effect. The description's destination was initially open.

<h3 id="density">02.8 / Figures</h3>

The fullest comparison was selected: mass plus the key facts, up to four figures. Examples included thrust, damage per second, energy capacity, running energy cost, cruise ceiling and reach. Weapon damage is a range because it falls with distance. Example icons were placeholders.

<h3 id="room">02.9 / Space for content</h3>

A separate drawing column squeezed the figures and made them wrap; the drawing is a small distinguishing mark, not a content column. At 222 pixels the figures wrapped in every version; at 299 they stayed on one line only in the smallest version. Catalog descriptions were too long and sometimes repeated figures. Loading coil and Gravity coil had only mass as a data figure; their effects were sentences.

<h3 id="card">02.10 / Drawing and prose</h3>

Version 4 was selected: the corner drawing, name, two lines of prose and figures along the bottom. Giving the figures the full width kept them on one line at 299 and 400 pixels; at 222 pixels the three weapons with four figures still wrapped. Cards without drawings were shorter in this comparison.

<h3 id="prose">02.11 / Four columns</h3>

The figures sit in four columns, mass first, so corresponding figures align. At 299 pixels cards in the packed and four-column versions were 86 pixels high; adding a rule above the figures cost 9 pixels. At 222 pixels four columns overflowed. The proposed prose was draft, near 54 characters per part to fill two lines at 299 pixels; the game's tone of voice was undecided.

<h3 id="air">02.12 / Spacing</h3>

The selected spacing is 16 pixels horizontally and 8 vertically around the figures. At 299 and 400 pixels the three spacing comparisons were 90, 98 and 106 pixels tall; at 222 the columns did not fit.

<h3 id="size">02.13 / Figure size</h3>

The figures stay at 12 pixels. At 299 pixels the 14-pixel alternative fit but neighbouring figures nearly touched; it increased card height from 94 to 96 pixels. Neither size fit at 222 pixels.

<h3 id="box">02.14 / The slot as a model</h3>

The slot's outlined box was rejected: a slot is a target for choosing a place on the ship; a card is pressed to act, closer to a button. At 299 pixels the fitted word also forced names onto two lines and increased card height from 94 to 108 pixels.

<h3 id="button">02.15 / The card as a button</h3>

A list of raised buttons looked like an early-2000s web game. The cut corner and bracketed edge held up, but the button body did not.

<h3 id="edge">02.16 / Edge without body</h3>

The unfilled card on its pane was close. Cards were too close together, the fitted bottom bar looked like web design, the hatch was too narrow, and the blocking part needed more than red lines. [02.17](#close) corrected these.

<h3 id="close">02.17 / Corrections</h3>

The corrections were an 8-pixel gap, black fitted ground with lit brackets instead of a bar, a 10-pixel hatch, and a faint red ground for the blocking part. Using the example showed that the list needed its own emptying control, transitions should not snap, and unsanctioned parts must stay visible when Playground is off.

<h3 id="pane">02.1 / The pane</h3>

The first comparison kept the hatched band, which carried onto a fitted row. Individually cut stacked panes read as push buttons, unlike tabs sharing one line; black windows around drawings looked like early-2000s web games. The row needed its container. This comparison did not show the Hangar column, its width or touch.

<h3 id="display">02.2 / One display</h3>

A separate readout above a scrolling list was rejected: the list is part of the Hangar, where the large picture already appears on the ship. The row retains a small drawing, name and figures. A pointer-following readout also fails to offer comparison on touch before fitting.

<h3 id="anatomy">02.3 / Beside the ship</h3>

The first three rounds made general list controls that did not belong in the Hangar. The tool is bespoke, already sits beside the ship, and should be amended rather than reinvented; its nearest relative is the slot it serves. The Hangar slice was narrower than the real Hangar, omitted other panes and was not tried by touch.

<h3 id="tool">02.4 / The existing tool</h3>

The tool already named the selected mount, said whether it could be empty, and drew the same fitted part on the ship and in the row. Selection belonged to the slot, not the row. Neither ship nor row clearly showed class beyond a small caption; the launch row covered the bottom of the list. The live Hangar embedded in the original was not historical evidence and is not retained here.

<h2 id="readiness">03 / Limits and open questions</h2>

Pointer and fitted states both light the brackets; only the black ground distinguishes them. Keyboard and touch were not tried on the card, and whether an unavailable card can receive keyboard focus was undecided. The ship's corresponding state is a separate [study](/lab/studies/ship-slots-2026-11-11). Three parts had no drawing. The future schematic state needs its own appearance. Icons and prose were provisional, not approved final assets or copy.

<a id="stamp"></a>

The reconstruction above preserves the final default states only. The other live comparisons and their selected or rejected variants have no retained stills; their findings are recorded as text, not as visual proof. The original question review found issues that were corrected or declined, but the revised question was not retested; see the [review](/lab/reviews/part-rows-2026-11-11).

<h2 id="scope">04 / Where the work was to go</h2>

The study proposed changes to the design system, parts, Hangar and Playground specs: the band rule; computed key figures and data for the coils; reviewed two-line prose; a separate card component with states and Keep empty; sanctioned-first ordering; a fixed-width parts column; and eventual replacement of placeholder icons. At 1024 pixels, the estimated columns needed at least 60 pixels more than available, so the estimates column would need to narrow or fold away. Changes to screen terminology, ship-slot states, schematics and tone of voice were outside this scope. The study explicitly said delivery needed its own go-ahead.
