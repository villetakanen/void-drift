## 01 / A system with a place in the galaxy

A pilot coasting past a planet should feel that this small system belongs to a much larger galaxy. The flight sky currently draws white discs on a dark ground. Earlier studies found promising moments in a moving photograph of the Milky Way, but ended without choosing a whole sky. This cycle concerns the art direction of that sky: the relationship between its broad composition, fine texture and travel behind the local system.

The desired impression is a view from within the Milky Way. A proposed starting direction is a reach of distant starlight continuing beyond the frame, interrupted by uneven dark lanes and quieter open sky. Fine texture should give the light substance; darkness should give the local bodies and ship room. This is an artistic hypothesis to judge in a composed flight view. Its palette, orientation, scale, intensity and degree of abstraction remain open.

Panning belongs to the composition: a feature passing behind a planet can suggest a larger place and make a coast satisfying. Judge that movement with the artwork it carries. A convincing, distinctive picture takes priority over astronomical precision and polished rendering details. Keep the existing flight mechanics and readable flight cues; leave the base sky drawing open to change within the shared rendering method. The result would guide a proposed replacement for the flight background.

### What the earlier studies actually leave us

- [Flight starfield](/lab/studies/background-stars-2026-11-18#sky-in-the-scene) found that whole-field structure mattered. Its separately moving sky plane suggested depth, especially during widening. Neither the artwork nor its camera relationship was settled for play.
- [The distant flight sky](/lab/studies/distant-flight-sky-2026-11-18#photographic-sky) found the photographic Milky Way more convincing despite blur, pixelation and a rigid horizontal composition. A dark pocket or bright patch gave the eye something to follow. A preceding smooth edge read as “grey goo.” These are observations about those examples, not proof of a chosen drawing technique.
- [Eleven close stars](/lab/studies/close-g-stars-2026-11-18) ended with the individual lights unresolved. Its density decision belongs to that study. Eleven lights, a 6% stellar population, particular cores and gleams are not requirements here.

### The first artistic claim to test

**The local system sits in front of an immense, uneven field of starlight.** Let a broken luminous reach enter and leave the picture, with enough darkness around it for the system to feel suspended in space. The eye should have a feature to follow through a coast and be able to return to the ship at the next burn.

Begin with one deliberately composed whole sky behind the existing Practice system. A still establishes its composition and texture; the same scene in motion establishes how its features travel. Keep a production-sky comparison available. Use the retained photographic departure as an aesthetic reference, without inheriting its horizontal crop or pan setting. Texture construction and base stars serve this composition; their individual marks can remain rough while the whole picture is being judged.

This first claim leaves room to reject the band, the texture or their relationship to the system. A new example needs an explicit visual intention and a reason grounded in what the previous picture revealed. Tuning an isolated light cannot settle this study’s question.

### Boundaries

The subject is the complete distant flight sky. Panning, zoom response, texture and base stars are facets of that one view. Existing simulation, controls, local bodies and their effects stay in place. Star travel is an expressive camera relationship; it need not model physical parallax at galactic distances.

Use the shared layer and pass method, native Canvas drawing and prepared assets; no per-frame pixel processing. The game’s procedural artwork direction still applies. Whether this sky needs a different asset policy is a later project decision, not an assumption of this study. A photograph remains reference material. This cycle proposes no new galaxy navigation, system generation or change to the local star’s appearance.

Any adopted result would need project decisions about its scope and changes to the owning specs before production implementation. [Section 03 records the direction selected for the next flight sky.](#next-flight-sky)

<summary>02.1 / A reach of starlight</summary>

A broken diagonal sweep carries dense fine grain, a dark seam and quieter blue-black space. The local system passes in front of it. The base stars and the broader texture are prepared as one composition; the sky holds its apparent scale as the camera zooms.

**Retained finding · 19 November 2026.** The complex background form made the system feel situated in space and was a substantial improvement over the earlier skies. Its separate luminous banks did not yet give the desired single-stream impression. The overall brightness overpowered the scene, as dense grainy stars had in an earlier study. The absence of identifiable stars also felt wrong. These findings support continuing this direction; they do not settle the final stream, brightness or star drawing.

<details id="the-ivory-bank">
<summary>02.7 / The ivory bank</summary>

In Brunier’s panorama, the eye can follow a thin line of light a long way before it opens into the crowded, warm centre. During the earlier photographic departure, a single patch could hold the eye as the system moved across it. This sky borrows those two relationships. A shallow sweep crosses the Practice sky. Most of its length is a faint, cool fringe. At one end of the flight view, it widens into an ivory bank with a deep notch cut across its face. Fine, uneven grain follows the exposed light.

B1 opens on the coast at 2.50 s. Follow the notch past The Cradle, then look back to the Wasp as it burns. The open space below the bank gives the ship a dark ground. The pan carries the same landmark through the frame at a fixed apparent size. The pane switches to the production sky at the same instant.

Pan 65 stage px per 100 world u, stream 45%, identifiable stars on, fixed sky scale, seed 191126. The planet labels and orbit markings in the current Practice scene are stronger than in the retained 02.4 still.

*Resumed 19 November 2026 from the findings in 02.1–02.4. Steps 02.5–02.6 were set aside as conversational oversteering.*

**Retained finding · 19 November 2026.** The ivory bank gives the coast a distant landmark. As The Cradle passes it, the pilot has a place in the distance to follow. The distinct areas of colour and the chaotic, intriguing shapes in the blacks draw the eye into the scene and give the coast a sense of adventure. These are qualities to carry forward. The sky’s artistic expression remains open.

**For later exploration.** The curvature weakens the impression of looking along the Milky Way. The bright passages and highly visible stars feel less convincing than the dark forms. How to give those lights presence while keeping the ship and flight cues easy to read remains open, as does whether the texture feels smudgy.

Brightness should remain adjustable, with 45% as the working default. A slightly different view in each system could give each arrival its own sky while keeping the landmark familiar. A change of orientation is one possibility the Practice example has yet to test.

## 02.13 / A sky for each system

On arrival, what could a pilot recognise about a system from the sky alone? The proposal is to share the straight galactic reach, natural colour, quiet ground and rate of travel. Each system would keep a particular heading and a repeatable arrangement of dust and light. A return visit would bring the same landmarks back into view.

Three sightlines share the same Practice flight. **Practice** is the 02.12 bank crossing. **Narrows** tips the reach the other way and concentrates its light into a narrow shelf with deeper cuts. **Broad reach** spreads a dimmer light over a wider shoulder with gentler contrast. Each local form uses a fixed seed to place the bright crest and shape the dust. The headings, breadths and exposures are authored examples of how much variation the game might allow.

At the 2.50 s coast, **Turn only** and **Local form** used each sightline’s angle. Turn only rotated the original Practice artwork. Local form also changed the breadth, internal contrast and dust contours. Lowering the stream brightness to 30% left the landmarks in place. The stills below preserve the three local forms at 45%.

The opening is Narrows at 45%, B1 / Game, 1.25 s, pan 65, fixed apparent scale, stars on. The ship, local bodies and star treatment give the eye the same scale throughout. These are candidate views for different systems, staged on one departure so their differences are visible.

**Retained judgment · 19 November 2026.** Keep the spectrum from Narrows through Practice to Broad reach. All three local forms work as examples of systems within the same galaxy. Their position and rotation are open design choices.

## 02.14 / Placing the galactic plane

The bank can give a system a bearing as well as a landmark. This departure places the warm gathering high and to the right, with the thinner reach rising towards it from the lower left. The Wasp and The Cradle sit in the dark above the band. Across it, the Sun sits in open sky. The same Narrows artwork supplied both this view and the first still above.

At the 2.50 s coast, the position controls moved the bank relative to the ship, planet and Sun. Rotation turned the field about the centre of the bank. Pattern zoom scaled the dust and stars together around that point from 80% to 120%. The chosen scale held as the game camera widened.

## 03 / Ready for the next flight sky

**Design decision · 19 November 2026.** Use the 02.14 model for the next version of the distant flight sky. Its straight reach, turbulent dark lanes and pockets of warm and cool light give the pilot a landmark to follow on the coast. When the camera widens, the local system looks small against it. The Wasp’s silhouette and plume still carry the turn across the bright bank. That whole composition is already enough of an improvement to take forward. [Camera widening in 02.10](#when-the-camera-widens) · [Bank crossing in 02.12](#crossing-the-bright-bank)

**One galaxy, a recognisable view from each system.** Generate forms along the Narrows–Practice–Broad reach spectrum. A narrow reach concentrates the light and cuts deeper into it. A broader reach spreads a dimmer light across a gentler shoulder. Intermediate systems should carry those relationships through. Give each system repeatable dust contours, a heading drawn from the full 360° and a pattern scale from 80% to 120%. Stars and dust turn and scale together. Returning to the same system and camera position should bring the same sightline back.

Keep the 45% stream brightness and travel of 65 stage pixels per 100 world units as working defaults. The chosen pattern scale holds as the flight camera zooms. Stream brightness stays independently adjustable for art tuning. The 30% coast remains a useful reference. Visible stars carry forward as drawn. Individual star detail and contrast within the galactic light can improve in a later pass.

**What remains to establish in the game.** The three forms and manually placed banks were judged over one Practice departure. A range of generated arrivals has yet to be seen. Start with a repeatable offset that keeps some reach visible on departure. How far the warm bank can sit towards an edge, and how often a system falls near either end of the spectrum, are tuning choices for that first set of arrivals.

The game already has a fixed Practice system, a repeatable seed for generated systems and a background pass behind all local bodies. The camera spec currently gives background stars the world’s zoom and one pattern across runs. Adopting this proposal would require the **Camera** spec to describe the sky’s own travel and scale, **Seeded Solar System** to define which view recurs, and **Render** to place the prepared sky in stage coordinates at the existing World-layer background pass. The Lab shows that composition in flight. Preparation time and memory for a new system still need checking on the game path.

## 04 / A first delivery hypothesis

**1. Give Practice its reference sky · medium.** If the project adopts the flight-sky proposal, use the fixed middle sightline as the first playable slice. Start with the 02.12 bank crossing, 10° heading and 100% scale. Prepare the procedural artwork before drawing flight frames and use the shared background pass in play and in the Lab’s production flight view. Compare the same B1 coast at 2.50 s and powered turn at 3 s with 02.12. Check that the bank holds its apparent size through camera widening and covers the stage to every edge. This slice can stand on its own as a replacement for Practice’s placeholder.

**2. Give generated systems their own sightlines · small to medium, after Practice.** Use the existing system seed in Survival, Derelicts and Gauntlet to select a place along the spectrum, the exact dust pattern, offset, heading and scale. Derive these choices independently of gameplay randomness. Compare a few fixed seeds spanning the range at arrival and on the coast. Revisit one seed across modes to check that its landmark returns, then inspect both zoom extremes and a steep heading for exposed tile edges or a band that disappears from the opening view. Check the cost of preparing and releasing artwork as systems change.

The sizing assumes the shared background pass can reuse prepared artwork within the game’s frame and memory budgets. The main uncertainty is the cost of that first playable slice. It would also provide the flight reference for judging how much variation the generated systems can carry.
