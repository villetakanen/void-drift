## 01 / A Plasma Projector in the game’s weapon language

**What physical construction gives the Plasma Projector a convincing identity
within the game’s weapon language?**

The Plasma Projector is an elongated sci-fi gun for compatible equipment mounts,
not a Wasp-specific device. Its loaded close eruption, backward kick and sustained
venting supply its role; they do not require an engine-like body. It needs a
convincing inactive assembly whose working parts, supporting housing and attachment
have distinct jobs and constructed volume. Existing weapons supply that language,
not a mandatory parts recipe. Energy should have a physical place within the
working head; its exact construction remains open.

This study supplies one provisional physical reference in the Lab: a form with
dimensions, attachment, central opening and paired vent origins for the parked
effects inquiry. One weapon uses one compatible equipment mount, with both vents
belonging to it. Wasp, Fighter and Scout are current mount contexts for inspection,
not separate weapon designs or a claim of production plasma compatibility. Their
mount coordinates stay fixed. SVG drafts show the whole assembly and its visible
portion on current ships, enlarged and at game scale.

The retained action includes venting after a lost target, continuous backward
vent thrust and no weapon-induced rotation. These are study agreements, not
production mechanics. Outside FX/LFX, orange is limited to small bright luminous
features; world-layer surfaces must not change to orange to report loading.
Silhouette, proportions, mechanical arrangement and light remain open. Twin jets
and Open bell are unselected; final effects, balance and implementation remain
outside this physical-form question.

## 02.1 / Engine language does not construct a weapon

*17 November 2026 · Evidence for the amended question: engine-derived construction failed to establish a weapon. Its forms are not candidates.*

Three forms tried to establish the fitting by recomposing the plasma engine's
faceted gunmetal shell, bands and apertures: Crucible, Sleeve and Split jacket.
They could be mounted, drawn at scale and given explicit outlets. **That did not
make them a convincing weapon.** None is carried forward as the provisional form.
The original captions in these captures describe their hypotheses, not accepted
qualities.

The valuable finding is a gap in the design documentation: it describes hull
construction and preserves individual weapon artwork, but did not explain how
weapon forms are constructed. A material-family reference was made to do that
missing work. Recombining an engine's visual parts is not the path forward for
this weapon. These images remain because they make that distinction concrete.

## 02.2 / Build the weapon as an assembly

*17 November 2026 · Evidence for the amended question: existing weapon construction supplies shared principles; its individual parts and colours are not a universal recipe.*

The current cannon has a **dark barrel through a separate coloured housing**.
Two side blocks and a crossbar make that housing; a narrow active channel runs
to the muzzle. Point defence retains this construction with a shorter barrel
and a round base. Its pivot is the base centre. These are identifiable solids
with different jobs, proportions and materials, rather than a single faceted
shell decorated with bands.

## 02.3 / A pale shroud around a dark emitter

*17 November 2026 · Evidence for the amended question: distinct working parts established a credible weapon assembly, but not its plasma identity. Its Wasp-only mounting data is a historical fit record.*

**Retained finding · 17 November:** the assembly now reads as a weapon, supporting
the construction work in 02.2. Its plasma identity is still unresolved. This
example and its inspection data remain an intermediate result; no fitting has
been selected for the effects study.

17 November: this example sets aside 01's pressure-vessel hypothesis. The
question becomes how a receiver, emitter and power supply can form one heavy
plasma weapon; the role, single-slot mounting and required action remain fixed.

The next proposal takes the rail-shrouded tactical direction: an angular pale
receiver around a gunmetal emitter, slotted shoulder vents and one angled power
pack. The emitter has a recessed core between short guide edges, rather than a
conventional bored tube. Those edges frame one central strike; they do not trace
the old antler vectors. The two vents remain cuts in the shoulders of this one
fitting. Both can feed Twin jets or Open bell beyond their roots.

The supplied plasma-weapon tropes are creative references, not established game
mechanics. This example uses the shroud, slots, power pack and white/grey/amber
contrast. Heat staining, steam, moving coils and new thermal rules are outside
this form round. The core and slots illuminate only in the corresponding
inspection states; the idle object must carry its identity through construction.

### Light belongs to the openings

The opening cuts, anchor rings and static light marks share the same geometry
and mount transform. Dark, Loading and Vents show just enough light to inspect

eruption or vent form.

The left mounting is intentionally 7.8 u off the ship's centreline. The earlier
reported FX displacement remains unresolved: the effects study will need to
judge its light against these physical outlets once the form is useful. No
renderer correction is established by this construction comparison.

### What the effects study can take next

inspect accumulated light while moving, and judge whether the hardware remains
readable through the large eruption. No vent form has been selected here.

The open design questions are the assembly's character at flight scale, the
power pack's contribution to its silhouette, and the relationship between the
recessed core and the eventual eruption. Final FX, weapon naming, mass, power,
recovery, damage, force and production delivery remain unsettled. The parked
study stays blocked until the physical reference is taken up.

## 02.4 / Give the energy a physical presence

*17 November 2026 · Evidence for the amended question: the elongated gun intent, reference provenance and illustrative sketch remain useful. Earlier pressure-vessel and bulk recommendations are outside the amended basis.*

The fitting should be a **compact, elongated sci-fi gun with a clear forward
firing axis**. Shotgun proportions can shorten it while preserving a projecting
working head and distinct muzzle. Heavy commitment describes its action; it does
not require a bulky body. This character is now a constraint within the study.

Titanfall, Star Citizen and District 9 supply the intended reference family.
Their details organise an already directional gun. These images are construction
and character references; only the Kastak Arms example is identified here as a
plasma weapon. The observations and proposed transfers are interpretations.

### The next form starts with the firing axis

Begin with a compact receiver and a longer projecting emitter, carried within
an angular pale shroud. Keep the whole object visibly longer than it is wide.
The working head can be shorter than a rifle's while still pointing decisively
forward. Its relation to the receiver should carry the gun reading before light
is added.

Plasma identity can then come from a visible energy-bearing recess, a few slotted
openings and an integrated power supply along that axis. Their exact construction
is still open. This narrows the next drawing; it does not select a fitting or
require every cue to appear together. The handheld references' grips and stocks
do not transfer automatically to ship equipment.

The single fitting still needs one central strike and two secondary exhaust
outlets. The required loading, close eruption and rotation-free backward forces
remain unchanged. Neither vent effect is selected. Final FX, balance and the
reported emission offset remain outside this reference comparison.

### Retained finding · 17 November 2026

Putting the proposals on the ship made their mismatch with the intended game
more visible. The sheet is useful negative evidence: none of the three forms
is a direction to develop. Their relationship to the Wasp is unconvincing, and
the assembly and light do not explain the weapon clearly enough in context.

Material agreement is partial. The darks are closer; the pale equipment planes
still drift toward grey instead of the ship's blue family. The orange working
surface has not earned its place in the composition. Its connection to the
plasma-engine reference does not establish a successful equipment material.

Excessive size is a hypothesis, not the established cause. A reduction of about
20% might help some forms, but has not been compared. The draft lengths of
19–25 u are roughly 59–78% of the Wasp's 32.175 u axial hull span; size needs
to be judged alongside the placement and material relationships.

The ship reference is incomplete. Its hull facets and mounts are current, but
the drawing replaced the approved cockpit with flat black and omitted the
engine. Current Wasp glass has two green–space–gold panes, reflections and a
gold perimeter, provided by cockpit-material.ts. The sheet therefore does not
show the complete current ship appearance. Preserve it as the comparison that
revealed the mismatch; subsequent comparisons need the actual ship materials.
No further fitting direction is selected by these observations.

**A / Spine** gives most of the length to a narrow working head in a short
saddle. A recessed energy channel and two small forward relief tips project
beyond the receiver. Its draft envelope is 25 × 6.8 u. It tests whether a lean,
continuous firing axis can carry the plasma character.

**B / Split crown** uses a shorter, open working bed between two stout jaws,
carried by a transverse receiver. Its draft envelope is 19 × 12 u. The open
centre is one primary discharge opening; the short outboard lips are paired
secondary outlets. It tests a close-discharge weapon with a broader face.

**C / Offset breech** places a stepped barrel beside a longer side housing.
Its draft envelope is 22.2 × 9.5 u. A broad recessed working surface occupies
one side of the head, while the connecting bridge and paired relief tips keep
the assembly legible. It tests an asymmetric power-and-emitter relationship.

The rough lighting distinguishes a warm inactive working surface from a
contained loading cue; it does not demonstrate the eventual action. Dimensions,
palette and outlet positions are provisional. The single-slot assembly,
loading and close eruption, lost-target venting, continuous backward thrust and
rotation-free handling remain fixed. Neither a fitting nor a vent effect is
selected. The enlarged drawings expose construction; the small mounted row
shows which distinctions survive at game scale.
