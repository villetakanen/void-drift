## 01 / A Plasma Projector in the game’s weapon language

**What physical construction gives the Plasma Projector a convincing identity
within the game’s weapon language?**

The Plasma Projector is an elongated sci-fi gun for compatible equipment mounts.
It is viewed from above as fighter-jet- and tank-inspired space equipment;
handheld side-profile anatomy does not supply its construction. Its loaded close eruption, backward kick and sustained
venting supply its role; they do not require an engine-like body. It needs a
convincing inactive assembly whose working parts, supporting housing and attachment
have distinct jobs and constructed volume. Existing weapons supply that language,
not a mandatory parts recipe. The primary channel is exposed from above so hot plasma can be painted over
it on LFX. That spatial relationship is selected within the study; its
surrounding machinery, proportions and final effects remain open.

This study supplies one provisional physical reference in the Lab: a form with
dimensions, attachment, central opening and paired vent origins for the parked
effects inquiry. One weapon uses one compatible equipment mount, with both vents
belonging to it. Wasp, Fighter and Scout are current mount contexts for inspection,
not separate weapon designs or a claim of production plasma compatibility. Their
mount coordinates stay fixed. Planned SVG inspection shows the whole assembly and its visible portion on
current ships, enlarged and at game scale; earlier evidence used Wasp alone.

The retained action includes venting after a lost target, continuous backward
vent thrust and no weapon-induced rotation. These are study agreements, not
production mechanics. Outside FX/LFX, orange is limited to small bright luminous
features; world-layer surfaces must not change to orange to report loading.
Silhouette, proportions, mechanical arrangement and light remain open. Twin jets
and Open bell are unselected; final effects, balance and implementation remain
outside this physical-form question.

## 02.1 / Engine language does not construct a weapon

*17 November 2026 · Evidence for the amended question: engine-derived construction failed to establish a weapon. Its forms are not candidates.*

Three forms tried to establish the fitting by recomposing the plasma engine's
faceted gunmetal shell, bands and apertures: Crucible, Sleeve and Split jacket.

weapon forms are constructed. A material-family reference was made to do that

17 November: this example sets aside 01's pressure-vessel hypothesis. The
question becomes how a receiver, emitter and power supply can form one heavy
plasma weapon; the role, single-slot mounting and required action remain fixed.

The next proposal takes the rail-shrouded tactical direction: an angular pale

contrast. Heat staining, steam, moving coils and new thermal rules are outside

### Retained finding · 17 November 2026

Putting the proposals on the ship made their mismatch with the intended game
more visible. The sheet is useful negative evidence: none of the three forms
is a direction to develop. Their relationship to the Wasp is unconvincing, and
the assembly and light do not explain the weapon clearly enough in context.

Material agreement is partial. The darks are closer; the pale equipment planes
still drift toward grey instead of the ship's blue family. The orange working
surface has not earned its place in the composition. Its connection to the
plasma-engine reference does not establish a successful equipment material.

Excessive size is a hypothesis, not the established cause. A reduction of about
20% might help some forms, but has not been compared. The draft lengths of
19–25 u are roughly 59–78% of the Wasp's 32.175 u axial hull span; size needs
to be judged alongside the placement and material relationships.

The ship reference is incomplete. Its hull facets and mounts are current, but
the drawing replaced the approved cockpit with flat black and omitted the
engine. Current Wasp glass has two green–space–gold panes, reflections and a
gold perimeter, provided by cockpit-material.ts. The sheet therefore does not
show the complete current ship appearance. Preserve it as the comparison that
revealed the mismatch; subsequent comparisons need the actual ship materials.
No further fitting direction is selected by these observations.

## 02.24 / Recover the artistic context behind the parts

The intended machinery takes deliberate artistic liberties: simplify, omit and
exaggerate a few features so the image suggests more technical richness than it
literally draws. Appleseed, Ghost in the Shell and Gundam are the
references for that selective suggestion. No particular design or rendering
treatment from those works is selected.

The earlier studies contain concrete distinctions that a palette inventory
misses:

- [Wasp 02.5–02.6](/lab/studies/lhi-wasp#ab306-refinements) makes side and rear winglets read as a continuing surface through a shared bridge and parallel edges, alongside matching shade.
- [Fiddler 02.3–02.6](/lab/studies/lhi-fiddler-2026-11-03#surface-language-lesson) finds that copied outlines lose their meaning without the ridge, sloping section, bend and inner contour. Its explanatory height is illustrative, not a recovered ship measurement.
- [Coil 02.6–02.7](/lab/studies/gravity-coil-2026-11-03#solid-machines) explicitly permits convincing graphic connections without explaining a mechanically complete assembly. Raised blocks and offset extrusions had failed.
- [Coil form 02.3–02.5](/lab/studies/gravity-coil-form-2026-11-03#shoulder) distinguishes inward- and outward-facing slopes under the same light; its accepted lower ring remains inside the silhouette, visible through gaps.
- [Potential Well 02.6](/lab/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02#chambers) replaces artificially extruded rims with broad slopes and quiet underlying rails. Its fictional identity gives the openings a purpose without explaining the science.
- [Engine identities 02.9](/lab/studies/engine-identities#plasma-simplified) simplifies a chosen assembly, preserving its main proportions and a few technical cues while removing valves, straps and small fittings.

**Working diagnosis:** the missing context includes implied sections and surface
continuity, the direction of faces under light, the hierarchy of working and
supporting forms, and intentional graphic simplification. The previous exploded
assembly pursued mechanical completeness before those visual relationships
were understood. These are interpretations to review, not proven causes of
02.23’s failure or new rules for every part.

`docs/plans/2026-11-17-plasma-art-context.md`. It also distinguishes the failed
utility explorations from accepted forms and notes that the cannon and point
defence began with supplied visual solutions. Those studies do not establish a
generative recipe of dark machinery, pale side blocks and a cyan crossbar.
