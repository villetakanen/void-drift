**Exploration stopped · 30 October 2026.** The study produced an agreed Fighter design, but no usable Scout design and no accepted Heavy Corvette design. The three-ship question remained open.

## Question

How can part placement and engine angles give three ships recognizable silhouettes in the builder and in flight?

The study aimed for outlines distinguishable without labels or color, with color supporting identity. It considered both visual composition and the flight consequences of engine placement.

## Fighter direction

The designer selected the low-poly **Lance · clipped tips** hull: a pointed Fighter with flat facets, a black forward cockpit, large stacked hull slots, a central aft pair and rotated side-engine slots partly outside the chassis. Lance and Cutlass had been the promising forms; Trident and Dart were outside the intended direction.

![Agreed Fighter composition with compact mounts](/studies/ship-silhouettes/step-37.png)

The forward cockpit became one solid black shape. Soft canopy treatments were rejected because gradients and blurred glow did not fit the crisp colors and heavy accents. The crisp-accent remake was also rejected. A colored recess was closest to the classic space-game references but still felt smudgy and broken. Neon identity color remained unresolved. The [rejected soft canopy sheet](/lab/studies/archive/fighter-canopy-soft) is preserved separately.

## Scout and Corvette

The roadster and fast-boat Scout sheets were rejected as too complex and lacking character. A compact Wasp with forward side engines, one hull slot and one aft engine was the strongest arrangement so far, but its hexagonal window and tapered hull looked like a coffin. It used a trial hull length of 80% of the Fighter and provisional 32° side-engine angles. The later six-spacecraft comparison produced no selected design. Heavy Corvette identity also remained unresolved.

## Where the work went

The Fighter was implemented locally in the shared builder and custom flight, retaining saved builds. The initial 02.9 integration used 66.8° side-engine mounts, a 0.65 drawing scale and a conservative 31.2-unit circular collision envelope. Its lateral overestimate and flight feel needed review.

After flight review on 30 October, the designer found the engines too widely rotated and restored the original 26° angle; the side pair moved inward from ±18 to ±15 units. Fittings, exhaust and flight calculations shared the corrected angle. Following feedback that turning was still too slow, a 2.5× angular-response adjustment gave default fission turning of about 96.6°/s, clockwise with the left engine and counterclockwise with the right. Handling remained open for review. The [builder and test flight](/hangar) show the implemented Fighter.
