**Accepted · 30 October 2026.** The designer accepted the study and its
implementation plan. The values remained provisional. The same day the plan was
implemented in the shared game and the [flight Lab](/hangar): complete fading
plumes, output-based size, and the provisional 750 directional cutoff.

## Question

How can engine effects turn flying into a flashy graphic performance through shaped surfaces, glow, and fluid transformation?

The visual reference was the late-nineties and early-two-thousands vector art and
effects era: flashy, graphic and sharply composed. Here “vector” means surfaces,
glows, interpolation and effects, not lines pointing in different directions.
Engine activity should be readable at once and leave a playful trace that
fades.

## What was accepted

**Glowing surfaces left behind that vanish over time · 30 October 2026.** A
filled, curved plume with a bright core and soft glow. Impressions of it remain
in space, spread and fade over 1.5 seconds. The designer judged this a match for
the intended aesthetic. The straight-back engine orientation was incomplete, and
exact colour, timing and geometry were not settled.

![A turning ship with a bright glowing plume; a line of fading oval impressions trails behind it.](/studies/engine-effects/021-surfaces-both-engines.png)

*The 02.1 sketch at 2.5 s of its 6 s timeline, both engines firing, glow on.
Production balanced ship, engine physics and drawing; gravity, hazards and arena

running today’s code; a reconstruction.*

**The plume fades out after release · 30 October 2026.** Each engine emits complete,
softly bounded glowing surfaces. They overlap into the flame, drift, expand and
fade. Release stops new emission, and even the newest surface keeps its remaining
lifetime, so there is no flame object to switch off and no ribbon to cut. The
designer confirmed this as the intended plume lifecycle. Engines point 12° off
straight back. Trial values, not accepted tuning: each surface lives 0.65
seconds, keeps 80% of the ship’s velocity at emission and drifts backward at 45
units/s. There is no connected strip and no distance cutoff.

**Directional thrust cutoff · designer direction, 30 October 2026.** Engines add
no further thrust at roughly 750 units/s in the direction they push. Thrust
tapers linearly from full contribution at rest to zero at 750. The intent was
agreed; the value and the taper shape remained tuning proposals. Motion already
faster is kept, and braking and sideways thrust remain available. The cutoff is a
starting interpretation and does not cap total speed.

**Plume size follows delivered thrust.** “Power” here means the thrust an engine
delivers on a simulation step, not the ship’s stored energy or its speed. Plume
area is proportional to the engine’s fraction of full thrust, so length and width
each scale by its square root. Each surface keeps its emission size through its
fade. The study called this mapping exploratory. The implementation plan sized
each plume by the engine’s contribution.

The implementation plan asked for:

- the fading surfaces to replace the game’s separate flame and line trail
- plume size from each engine’s contribution, not from velocity changes caused by
  gravity
- the 750 rule in the shared engine calculation as a shared constant, with no new
  settings system
- surfaces aged by simulation time, frozen on pause, cleared on reset, and with
  no streaks across arena wraps
- the Lab and the game using the same effect code

## Ruled out

- **Separate stamped impressions.** The angled plumes were liked, but their
  residue neither followed a turn convincingly nor combined into one flame in
  straight flight.
- **A connected, tapering tail.** Both engines fed one central tail and a turning
  engine fed an angled tail. Residue moved backward at 70 units/s, faded over
  1.2 s, with a distance fade over 145 units. On release the residue showed a flat
  cutoff while the attached flame disappeared instantly. The ribbon construction
  was overcomplicated and did not give the intended arcade effect.

![Two coasting ships. Left: no flame or tail. Right: only a short curved ribbon remains behind the ship.](/studies/engine-effects/022-tail-released.png)

*Connected tail, about 0.35 s after release: the attached flames are gone and
the residue ends in a flat cut. Captured 17 November 2026 at 1440px; a
reconstruction.*

## What remained uncertain

- The exact reference within the vector era had no shared visual basis.
- Cutoff, taper, plume size, angle and lifetime could change in use.
- Whether a shrinking plume makes an engine’s declining contribution clear, and
  whether its growth through a turn feels right.
- Readability at normal scale and among planets, full-scene feel, and
  performance on a phone.

## Where the work went

Commit `97190680` shipped the fading plumes and the thrust cutoff in release
0.3.5. The cutoff is `ENGINE_CUTOFF` in
`packages/core/src/lib/effects/engine-plume.ts`. The effects spec,
`docs/specs/factory/effects/spec.md`, and the engine taper section of
`docs/specs/game/world/gravity-field/spec.md` state the intent.

The full study before conversion is in commit `6c2949f8`, at `apps/web/src/pages/lab/studies/engine-effects.astro`, with its components `apps/web/src/components/studies/engines/EngineEffects.svelte`, `PlumeLifecycle.svelte`, `TailComparison.svelte`, and `ThrustPlume.svelte`.
