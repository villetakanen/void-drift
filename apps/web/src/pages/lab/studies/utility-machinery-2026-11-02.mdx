**Accepted · 2 November 2026.** Utility machinery says **“I create opportunities.”** The family is recognizable by a vivid **#CEDB17** accent on connectors, struts and attachments, not by a shared silhouette. The study accepted four resting drawings; deployment, working effects and gameplay rules were outside that decision.

<h2 id="reference">01 / Production reference</h2>

The parts should look like manufactured objects alongside the ships and engines: solid masses, clear changes of plane and smaller working parts visibly seated in supports. At flight scale, the silhouette and largest masses have to do the work.

![Eight production references: Wasp, Scout, Fighter and Corvette above Light ion, Heavy ion, Fusion and Plasma, each enlarged and at flight scale.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/01-production-reference.png)

*Original 2 November artwork: nose-up ships with default loadouts at 2.5×; engines in the aft lighting pose at 8×; small views at 0.65×.*

<h2 id="exploration">02 / From construction to character</h2>

**02.1 / Cassette or cradle.** A protected paired storage cells with deep shoulders; B held the same cells in end blocks and clamps, leaving dark gaps around the recessed spine. Both used a 22 × 28-unit footprint, identical lighting and the same palette. Neither was selected: the difference explained assembly, not why the family mattered to the pilot.

![Wasp and Light ion references beside an armoured cassette and an exposed cell cradle, enlarged and at flight scale.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/02-1-construction.png)

<h3 id="family-promise">02.2 / What does the machinery promise?</h3>

Three sketches held the core and lighting fixed while changing its stance: low enclosing shoulders suggested dependable reserve, outward-reaching ends suggested gathering, and raised reinforcing masses suggested added capability. The postures were metaphors, not equipment proposals.

![Three provisional postures: enclosing to sustain, reaching to gather, and reinforcing to empower.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/02-2-postures.png)

<h3 id="wartime-roles">02.3 / Keep going and make it count</h3>

Endurance and resourcefulness were more specific than broad empowerment, but neither alone fit the family: not every part harvests something, and staying operational is a means to act, not the whole point.

<p id="family-decision"><strong>Decision: “I create opportunities.”</strong> Protection can make a risk possible, repair can give another chance, charging can make an action affordable, and storage can carry that option forward. These examples express the promise, not additional mechanics.</p>

<h3 id="family-identity">02.4 / Put the accent on a physical part</h3>

The open yoke and transverse power bridge each put amber on a substantial structural part across the same four bodies. The greyscale checks separated shape from hue. Amber was an effective example, but Plasma already used orange; hue alone could not establish a distinct identity. The repeated accent survived as a principle. Neither structure nor amber was selected.

![Open yokes on storage, solar, gravity and repair, with small colour and greyscale comparisons.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/02-4-structure-1.png)

![Transverse power bridges on the same four bodies, with small colour and greyscale comparisons.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/02-4-structure-2.png)

*Both plates use 6× enlargement and 0.65× small views. The mechanisms are experimental fixtures.*

<h3 id="working-character">02.5 / Let each member work differently</h3>

Storage quietly held reserve; solar tried hinged collectors, gravity a field, and repair four service drones. Different working behaviours could belong to one family if colour marked what held, joined and attached the parts. The animated trial informed the discussion but its mechanisms, timings and effects were not accepted for delivery.

<h3 id="family-colour">02.6 / Accepted resting parts and colour</h3>

The four resting bodies and their simple shading were accepted, with RGB **206, 219, 23** on the existing attachment geometry. Keep the accent on physical structure and let each device keep its own mass and outline.

![Two Light ion Wasps fitted with the accepted resting parts: storage and solar on Wasp A, gravity and repair on Wasp B. Both are also shown at flight scale.](/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02/02-6-accepted-fittings.png)

*Colour fixture: 4× and 0.65× ship views, parts at 35% of the earlier drawing units and placed above the wings at the production mount centres. Neither size nor layering in this fixture establishes a production mounting rule.*

<h2 id="readiness">03 / What still needed checking</h2>

The static faces could be separated from gameplay. Mount-size readability, clipping and layering on other hulls, and separation from nearby bright colours still needed inspection. Battery could use its existing equipment contract; drawings alone did not establish new device IDs, loadout entries, charging, repair or deployment rules.

<h2 id="scope">04 / Resting artwork first</h2>

The study scoped reusable resting faces and shared accent shading, not working-state animation or FX. Later device studies could refine their shapes and behaviour independently. The [Utility artwork Lab](/lab/utility-artwork) shows the current implementation rather than this frozen reference.
