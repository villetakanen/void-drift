## Stopped · 15 November 2026

The study stopped while looping without resolving its central question. No planet appearance, atmospheric mechanic or implementation direction was accepted. The cause of the loop was not established. Detailed planets did not belong to the game, subsequent drawings read as vector icons, and the encounter example misused the Motion instrument. Repeatedly converting corrections into new directions without a grounded account of the mismatch is an interpretation, not an established cause.

On 16 November, the [rock-atmosphere study](/lab/studies/rock-atmospheres-2026-11-15) selected Impact with Cirrus and atmospheric heat as placeholder Earth for Practice. That does not accept these Earth or habitation designs.

<a id="01"></a>

## 01 / Life below, danger on approach

The question was how to suggest a vast inhabited world and atmosphere at flight scale without losing flight clarity, beside the Sun, Mercury, Venus and a small ship. Recognizable Earth was the first prototype for a language that could also accommodate fictional geography. Gravity and contact were fixed; atmospheric heating was a provisional possibility, not an accepted mechanic.

<a id="02"></a>

## 02 / Findings

<a id="02-1"></a>

### 02.1 / Three worlds in the same flight scene

<a id="02-1-findings"></a>

Familiar continents likely carry Earth recognition, but exposed geography also left the world feeling uninhabited. Weather made it feel more alive and plausibly inhabited, without establishing technological civilization. In the settlement example small lights vanished at flight scale; if lights are the cue, visible habitation needs deliberate exaggeration. Its amount, distribution and drawing language remain open. These observations concern the static scenes, not all Earth-like worlds or motion.

The generated concepts were separately authored, not a single-variable test; geography, shadow and colour differ. Earth had baked left illumination; other bodies used production lighting. C's long coastal lights risk an outline-like reading. Rotation, global geography, generation reliability, flight feel and a production renderer remain unproven. <a id="02-2"></a>

### 02.2 / Belonging

Detailed Earth images read as rendered planet images rather than objects belonging to Void Drift. Enlarged settlement lights exposed that mismatch. The light proposals were withdrawn; neither fewer details, flat shapes nor a particular rendering technique was established as a solution.

<a id="02-3"></a>

### 02.3 / Cloud depth

Cloud coverage appearing above ground was promising, but both drawings otherwise read as vector-style icons. Neither drawing was accepted.

<a id="02-4"></a>

### 02.4 / Atmospheric encounter

The encounter example was rejected because it did not follow the Motion instrument's specification, intended use or design system. It established no finding about atmospheric boundaries or heating cues. The encounter and broader visual-language questions remain unanswered. Technical feasibility checks did not establish visual belonging.
