Historical record · accepted 16 November 2026. This page preserves the selected
appearance, interaction and pending adoption scope. The live apparatus was retired
on 25 November after capturing approach, contact and fading exit. New images are
explicitly dated reconstructions; they do not replace the earlier evidence or add
new conclusions. Statements about implementation below describe the closure on
16 November, not a fresh assessment of today’s Practice.

## Accepted / Placeholder Earth for Practice

Impact with Cirrus was selected as placeholder Earth in Practice. This accepted
an atmosphere and ship-entry treatment, not Earth geography or habitation. The
selected reference used Bright face at **91%** lit-cloud brightness, the original
02.11 cloud shadow at **42%**, shared sunlight, **1.45×** rock shadow and the fixed
blue Dayward rim with narrow peaks **20° toward the Sun from the tangents**.
Opaque clouds retain openings. The comparison conditions were 02.5 footprint,
Separate air and the existing atmosphere extent.

For entry, soft planet-facing LFX glow and stronger flame on that side were
accepted. Heat FX uses the **curved band at 180% brightness and 150% width**.
Accepted production intent was hull-overlap entry, **1 hull/s in the outermost
30%**, **2 hull/s deeper**, and modest drag opposite current travel without
steering. The fixture’s 0.8/s drag was not selected as a production value.
No accumulated-temperature or delayed-damage model was required.

<h2 id="01">01 / Air the pilot can read</h2>

A close pass should reveal air before entry without losing the rock’s mineral
regions, terrain and turning limb. A small, understandable flight consequence
must coexist with a convincing world. The fixed quality references were
[Mercury 02.19’s](/lab/studies/mercury-2026-11-12#02-19) Mineral mosaic, Ancient
highlands and Impact palimpsest, with spherical mapping and independent 31.3-second
turns. The fitted Wasp and production gravity, controls and solid contact supplied
context. Venus’s approximately 40.33u radius, orbit and gravity were comparison
conditions, not an Earth-like identity.

The opening hypothesis was 10u of air with drag, not a required damage rule.
Production Venus then had a visual atmosphere without atmospheric drag or damage.
Surface redesign, geography, habitation and a planet taxonomy were outside scope.
The 15 November [question review](/lab/reviews/rock-atmospheres-2026-11-15) passed
One subject and Consequence, and Grounded with findings. The corrected framing
separated Lab exemplars from adoption and proposed mechanics from existing Venus;
it was not reviewed again.

<h2 id="02">02 / Skim the air</h2>

<h3 id="02-1">02.1 / A visible envelope and small braking consequence</h3>

On 15 November the Flight instrument was accepted as a useful method: real ship,
standard game context, fixed burns and an inspectable timeline. Pale mineral-blue
air and drag were usable starting points; appearance, depth and strength remained
provisional. The original fixture staged one planet at orbital angle zero with the
production Sun, omitted other planets, and started Wasp 82u left and 100u above it,
heading down at 230u/s plus the planet’s orbital velocity.

Hull collision-circle overlap triggered entry; density rose linearly toward the
surface. Each 1/60-second step multiplied planet-relative velocity by
exp(−0.8 × density × dt). No winds, atmospheric co-rotation or damage were simulated;
solid contact retained production bounce and hull cost. B1/B2 entered near 0.37s
and exited by 0.55s; B3 exited by 0.58s. All three completed 14 seconds without
solid contact. These are fixture measurements, not interaction-quality judgments.
Historical Follow used 0.8 render px/u, Follow 4× used 4, before display scaling.

<h3 id="02-2">02.2 / Shadow beneath the air</h3>

Shadow up to 1.45× worked without making bare rock feel plastic and also worked
under the initial air. It became the next baseline, not a universal planet-lighting
rule. The old combined light mask also shaded face air, leaving its lighting
separation unresolved.

<h3 id="02-3">02.3 / Haze, veil and layered air</h3>

Limb haze, Thin veil and Layered air made a useful comparison, but entry was hard
to identify without a visible boundary. Cloudless gas felt appealing yet too thin
for the needed presence. A thin upper layer and cloud alternatives were directions
to examine, not selected results. A possible 1.75× shadow was discussed; 1.45× remained
agreed.

<h3 id="02-4">02.4 / A visible extent and cloud substance</h3>

Upper layer, Cloud banks and Cloud bands were compared against Limb haze. A visible
edge was cleaner and became the direction, but its thin line remained naive. Air
also needed to read over the face: an envelope only outside the rock felt detached.
Neither procedural cloud pattern was selected. The cloud overlay sat at 4.2u with
a provisional 38-second turn, while depth, drag and rock rotation stayed fixed.

<h3 id="02-5">02.5 / Light the air separately</h3>

02.4 light shaded terrain and air together. Separate air restricted the rock mask
to rock; Face scatter also added projected sunlit gas across the face. Light cloud
banks improved at game scale but still merged with terrain. No option was accepted
in this round; Separate air became a comparison condition. This isolated lighting
overlap, not material adequacy. It did not settle a production lighting architecture,
physical scattering or nearby engine/weapon illumination.

<h3 id="02-6">02.6 / Preserve rock through openings</h3>

The existing authored rock quality stayed fixed. Preserving its identity meant
openings between clouds, not transparency everywhere: opaque cores could conceal
terrain completely. Coverage and opacity are independent. A small composed texture
library was a possible means, not a chosen pipeline or size. Shared sunlight had
to make rock, clouds and gas coherent without treating render layers as physical
materials. Cloud-cast shadows, nearby illumination and edge refinement remained open.

<h3 id="02-7">02.7 / Opaque clouds with room for rock</h3>

**Accepted quality, 16 November:** opaque masses with openings were a major
improvement. Clouds read above the rock while remaining attached to its rotation;
exposed terrain preserved identity. 02.5 / Opaque and Sparse / Opaque under Separate
air were strongest; Broad opaque also improved on translucent cover. Thin capped
opacity at 62%; Opaque reached 100%. Sparse/Broad were relative footprints, not
measured globe percentages. Texture, colour and the naive edge stayed provisional;
this accepted arrangement and concealment, not rollout.

<h3 id="02-8">02.8 / Connect the edge to the body</h3>

Current line, Soft layer and Connected air kept the same 10u interaction extent.
Increasing visible gas volume made a bubble or solid ball. That finding redirected
the edge toward luminous contrast while retaining opaque clouds and readable entry.

<h3 id="02-9">02.9 / Reveal air through light</h3>

Sunlit rim improved substantially on gas-volume options. Tangent, Nightward and
Broad arcs tested angular placement; Nightward was the best built comparison.
The selected modification was **Dayward**, narrow paired peaks 20° sunward of the
tangents (70° from the Sun axis), with 18° falloff, fixed blue and retained core/halo.
This was an illustrative light treatment, not an ionization claim or universal
atmosphere colour. The core sat 0.7u inside the interaction extent and its optical
feather reached up to 3u outside: a display cue, not a collision outline.

<h3 id="02-10">02.10 / Fixed blue beside different stars</h3>

A 2300–12000 K star-colour comparison used interpolated stellar-colour references,
not a spectrum, luminosity model or spectral reclassification. Fixed blue was
selected over Star tint. The comparison held rock and cloud lighting fixed, so it
did not establish their coloured-starlight response. Rim placement, colour and
brightness stayed settled for the next round.

<h3 id="02-11">02.11 / Shared sunlight</h3>

Shared sunlight on rock and cloud coverage was accepted on 16 November. The LFX
highlight worked with existing shadows and fixed blue Dayward rim. Low gas had no
new light treatment; this did not test physical scattering or cloud-cast shadows.

<h3 id="02-12">02.12 / Cloud night brightness</h3>

The 42% floor, Readable night at 18% and Deep night at 4% isolated shaded RGB
without changing opacity. 18% was closest but too dark; **24%** was selected from
a narrow useful range near 23–25%. Clouds still looked smudgy, prompting material
exploration rather than more light tuning. This choice was later superseded for
the white material in 02.14.

<h3 id="02-13">02.13 / Generated cloud materials</h3>

Cumulus, Fronts, Cellular, Cirrus and Mixed weather explored cloud structure;
additional 20/40/60/80% maps varied covered area. These were generated Earth-inspired
interpretations, not observations or exact classifications. Coverage targets used
source-map area above half opacity, not the visible hemisphere or spherical-area
weighting. Generated maps projected at 384 × 384 versus the procedural reference’s
128 × 128, so the comparison was not resolution alone. Seam joins and polar
distortion remained inspection items, with no production pipeline or budget selected.
[Material prompts](/studies/rock-atmospheres-2026-11-15/cloud-materials/prompts.json)
and [coverage prompts](/studies/rock-atmospheres-2026-11-15/cloud-materials/coverage-prompts.json)
remain available with their source maps.

The comparison was useful but still showed gray, goo-like face clouds. The lit
material needed to remain nearly white, with sunlight making it brighter. Rock
and cloud rotation worked; differential rotation-speed exploration was explicitly
excluded.

<h3 id="02-14">02.14 / White material and spherical shading</h3>

Bright face removed double darkening of camera-facing cloud material while keeping
coverage and dark-side RGB. A missing camera-facing light component was corrected
so the shadow curved around the sphere instead of forming a straight band; earlier
captures retain the pre-fix geometry. Full-brightness white felt too bright and
chunky. With corrected white clouds, the original **42%** night setting was preferred:
24% had compensated for earlier double darkening. Cloud colour remained white;
lit-face brightness became the next variable.

<h3 id="02-15">02.15 / Accepted cloud prominence</h3>

**Accepted, 16 November:** Cirrus at Bright face 91%, night 42%, shared sunlight,
rock shadow 1.45× and Dayward blue. The 20% and 40% maps and Cumulus also worked
across Mineral, Highlands and Impact. Mixed weather was ruled out as an offered
material because one recognizable cloud form was more readable. No wider taxonomy
was settled. The retained 02.5 / Opaque, Separate air and rotation conditions stayed fixed.

<h3 id="02-16">02.16 / Make atmospheric entry feel hot</h3>

Entry became a shallow heat hazard with secondary drag. None, Airflow and Heat
envelope compared the warning before damage or temperature modeling. Soft live
LFX glow on the planet-facing side and stronger flame on that side were accepted.
The tapered crescent was only an earlier reference. Borrowed luminous plasma blobs
were rejected because they looked like plasma. Current envelope, Soft patch,
Tapered streak and Curved band isolated the source mark from its accumulated FX.
Their different areas meant equal stamping rates did not imply equal emitted light.
The **curved band, 180% brightness and 150% width**, was accepted.

Fresh light followed planet-relative velocity, overlap and speed. Shared FX owned
history and fade after exit, with no independently aged trail marks. This was a
visual excitation proxy, not temperature, dose or hull damage. The historical
Canvas starfield remained behind the comparison.

| Outside | No overlap | Baseline without heat |
| Skim | Entry 0.38–0.40s; exit 0.52–0.55s; peak density 0.22–0.33 | Brief shallow warning |
| Deeper | Entry 0.35s; exit 0.57–0.62s; peak density 0.70–0.81 | Stronger escaping exposure |

All 840 ticks of every burn were checked without solid contact; hull stayed 100
because atmospheric damage was absent. Depth stayed 10u and drag 0.8/s. B1/B2
engines fired during entry: this was grazing entry, not a sustained engine-off coast.
Whether the cue sufficiently communicates “I am burning; get out” for a later
heat-damage experiment remained unresolved despite the accepted visual settings.

<h2 id="03">03 / Implementation assessment at closure</h2>

On 16 November the appearance and interaction were accepted but not adopted in
Practice. An integration attempt had changed shared rock lighting and production
passes, caused regressions and been removed; it was not retained as a baseline.
Future adoption needed comparison with established rendering and other active work.
Retained media now provide the historical reference independently of the retired
instrument.

Production intent used the same ship-hull overlap for visible entry, damage, drag
and heat activation, with the accepted hull rates integrated over simulation time
and normal hull lifecycle. Exact classification at the 30% boundary was not a design
requirement. Study acceptance did not itself modify the owning production specs.

<h2 id="04">04 / Pending adoption scope recorded at closure</h2>

No design-principle change was proposed. The following scope remained pending:

1. **Appearance and Practice body:** promote the accepted composition through
   established renderer owners, define new data with Zod first, and add Impact as
   placeholder Earth to Practice and its canonical preview while preserving existing
   bodies and loading fallback.
2. **Atmospheric gameplay:** adopt hull-overlap damage and modest direction-preserving
   drag, retaining gravity, solid contact and death. Verify outside, shallow, boundary,
   deep, exit, reset, pause, fatal damage and equal simulated time.
3. **Heat integration:** use the accepted curved band and soft LFX with actual overlap
   and planet-relative motion through established FX ownership. Preserve shared history
   and verify entry, fading exit, replay/wrap, production scale and keyboard/touch play.

Earth geography, habitation, new cloud materials, differential rotation, temperature
accumulation and wider planet taxonomy remained outside scope. Earlier Earth
studies stayed stopped; this placeholder did not retroactively accept their designs.
Further Earth exploration was deferred.
