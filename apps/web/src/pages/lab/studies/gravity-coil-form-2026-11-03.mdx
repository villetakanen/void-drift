<section>

**Accepted · 3 November 2026. M / Inset ring at 100%.** Keep E’s creased sector shells and add an engine-coloured ring underneath, visible through the gaps but not around the outside. Acceptance covers the static form and its size; the separate selectable fitting was explicitly added to the delivery.

</section>

<section>

<h2 id="problem">01 / A whole component, belonging to the ship</h2>

A coil that looked convincing over space and under a wing could still look like a floating icon on the chassis. The segmented toroid, open centre and yellow connections were already substantially decided. Removing detail did not settle the fit, and neither did the promising C and D treatments. The cause of the icon reading was not established.

The aim was sleek, flat-faceted machinery in the light ship or dark engine colour families, with safety-yellow supports and connections under upper-left starlight. Game references gave context, not a finished colour target. The form comparisons held size and placement fixed; fleet sizing kept the selected form and varied size at real mounts. Backing plates, pedestals, isometric views and literal realism were excluded, as were FX, animation, mechanics, balance and production implementation from the form inquiry. The [stopped coil study](/lab/studies/gravity-coil-2026-11-03) remains separate.

</section>

## 02 / Surfaces that make an assembly

<h3 id="references">02.1 / Chosen references in game context</h3>

[1A / Inner ribbon](/lab/studies/gravity-coil-2026-11-03#depth) supplies the connected shape. [C / One light and D / Housed winding](/lab/studies/gravity-coil-2026-11-03#light) suggest two ways to give it volume: adjoining faces can show an outward slope and an aperture wall; dark working material can sit within a light support. Potential Well shows separated working bodies joined by yellow links. Light ion shows a dark body with broad facets and a defined end.

<h3 id="construction">02.2 / Two constructions inside the chosen shape — E retained</h3>

Eight sectors, the outer contour, the open centre and the yellow inner connection were held fixed. C was the unchanged comparison. E used creased light shells; F used dark working faces held by light saddles.

**E / Creased sector shells.** Broad light faces slope away from a crest, one outward and one into the aperture. A yellow inner ribbon joins them. **F / Dark windings in light saddles.** The saddles hold the dark bodies at their ends and inner shoulders, making working material distinct from support without changing the silhouette. The saddles risked reading as borders or losing their construction at 1×. F was not selected. Dark tones might have been under-used in E, but more dark could also make the coil look like an icon.

<h3 id="shoulder">02.3 / A broad face and a narrow shoulder</h3>

**G / Narrow shoulder** moves the crest outward, widening the face toward the aperture compared with [E’s nearly equal faces](#creased-shells). The contour, yellow connections, dark inner return and light assignments stay fixed. G’s largest bright faces sit on the lower right, opposite the hull’s broad highlight, because the broad face points inward. This is consistent with the proposed peaked section; it does not show the light is wrong.

<h3 id="dark-faces">02.4 / Dark bodies or dark returns?</h3>

**H / Dark sector bodies** uses engine-coloured bodies with narrow lit shoulders and yellow connections rather than F’s surrounding saddles. The body risks becoming too quiet against space. **I / Shallow crowns with steep returns** uses a broad light upper face like the hull’s, darker steep walls, and a sloped end meeting the inner connection. It risks looking flatter and less distinctive. Neither adds a continuous dark frame, a backing surface or a shadow painted onto the hull. Reshaping was not carried forward, including the shallow crown. E stayed unchanged for the under-ring comparison.

<h3 id="under-ring">02.5 / E on an engine-coloured ring</h3>

E was unchanged in all five columns. J, K and L tested narrow, medium and wide exposed rims. The open lower ring used Light ion charcoal with a shallow upper-left-lit edge. It tested whether a continuous body would seat the coil on the hull, without adding a filled backing plate. A broad dark border could instead reinforce the icon reading.

<h4 id="decision">Selected: M / Inset ring</h4>

M puts the continuous ring inside the silhouette, visible only through the sector gaps. It adds continuity beneath the shells without an exposed outer rim. The creased sectors, open aperture and yellow connections stay unchanged. This selection does not establish why earlier versions read as icons.

<h3 id="sizing">02.6 / M across the fleet — 100% selected</h3>

**100% is the final selected size** and matches the ring comparison. All engine-compatible slots, including universal aft slots, carry Light ions; every remaining equipment slot holds M at the position used by the retained sizing fixture. In Wasp, Scout, Fighter and Corvette order the fixtures carry 1/3/4/5 engines and 2/1/2/3 coils. The sizing fixture’s normal layering puts the Wasp and Scout fittings below the hull and the Fighter and Corvette fittings above; the above-hull comparison reveals concealed parts without moving them.

<h2 id="readiness">03 / Ready at the selected size</h2>

The shared filled-face renderer supports M without a new dependency or FX system. The artwork scale is **0.5** inside the ship’s scene scale, including at **0.65** flight scale. The conversion preserves all 86 faces, colours and paint order, with the ring beneath the shells and the aperture open. Nose-up coordinates become local +x forward, and RGB strings become equivalent hex colours for the shared face schema.

The separately authorized **Gravity coil** fitting uses the existing catalog, loadout persistence and builder/flight renderer. At this handoff its provisional mass was **10**, matching Loading coil; it added no collection, capacity, thrust or energy draw. Loading coil retained solar charging. Static-form acceptance did not settle future collection behaviour or effects.

<section>

<h2 id="scope">04 / Accepted delivery</h2>

No change to `docs/design.md` was proposed. The [ship-builder record](https://github.com/villetakanen/void-drift/blob/b6e638d3/docs/history/ship-builder.md#gravity-coil--implemented-2026-11-03) records the handoff and distinguishes the subsequent FX work.

1. **Shared static artwork.** Replace `gravity` artwork with M at default scale 0.5. Use the shared face schema and painter in Canvas and SVG without changing other utility sizes. Compare every face and colour with the independent M reference.
2. **Separate selectable fitting.** Add `gravity-coil` to compatible equipment and universal slots with a catalog preview and shared builder/flight artwork. Preserve saved builds and starter fleets. Describe collection as inactive and verify the fitting adds only its provisional mass.
3. **Mounted fidelity.** Compare real loadouts on all four hulls at 3×, true 1× and 0.65× with the 100% study reference. Fit every equipment mount and fill engine-compatible slots with Light ions. Check selection, persistence, test flight and return in the actual builder.

The study recorded local implementation in [shared artwork and fleet views](/lab/utility-artwork#gravity-coil) and the [ship builder](/hangar), not publication. Collection, balancing, FX and animation were outside that delivery. The [study-quality review](/lab/reviews/gravity-coil-form-2026-11-03) preserves the original judgments and verification limits: scale and rendering checks do not establish aesthetic quality, and the historical checks were not a full release or backend pass.
