**Stopped · 2 November 2026.** No utility design was accepted. The designer identified **02.2** as the last useful visual step: establish a substantial object first, then decide what machinery to expose. The later family comparison did not advance the inquiry.

## 01 / Machinery with a visible job

Could a shared frame hold four recognizable working parts—storage, solar charging, gravity charging and repair—without competing with the ship? At flight scale a seam or indicator disappears, so the working mass and the spaces between parts had to carry the silhouette. Enlarged, the same parts needed to read as assembled solids. The Wasp and ion engine set the reference: top-down construction, flat facets, light from above-left and dark recesses. Mechanics and animation were outside the study.

![Production reference: the Wasp hull and light ion engine establish the faceted construction the utility sketches are compared against.](/studies/open-frame-machinery-2026-11-02/02-1-production-reference.png)

## 02.1 / One frame, four working parts

Rails and clamps held a different mechanism for each role. Review found that the results read as flat vector icons beside the Wasp and ion engine. An exposed component can explain what the object does, but it cannot give that object depth by itself. The requirement that survived was solid machinery seen from above, with planes that follow its edges, height and joins.

![Four flat open-frame sketches: storage, solar charging, gravity charging and repair, enlarged and at 0.65×.](/studies/open-frame-machinery-2026-11-02/02-1-flat-family.png)

*Provisional top-down drawings, not mounted equipment. The gravity rotor was a visual hypothesis, not a proposed game mechanic.*

## 02.2 / Build a solid object first

Storage gave the round one job to draw. Twin pressure vessels, a clamped plate stack and a split armoured accumulator shared a low cradle, cool metal and above-left lighting. Broad faces made the raised volumes legible; dark gaps separated the parts. The designer found these more convincing as objects in the game world. That was a finding about **construction**, not approval of a particular shape, material or housing format.

![Three solid storage sketches, each with an enlarged drawing and a 0.65× sample.](/studies/open-frame-machinery-2026-11-02/02-2-storage-volumes.png)

*Top-down projections, not tilted views. They compare mass and depth, not capacity or hull fit.*

## 02.3 / Four jobs, one solid construction

The last comparison carried the construction into four distinct silhouettes: paired storage cells, solar wings around a converter, gravity weights around an axle and repair jaws around a service core. Exposed parts followed each machine’s proposed job rather than a repeated decorative opening. None was selected; the designer stopped the cycle after the later exploration began to repeat itself.

![Four provisional solid machinery silhouettes with enlarged and 0.65× views.](/studies/open-frame-machinery-2026-11-02/02-3-solid-family.png)

## What carried forward

The closure proposed restarting with the four production ships and engines as common reference, with engines shown exhaust-down. The separate [Utility machinery study](/lab/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02#reference) preserves that reference panel and its own accepted direction. It does not validate the mechanisms or silhouettes above.
