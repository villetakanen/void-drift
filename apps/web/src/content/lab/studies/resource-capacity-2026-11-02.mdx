## 01 / More of what keeps a ship running

**Accepted · 2 November 2026.** The Potential Well adds capacity to the ship’s reserve without generating resources. The UI still calls that reserve **energy**; in the fiction it meets both material needs (such as oxygen, food and fuel) and energy needs (such as heat, light and electricity). Sunlight and gravity replenish it without the study prescribing how conversion works.

The study took 100 intrinsic capacity, +85 for the normal fitting and +140 for the heavy at twice the mass as givens. Both sizes needed to read as the same static equipment above or below a hull, without animation or sound. The [utility family](/lab/studies/utility-machinery-2026-11-02#family-colour) supplied the cool body palette and #CEDB17 structural accent.

## 02 / Giving the reserve a physical identity

<h3 id="name-description">02.1–02.2 / Potential Well</h3>

“Containment” suggested how the machinery was built, “accumulator” what it did. “Differentiator” implied processing beyond the brief. **Potential Well** names what it holds: multiple containment units maintain a reserve in subspace. What the potential actually is stays unspecified.

Approved part-list description:

> A bank of well containment units stores potential in subspace, ready to supply the ship with matter and energy as needed.

<h3 id="construction-ideas">02.3–02.4 / Make the separate wells legible</h3>

The nine constructions tested whether a dark opening, repeated capsules or a protective housing made containment legible. The slot locates the fitting, but does not have to bound its silhouette. The cassette, ring bank and nested cradles went forward because they made multiple contained units understandable.

<h3 id="chambers">02.6 / Broad planes over underlying rails</h3>

Layered rims and bevelled rails looked artificially extruded. Replace the stacked lips and inner-wall bands with broad sloping faces around dark recesses. The yellow rails sit underneath the chambers: straight in plan, with two shaded longitudinal surfaces and no end bevels. The plate compares square, diamond and elongated chambers in different arrangements at 8× and 1× drawing scale.

![Nine chamber and rail combinations compare transverse pairs, longitudinal pairs and groups of three.](/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02/02-6-chambers.png)

<h3 id="long-pairs">02.7 / Bring back the simple ring wells</h3>

Pair the original single band of broad facets and dark centres with the longitudinal layout and underlying rails of 02.6 option 05. Vary chamber size, spacing and rail separation without adding extra rims. **Option 03, Close coupling**, was selected for the normal device.

![Three longitudinal ring-well pairs, ending with the selected close coupling.](/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02/02-7-long-pairs.png)

<h3 id="sizing">02.8 / Normal: Close coupling</h3>

Use **0.55× artwork scale**, upper-left lighting, two yellow connections only between the wells, and a subdued grey lattice in the gap. Remove the yellow extensions beyond the chambers. The normal’s outer dimensions are about **7.7 × 16.5 ship units**.

<h3 id="heavy">02.9 / Heavy: larger containment wells</h3>

**A / Larger containment wells** beat a three-well bank and a broader pair. It stays recognizable as the normal device while looking heavier, keeping the same two-well construction, connector placement and palette with a slightly stronger lattice. It measures about **9.9 × 20.9 ship units** at the same 0.55× artwork scale, not a different display zoom. Shape and well count do not dictate capacity arithmetic.

<h2 id="readiness">03 / Preserve the selected drawings</h2>

The [normal SVG](/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02/close-coupling-sized.svg) and [heavy SVG](/studies/resource-capacity-2026-11-02/heavy-larger.svg) use different coordinate dimensions. Preserve those dimensions rather than normalizing their bounding boxes. The handoff called for checks of fit, occlusion and scale on all four hulls, with no change to ship geometry or new collector or conversion mechanics.

<h2 id="scope">04 / What adoption touched</h2>

The original page records local integration into the catalog, builder, flight and Lab: normal retains the `battery` save ID, adds 85 capacity and weighs 18; heavy adds 140 and weighs 36. Intrinsic capacity stays 100. The handoff also records inspection of every heavy equipment mount. That implementation record did not itself claim a published release.
