## Stopped · 15 November 2026

The study was declared failed and stopped after over-correcting and looping on irrelevant details. The cause was not established. No inhabited planet design was accepted. Improving isolated planet images did not establish how an inhabited world with an atmosphere belongs in the game. Surface, atmosphere, habitation and Earth's identity remain unresolved.

On 16 November, the [rock-atmosphere study](/lab/studies/rock-atmospheres-2026-11-15) selected Impact with Cirrus and atmospheric heat as placeholder Earth for Practice. That does not accept the earlier Earth or habitation designs. [Closure evidence](/lab/reviews/earth-2026-11-14).

<a id="01"></a>

## 01 / An inhabited world at flight scale

The study asked how Earth could be recognizable beside Mercury and Venus at flight scale while establishing a language for inhabited Earth-like worlds. The 15 November scope amendment favored an arcade reading shaped by Void Drift's visual language over preserving actual imagery and continental proportions. How far geography can be simplified, how atmosphere belongs and how habitation reads at night remain open. A climate around 2100–2400 and restrained technology were possible influences, not selected designs.

## 02 / Findings

<a id="02-1"></a>

### 02.1 / Atmosphere and surface

The comparison added blue limb haze, a fading lit edge and a warm transition into darkness to a mapped Earth. The surface did not yet feel convincing enough to judge atmospheric fit. Neither version was accepted. A separate atmospheric heat-damage band 1–5u above the body was an open gameplay proposal, not accepted tuning; visible haze did not define a damage zone.

<a id="02-2"></a>

### 02.2 / Appearance references

A withdrawn deeper-colour rendering felt candy-like and like limited-palette pixel art. Changing colour, cloud and illumination together did not isolate the problem. Intact Apollo 17, DSCOVR EPIC and Rosetta portraits were useful, but clouds hid too much surface; no appearance was selected. Their different illumination and weather make this a reference board, not one scene under one Sun. The portraits cannot establish motion, relighting or habitation.

<a id="02-3"></a>

### 02.3 / Surface and possible futures

Some ocean treatments looked like deep-blue plastic or a children's toy; less saturated photographic references were preferred. The cause remains unresolved and desaturation alone is not established as a solution. Research found regional and thematic projections, not a validated cloud-free true-colour globe for 2100–2400. A future globe would require authored interpretation of a scenario; none was selected. [Sources and provenance](/studies/earth-2026-11-14/references-02-3.json).

<a id="02-4"></a>

### 02.4 / Restrained appearance

All six references were useful; Earthrise, ISS sunglint and the two Celestia views particularly conveyed the preferred less saturated feeling. “Analog photographic” describes that restrained appearance, not film grain, a vintage filter or a rendering technique. Exact colour and atmosphere remain open. [Sources and provenance](/studies/earth-2026-11-14/references-02-4.json).

<a id="02-5"></a>

### 02.5 / Maps

Natural Earth I and II were too bright for the intended appearance, but their detailed seas and graphic treatment were useful counterpoints. Their ocean-floor relief is cartographic, not a photograph of visible ocean texture. No map was selected. [Sources and preparation](/studies/earth-2026-11-14/references-02-5.json).

<a id="02-6"></a>

### 02.6 / Nine sketches

All nine generated sketches were significantly better than the original attempts, but no individual variant or rendering method was selected. They used Celestia and Natural Earth II as visual references, generated 14 November 2026; they are appearance sketches, not satellite data or production assets. Geography, clouds, reflection and haze also vary, so the comparison does not isolate a single variable.

<a id="02-7"></a>

### 02.7 / Earth in games

X-COM's geographic interface, Star Control II's narrative state, and Mass Effect and Halo's reflection, atmosphere and habitation offered different ways to compose Earth. These are interpretations, not claims about the artists' intent. The period-game search was too narrow to support a general claim about the era. The SC2 starbase capture's port and the Mass Effect guide image's build were unidentified; Halo promotional imagery is not evidence of released gameplay fidelity. [Asset provenance](/studies/earth-2026-11-14/references-02-7.json).

The Star Control II 3DO ending was previously embedded as a video reference, not an inspected still; no frame-specific colour finding or timecode was recorded. The motion evidence is not retained here. [Upload provenance](https://forum.uqm.stack.nl/index.php?topic=4721.msg62710) and [version comparison](https://wiki.starcontrol.com/index.php/Version_Comparison) document the historical reference.

<a id="02-8"></a>

### 02.8 / The whole world

Familiar land shapes and the relationships between land, water, clouds and air may carry Earth's identity, but the allowable simplification was not established. Separating surface, weather, atmosphere and lights was a proposed way to investigate them, not a settled construction: the successful 02.6 images combined these elements and did not prove separation would preserve their qualities. Night lights were a possible cue, not a selected treatment.

<a id="02-9"></a>

### 02.9 / Shape and identity

H1 / Silver was a comparison anchor, not a winner. A retained its detail, B broadened painted regions and C simplified continents. None conveyed an inhabited atmospheric planet belonging in the game; no variant was selected. B and C also changed clouds, tones and geography, so the comparison does not isolate coastline detail. Clouds and atmosphere were baked in and night habitation remained unexplored.
