## Outcome / Three approved cockpit families

The Fiddler reference suggested gold at the forward tip, a space-dark middle and green toward the rear, with a central ridge dividing the reflection. The study approved a material language that keeps this colour family, dark depth and reflection balance while letting highlights, transitions and seams follow the shape. Curvature bends the dark band; a real ridge breaks it. An angular outline alone does neither. The study was accepted; its last content change was 26 November 2026 (Git history).

The approved scope is cockpit glass for Daehan, LHI and Aegis, with restrained gold framing. Hull planes retain flat fills; the remaining ship family and Mark II Escort Frigate are excluded. This authorized an art-direction exception to the universal black-canopy rule, not a general treatment for opaque panels.

## Four surface constructions

The four approved procedural sketches use a fixed forward-left light. Round and curved samples test continuous roll-off; the faceted sample interprets Fiddler's split planes. They are not ship geometry or physical reflection simulations. Colour, band width and contrast were provisional in this comparison; cockpit fit and flight-size readability remained open.

## Daehan / Space glass dome

Scout was approved as the family example: gradient direction **−70°**, reflection edge **−30°**, gold border retained. The green–space–gold gradient, dark edge roll-off and gold collar support a crisp bowed reflection crossing glass and collar; reflection and gradient rotate independently. It reads as a space glass dome. Fitting other Daehan cockpit geometries remained work; visual approval did not claim deployment or flight review.

## LHI / Cut glass at the ridge

Wasp's production canopy planes were compared with straight reflections and a thin gold perimeter. A connected the reflection at the ridge and changed direction; **B** stepped it between planes. B was selected to retain the offset, rather than the connected reflection. These were study overlays, not production changes, and static views cannot establish readability in motion.

![Wasp comparison of connected A and offset B reflections](/studies/gilded-surfaces-2026-11-03/reconstruction-1-1.png)

*Reconstruction, 27 November 2026, 1280 px viewport: Wasp A and B, 4× ship, 0.65 flight scale and isolated canopy detail. Not contemporaneous evidence.*

B was then approved on Wasp and Fiddler: offset **13% of canopy length**, darker right pane and thin gold perimeter, using production glass outlines. Fiddler tested the relationship against a larger surrounding hull. Integration and flight verification remained separate work.

## Aegis / Blue visor

The approved continuous visor uses gradient **−79°**, reflection **−39°**, blue reflection **#427fe0 at 32% opacity** and a thin gold perimeter. The crisp reflection rotates independently from the green–space–gold gradient. Study and production consumed the same material renderer.

## Delivery and evidence limits

Scout, XF-1, Wasp and Fiddler shared `drawBuild` across Hangar, Lab and flight. Existing cockpit clipping paths made silhouette, fitting and simulation changes unnecessary. Approved Canvas materials were to be painted after hull geometry and before fittings. Authored reflections stay in ship coordinates and rotate with the ship, rather than tracking world light; they are not physical environment maps. Local implementation was connected. The study did not claim a push, deployment or completed human flight review. Automated checks cannot establish feel, readability amid effects or sustained device performance. Still images cannot verify motion.
