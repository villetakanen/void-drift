Accepted on 7 November 2026 (date from Git history): establish a steady, Sun-like G star before exploring other stellar families. The study selected its collection, damage and HUD model and authorized a bounded integration; [the integration commit](https://github.com/villetakanen/void-drift/commit/d87aeba2a877984d74b24e390d646e4b033a4e5f) followed. The study's findings are not themselves a decision for other stars or HUDs. The owning [star specification](https://github.com/villetakanen/void-drift/blob/main/docs/specs/game/world/star/spec.md) records project intent.

## Stellar character in play

A sun should belong in the fiction and give a pilot a learnable encounter: why approach, what changes nearer the star, and when to escape. Real stars inform relationships; the game compresses distances, sizes and times for play. The pilot needs readable consequences, not a simulation of stellar evolution or detailed spectra.

<h3 id="stellar-model">Stellar model</h3>

Describe a star by temperature, radius, mass, radiant output and activity, choosing coherent families before compressing their extremes. Spectral class is identity, not difficulty. Preserve the temperature-to-colour ordering: hotter O–B stars are bluer and cooler K–M stars redder. A filtered flight display exaggerates differences and limits glare; saturated neon is a display convention. Colour alone promises neither safety nor gravity strength. [NASA: stellar classes](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/stars/).

Radius and output are distinct: luminosity depends on emitting area and temperature, L ∝ R²T⁴, so a cool giant can be luminous. A larger disc need not mean stronger gravity. Gravity attracts more strongly inward, with compressed strength and a finite gameplay cutoff rather than a physical edge. An inward dive gains speed but requires an outward climb; gravity is not a permanent free speed boost. [NASA: radiation model](https://spacemath.gsfc.nasa.gov/SMBooks/SMBook5.pdf); [NASA: gravity](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/weight-gravitational-force/).

Activity is independent of colour: even red dwarfs can flare. Begin with steady stars; a future event would need a legible build-up and bounded effect, with reliable warning a fictional sensor capability. A crisp luminous body is the fatal boundary; the restrained atmosphere is not an exact safety marker. Exposure represents ship response, not gas temperature. [NASA: solar activity](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/solar-storms-and-flares/); [Proxima’s flares](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/neighboring-stars-bad-behavior-large-and-frequent-flares/); [NASA: the Sun](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/).

<h3 id="stellar-decisions">Standard model decisions</h3>

- Use our Sun as the steady baseline. Size gravity reach in relation to maximum pull, with a finite cutoff; visible radius alone determines neither gravity nor collection reach. This relationship did not itself change gravity tuning or force law.
- Scope the model to regular stars, small to large; quasars and fictional miniature black holes are future subjects. A catalogue and eruptions were not settled.
- Fictional shielding permits brief proximity, not passage through the body. Compact integrated collectors harvest radiation without deployable panels; their mechanism is undefined. Their finite operating envelope is not the end of sunlight. Solar collection is distinct from gravity-coil recovery. A bare ship experiences the same radiation and gravity without a fitted collector's benefit. Use hull loss for exposure rather than introducing a heat reserve or cooling minigame.
- The three proposed, steady main-sequence encounters were cool K/M (compact, approachable, modest income and gentler pull), solar-like G/F (a deliberate sustained arc and escape), and hot A/B/O (a wider, earlier commitment and greater exposure). Greater luminosity does not guarantee a better net bargain. An active M star could overturn the calm. Giants and compact remnants need separate families. [NASA: stellar families](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/types/).

These encounter sketches used the same assembled ship, collection equipment, part-filled reserves and undamaged hull, without planets, debris or random eruptions. They were authored sequences, not simulated paths or measured flight results. Escape margins, profitability, warning and whether three felt families make seven class labels meaningful remained open. Other stellar types, colour and activity mappings, and eruptions were reserved for follow-up.

<h2 id="exploration">Collection and exposure</h2>

<h3 id="charging-field">Collecting inside the well</h3>

The initial Terra hypothesis placed collection's outer boundary at the gravity cutoff, so recovery requires entering the well. Extending collection could permit recovery without gravity; restricting it could require a deeper commitment. Neither weak pull nor an outer collection band guarantees safety from radiation or contact. Collection onset, net-power break-even and damage onset are separate thresholds; thrust, equipment and collector capacity change break-even.

<h3 id="charging-curves">Linear paths and selected reference</h3>

The first comparison used a linear collection curve from zero at the gravity edge to a surface peak, C(d) = peak × (reach − d) / (reach − radius), zero outside. The reference Wasp had one Light ion engine, stabilizers, empty equipment slots and one inherent collector-equivalent. Production motion and gravity generated the paths; ion draw changed costs, not handling. Sampled production consumption supplied the energy calculation.

The initial 4 E/s peak, 1.5 E/s life support and 2 E/s ion draw gave coasting break-even around 216 u and forward-thrust break-even around 71 u from centre, with surface 35 u and reach 324 u. All B1 passes spent more than collected over 14 seconds despite brief recovery on closer paths. These were initial-budget results, not the selected budget. The 60 Hz totals omitted battery saturation, power starvation and radiation damage; they did not establish survival or integrated flight experience.

<h3 id="charging-selection">Selected standard-Sun reference / 7.5 E/s</h3>

Human review found a 7.5 E/s peak per collector-equivalent satisfying across different burns and Wasp paths. The selected experiment used 0.8 E/s life support and 1.0 E/s Light ion draw; those were experiment settings, not a change to every ship's rules. The Path tool was to support independently defined collection fields, gross collection, consumption, net charging and cumulative battery change synchronized with path and time. Reusable configuration remained delivery work.

<h3 id="field-shape">Accepted field shapes</h3>

Both a 10% edge and a 10% edge with a 20 u inner shoulder were accepted as useful alternatives: 7.5 E/s maximum, 0.75 E/s at the shared outer gravity/collection boundary, and zero outside. The shoulder maintains the maximum from the surface to 20 u beyond it. At the reference budget the edge nearly pays the 0.8 E/s life-support cost, but forward thrust requires moving inward. The shoulder makes maximum collection accessible outside the luminous surface, without establishing a safe place to linger. The nonzero edge jumps from 0.75 E/s to zero across the boundary. Acceptance of both profiles concerned charging behaviour, not radiation balance; the later delivery selected the shoulder. Separate stills of the accepted shape comparisons are not retained; the reference-path image above does not show those variants.

<h3 id="exposure-economy">Trading hull for energy</h3>

Damage has independent reach, intensity and profile. It represents exposure beyond ship protection; separating it from collection is a gameplay distinction, not a claim that light is separate from energy. The damage envelope should be much smaller than collection. Permanent exposure must be distinguishable from intermittent flares. Colour and atmosphere were proposed cues, but their mapping to hazard remained open.

Maximum damage begins at the shoulder's outer edge, 20 u beyond the surface, and holds inward. Two linear alternatives used 1.5 hull/s maximum and ended at 70 u (A) or 100 u (B). At 80 u A permits radiation-free charging while B costs hull for the same energy; at 100 u both are radiation-free, not gravity-free. B was selected. Optional repair's 0.2 E/s running cost was a comparison assumption; its restoration rate remained unknown. The local ten-second budgets were rates multiplied by time, not hover or orbit simulations, and assumed sufficient battery space and hull. Manoeuvring costs extra; energy and hull curve crossings are not a repair exchange rate.

<h3 id="sun-hud-preview">Field states in the game view</h3>

The presentation trial used production G-star animation and the Wasp renderer with forced states, not live gameplay. Android green is the Suns HUD field language, not a change to other HUDs. Gravity is dim and dashed; solar is solid and brightens when collecting. Hazard is dotted, muted amber while inactive and red while damage is active. Active collection adds a 3.3% Android-green fill; active damage adds an 11% red fill. Inactive fields have no fill. Text repeats active states: collecting does not imply net charging.

World-anchored boundaries remain one CSS pixel wide at every zoom and supported resolution; active states do not thicken them. Device pixel ratio affects rasterization, not intended width. When gravity and solar coincide, draw solar 2 CSS pixels inside gravity while both are visible. The inset is screen-space only and changes no gameplay boundary. Production states must follow actual effects rather than preview toggles.

<h2 id="standard-sun-decision">Delivery decision / One standard Sun</h2>

| Property | Selected value |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and surface | G / standard Sun; 35 u radius |
| Shoulder | 20 u beyond surface; outer radius 55 u |
| Collection | 7.5 E/s per collector-equivalent through shoulder, linear decline to 0.75 E/s at outer edge, zero outside |
| Collection reach | Same as unchanged standard gravity field, approximately 324 u from centre |
| Damage | 1.5 hull/s through shoulder, linear decline to zero at 100 u from centre, zero beyond |
| Life support | 0.8 E/s |
| Light ion | 1.0 E/s; energy cost only, unchanged thrust and handling |
| Inherent collection | One collector-equivalent on every ship; fittings add to it |
| Reference ship | Wasp |

Distances are centre-based except the shoulder width: the 100 u boundary is not 100 u beyond the shoulder. Gravity tuning remains unchanged. Battery capacity, depletion and contact constrain actual flight. Optional repair output and implementation were outside this commitment. Wasp is not the only ship eligible for inherent collection.

## Fit and handoff

At handoff the production game already supplied inherent collection, fitted-collector scaling, resource capacity, propulsion and gravity, but solar charging and hull damage used radius-scaled stepped zones. For the G-star radius of 35 u the old damage boundaries were 87.5, 148.75 and 210 u: the 55 u shoulder lay within the innermost band. That code-derived overlap did not establish that the shoulder was too dangerous. The integrated profile needed verification against actual battery limits and contact. The preview did not establish in-game HUD behaviour.

The authorized scope was shared collection and damage definitions, standard Sun identity in game and landing presentation, the stated energy costs, HUD lines and states, reusable Path energy analysis, and a Suns Lab article explaining fiction, zones, inherent collection, battery limits and the HUD inset. The star, survival, propulsion, game-screen and Motion instrument specs needed reconciliation with implementation. Sun artwork, palette, corona redesign, other effects and stellar variety were deferred. Checks called for included boundary and collector scaling, cap/depletion, damage and contact; in-play idle, collecting, net-draining, damaging and full-battery states; and line width and inset across zoom and display density. This handoff did not claim completed integration, in-game verification or publication; see the integration commit and owning specs for subsequent work.
