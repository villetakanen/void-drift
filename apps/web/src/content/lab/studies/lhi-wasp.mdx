**Accepted · 1 November 2026.** The Wasp separates turning from forward thrust: one stern engine pushes the ship, and four recessed stabilizers turn it. The study kept that layout fixed and explored how to give the practice hull a distinct silhouette without making the tail look bulky.

<h2 id="ab306-refinements">Hull · 02.1–02.6</h2>

The gull-wing study kept variant **08**’s clipped nose, forward canopy and three-ribbon wings, but rejected its broad tail. **08.09** narrowed the wing-to-tail bridge; **AB-3** simplified its busy facets into a truncated wedge. **AB3-06** moved the stabilizer cheek higher, but its winglets still pinched at a single vertex. The approved **06.9** joined them along a shared edge and aligned their trailing edges so that the side and rear winglets read as one surface.

![Nine AB3-06 refinements, with the approved 06.9 hull in the last card.](/studies/lhi-wasp/02-6-winglet-refinements.png)

*02.6 compares bare hulls with the same cyan-slate palette and dark canopy. The accepted silhouette is 49.5 units long by 36 wide; the study used a bare mass of 60.*

<h2 id="flight-demo">Flight · 02.9–02.10</h2>

Both controls together (`LR`) fire the rear engine. Left alone fires front-right and rear-left; right alone fires front-left and rear-right. The opposite pair brakes residual spin on release. The study used 0.22 Light Ion strength per slit and drew plumes above the fitted ship.

## What adoption touched

The study identified three assumptions to revisit: loadouts requiring a linked side-engine pair, steering and effects assuming only one pair of stabilizers, and the renderer drawing wing equipment above the hull. Acceptance scoped the hull, controls and layering, not a change to global gravity or friction. The [Hangar](/hangar) shows the current game rather than these frozen prototypes.
