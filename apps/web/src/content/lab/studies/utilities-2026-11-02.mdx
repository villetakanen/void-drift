**Stopped · 2 November 2026.** The study was closed as failed during exploration. No utility design was accepted for implementation; readiness and delivery scope were not reached. The retained preferences apply only to this exploration. No causal lesson or standing process change was adopted.

## 01 / Equipment for surviving a dangerous pass

A ship can come back with energy left and a damaged hull, or with an intact hull and almost no power. Equipment should make those needs legible through its appearance and its effect on flight. Storage carries a reserve, solar and gravity chargers replenish it, and automatic repair restores hull damage. The study assumed invisible internal storage on every chassis, visible storage only for extra capacity, and either solar or gravity charging depending on ship type. At the time, ships had a battery and solar coil outside their optional slots; this did not settle how the new equipment would mount. Gravity charging was new to the study.

The equipment needed convincing low-poly bodies and recognizable silhouettes in the builder and in flight. Parts could be small or bulky enough to obscure the hull and change the ship’s character. Only extra storage received concrete exploration. When charging should be earned, how the two sources should differ, and whether repair should compete for energy remained open. So did charger and repair animation, including speculative solar wings and a visible response in a gravity well. Exact balance and final chassis assignments were outside this first inquiry.

## 02 / Extra storage as a visible object

### 02.1 / Light and Heavy, above and below

The chamfered case with recessed amber cells and no engine nozzle was rejected as a poor aesthetic fit. Light was a 20 × 12 drawing with two cells, trialled at **+50% of internal capacity**; Heavy was 30 × 24 with three cells at **+100%**. Light was lower mass and Heavy higher mass, without tuned values. Two sizes and the option to change the ship’s silhouette remained open.

The top-down fitting used the production Fighter hull geometry without engines. Both modules used the same illustrative local mount **(−8, 10)**. Above and below changed only the drawing order: the hull hides Light below it and partly hides Heavy. The isolated modules explain what the hull conceals. The comparison raised a need to reconsider the Fighter’s utility slots after changes to its engine slots. Moving one forward was a possibility, not a decision. Clearance and fit still needed checking against actual slots and engines.

### 02.2–02.3 / Industrial machinery and visible construction

The study preferred industrial machinery: less streamlined than ship bodies, but not deliberately ugly. The engines’ charcoal bodies and lit outlets showed how silhouette, body and light could work together, not that utilities had to be dark. Pointed guns were a contrast, not a settled weapon design. The machinery had to read when small and still hold up enlarged.

**A / Clamped cell assembly** places two transverse clamps across three separate pressure-like cells, leaving the gaps and stepped ends visible. **B / Load-bearing frame** holds the same cells in an open cradle with broad shoulders and projecting feet. Both used provisional steel tones and muted status marks. A was preferred because it exposed the machinery itself, not because its palette or cell geometry was settled. The alternatives compare construction, not Light and Heavy capacity.

### 02.4 / Nine exposed forms for one small store

No outer casing or protective frame: working parts, feet and connectors could remain visible, like equipment on a space station. These alternatives held materials, scale and static lighting constant. They represent one small extra store; cell count does not indicate capacity. Colour, the energy technology and physical operation were not decided.

| Form | Construction |

| 02 / Twin vessels | Two narrow reservoirs with a visible cross-feed. |
| 03 / Canted coil | A coil at 60° across a vessel’s upper end. |
| 04 / Open helix | A winding forming the body around an exposed core. |
| 05 / Pancake stack | Three broad discs on a spindle, separated by gaps. |
| 06 / U-loop | Two upright legs with a broad return bend. |
| 07 / Unequal pair | Main reservoir and smaller accumulator with external plumbing. |

**05 / Pancake stack** was close enough to explore further, though neither its large nor its small appearance was settled. **04 / Open helix** and **07 / Unequal pair** looked industrial at small size but did not clearly suggest an energy reserve.

### 02.5 / Layered storage with visible working parts

The first disc studies lost the sense of separate working parts. The replacement returned to gaps between layers and added visible contacts, recesses, connectors and top hardware. All nine used three layers at the same scale. Shape and colour changed together, so the comparison does not isolate their effects.

| Form | Construction |
| --- | --- |
| 01 / Terminal crown | Black top plate, paired terminals and yellow ceramic bridge. |
| 02 / Recessed core | Deep black recesses and a lime core. |
| 03 / External bus | Offset yellow conductors connect the plates to a top junction. |
| 04 / Split plates | Paired lobes, dark central channel and exposed contacts. |
| 05 / Black reservoirs | Dark plates, pale rims and lime terminals. |
| 06 / Service head | Asymmetric top and side return line, drawing on Unequal pair. |
| 07 / Insulated layers | Yellow separators distinguish the black plate faces. |
| 08 / Coil junction | The exposed winding from 02.4’s Open helix sits above the stack rather than forming the body. |
| 09 / Contact shoes | Broad yellow contacts on each layer and a black top manifold. |

**03 / External bus** and **07 / Insulated layers** were promising for their exposed connections, insulation and separate working parts. **09 / Contact shoes** worked specifically in the small image. None was accepted as a whole. Repeated tilted slabs still risked looking like databases on a stick. Deep black was exploratory; orange was excluded, while yellow, brand lime-yellow and an Android-like green remained candidates.

The storage concept was science-fiction energy held in working surfaces, fields or lattices, not an electrical battery. Hexagonal lattices fit the idea but did not prescribe holes, a flat icon or a particular silhouette. Exposed machinery should look deliberately engineered, not improvised: rugged ship hardware or scientific apparatus, with working surfaces and structural members substantial enough for a battleship and a collision. No protective enclosure was preferred. No visible charge indicator, blinking or storage animation was wanted: remaining energy should not be disclosed to an opponent. This superseded the empty-store blink idea but did not settle charger or repair behaviour. Possible accent colours marked material or identity, not charge.

### 02.6 / Designs removed; recovery not attempted

The unavailable sketches are the first 02.5 (three or four discs with rounded, bevelled or angular profiles), its close-packed revision, the first 02.6 (flat hexagonal chambers and lattices), and the second 02.6 (nine lattice-plate, deep-cell and paired-surface forms). They were removed during the study and are described in the [closure record](https://github.com/villetakanen/void-drift/blob/fdc1772f/docs/loop-evidence/2026-11-02-utilities-failed-study.md); none is recreated here. The early stacks lost the sense of multiple working parts. Neither 02.6 answered the intended construction question. Changing geometry, viewpoint, proportions and visual language together, and taking the lattice idea literally, were possible explanations, not established causes.

The proposed recovery was to describe how 02.5’s **03 / External bus** and **07 / Insulated layers** appeared to be built before choosing one physical relationship to vary. The comparison would pair one unchanged reference with one bounded construction change, holding the other geometry, viewpoint, scale and colour fixed. A candidate was the relationship between the working surfaces, their edge thickness and the connections between them. Another nine-option grid was deferred. No such comparison was made before closure.

## 03–04 / Questions left open

What physical construction convincingly holds energy? Which materials and colours distinguish it, and what can be recognized at actual flight scale with engines and real mounts? Light and Heavy sizes, mass, capacity and slot placement remained unresolved, as did the designs and flight mechanics of both chargers and automatic repair. Historical checks showed that some iterations rendered or built, not that the design succeeded; there was no full final verification at closure. The [visual language of ship utilities](/lab/studies/ship-utility-language-2026-11-02) was a separate follow-up, not acceptance of these designs.
