**Accepted · 11 November 2026** (date from the page's Git history). A quiet action is a raised pane with partial brackets; a link is lime text with a line. The study's accepted design is a proposal for the project, not itself authorization to implement it. The implementation scope explicitly required a separate go-ahead.

## 01 / One lit action among quiet controls

The existing outlined actions spent lime on every button and filled lime under a pointer, competing with the filled call to action. They looked like shapes on a page beside the Hangar's raised tabs, toggle and switch. The question was how to make quiet buttons and links pressable without mistaking them for selection controls or labels, and how they should sit beside other actions. The filled call to action at rest and under a pointer, cut corners, 44px minimum target, action order and available actions were fixed.

<a id="stamp"></a>

The proposed reach covered shared actions in the game and Lab, including the title, Hangar, pause, run complete, Settings, Credits and Leaderboard. Flight-deck controls and Hangar mount and part controls were outside scope. The Leaderboard sort pair remained an adjacent choice; the red button was unsettled. The [question review](/lab/reviews/buttons-2026-11-11) concerns an earlier framing; the corrected text was not retested.

## 02 / Exploration

The images below reconstruct the examples using current code at 1280px on 27 November 2026; they are not evidence of the earlier observations. Forced states show appearance, not feel. Examples use sample ship facts and sentences, narrowed panels where noted, and inert action copies. The study did not test touch. Stills cannot preserve the motion or feel of pointer transitions and held presses; no motion evidence is retained here.

<h3 id="materials">02.1 / Four materials</h3>

A / Pane went forward because it matched the existing controls and was the only balanced title-screen set that belonged to this world. It was not pressable enough and was too sleek. Its link, focus and pointer wash were not accepted; the wash read as blinking. B, C and D were not pursued. The round did not show motion, selection, the horizontal strip, touch or the call to action's held, focus and unavailable states.

<h3 id="rest">02.2 / At rest</h3>

Heavier labels and an extra elevation step looked out of place: each bent a rule of the design language and read as a mistake rather than emphasis. The regular label and pane remained. Adding marks or replacing the object answered a different question and was discarded. The quiet buttons' states were not designed here; dimmed Gauntlet was a stand-in.

<h3 id="edges">02.3 / Partial edge</h3>

1 / Brackets became the working hypothesis: the incomplete edge was a technical detail rather than a web border and made quiet buttons look pressable. It was too bright and stood apart from the unframed call to action; the latter was accepted for now. Heavy sides and the stand-off frame were not pursued. States were not designed in this round.

2 / The pane's hue was kept: lighter violet belonged to the pane and held on panel and void, though it might still be too bright. Fainter neutral and dim brand colour were not pursued. Arm length and gap were fixed; states were not designed.

<h3 id="lightness">02.5 / Bracket lightness</h3>

25% was chosen: `lch(25% 32 290)`, visible on the void and close to the panel surface. The pane was 11% light on void and 16.5% on panel. The settled rest design has a regular label and a 1px bracket at each end, leaving the middle 40% of the top and bottom open. The narrowed title panels made buttons narrower than in the game; arms and gap were fixed and states were not designed.

<h3 id="together">02.10 / Gaps</h3>

Use 8px top to bottom and 16px start to end, rather than one gap for both. The title group already used 8px; the Hangar row used 20px and 180px-wide buttons. The strip and padding were not shown, and the comfort of a 4px gap under a finger was not checked. Targets were 44px tall.

<h3 id="strip">02.11 / Band behind a row</h3>

0 / Today's void band was kept, with 8px above and below and 16px at each end. The host chooses whether buttons gather at an end or push to both ends. The band is always one flex row, never wrapping or stacking; a row that does not fit has too many buttons. The narrowed comparison omitted the Leaderboard sort pair and the old stacking below 580px.

<h2 id="whole">02.14 / The whole action</h2>

The accepted sheet assembled the decisions:

- The pane is one elevation step above a panel, two above the void. At rest it has a regular label and 1px end brackets, open across the middle 40%, in `lch(25% 32 290)`.
- Pointer lights the brackets lime. Held gives the pane a 28% lime tint inside the lit brackets. Keyboard focus is like pointer; the call to action has dark brackets inside its fill.
- Unavailable buttons have an empty frame and muted label; the call to action's frame is lime.
- Columns have 8px gaps, rows 16px. A row stands on a void band, one flex row with 8px vertical and 16px end padding.
- Links have lime words and a 1px lime line at 20%; pointer and focus light the line fully without changing the words.

Four proposals were accepted together without alternatives: a held call to action dims its fill one step; a held link looks no different from pointer; an unavailable link has muted words and no line; actions lose the selected mark. No game action was to become a link: a link belongs in a sentence, while CREDITS among buttons looked out of place.

<h2 id="readiness">03 / Readiness and limits</h2>

The study judged that the shared clipped button could accommodate the design without rebuilding it. The call to action at rest and pointer, 44px target, label size and existing 8px column could remain. A surface would need to tell a button its ground; the bracket colour, state rules, band and link would need changes. Tabs, toggle and switch would retain their own state tokens. The design-system spec, canonical actions page, Lab, game uses and browser test were identified as affected. Earlier studies using shared outlined buttons needed protection; Lab buttons used as switches needed stateful replacements.

Focus visibility was not measured against any guideline: quiet focus is a 1px lime line, identical to pointer; call-to-action focus is 2px dark on lime. No touch screen was checked; touch receives only the held tint. Unavailable calls to action and pointed-at quiet buttons both have lime brackets, distinguished by pane and label. Bracket colour easing requires a registered custom property; immediate change in browsers without it was considered acceptable. Four 180px Hangar buttons need 800px, while the existing row wrapped below 1000px.

<h2 id="scope">04 / Scope recommendations</h2>

The study recommended a new quiet-button class, retaining the old outlined class for earlier studies; a ground custom property with void default, raised by panels and reset by bands; switches or toggles for switch-like Lab buttons; leaving the red button alone and unsettled; applying the link line to Lab and study prose; and narrowing the Hangar's four buttons below 800px while keeping one row. These were recommendations for implementation, not project adoption.

<a id="principles"></a>

The recommended slices were: shared action language and canonical page; game action migration; the band; Lab controls and removal of the selected rule; and one link style. The study proposed a design-system exception for lime retaining the place of an unavailable call to action, and a design-principle sentence about controls as a ship's console. Neither principle change was made by the study. The Leaderboard sort pair becoming a toggle, the red button, ground-aware selection controls, flight-deck controls and Hangar mount and part controls were outside its scope.

The [actions design-system spec](/lab/ds/actions) is the current canonical surface; the study's [implementation handoff](https://github.com/villetakanen/void-drift/blob/b6e638d3/docs/plans/2026-11-11-actions-implementation-handoff.md) records subsequent work and its authorization separately. Study findings do not by themselves establish project-wide intent.
