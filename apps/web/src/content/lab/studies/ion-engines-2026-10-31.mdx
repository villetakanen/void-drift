**Accepted · 31 October 2026.** Light and Heavy ion engines share a straight, precise beam made from complete luminous marks. The study accepted their visual treatment; propulsion and handling remained outside its final scope.

## Question

How should Light and Heavy ion engines share a precise visual language?

## Accepted direction

The approved 02.3 reference emits a complete beam mark from each nozzle on each simulation step. The marks overlap into the active stream, drift in world space and fade after release. Each has straight sides, a bright near-white core, a colored halo and a transparent end. Rotation leaves earlier emissions behind. The reference uses a **0.65-second lifetime**, **80% inherited velocity**, **45 units/s backward drift** and a **quadratic fade**.

Light retains a slender mint-cyan beam. Heavy has a deeper casing and a cooler blue-cyan halo, with fading beam marks **1.4× as long and 1.45× as wide**. Opacity, core color and fade lifetime stay the same. The accepted comparison replays Light’s 02.3 motion and output for both, so it does not establish Heavy’s performance.

## Ruled out and deferred

The 02.2 attached beam disappeared immediately on release while a separate faint wake faded. That split contradicted the intended shared effects lifecycle, so the study chose complete fading marks instead. The earlier compressed-plume sketch used 24% width as an exploratory proportion.

The study withdrew its propulsion and movement investigations because their growing complexity was not advancing the work. Thrust, speed ceilings, mass, power and handling were deferred. Gameplay-scale clarity and fidelity with mixed loadouts still needed inspection.

## Where the work went

The page records local integration of the shared painter in fitted flight and the [ion FX Lab](/lab/fx). The integration assigns visual identity to each emitted mark for Small ion (`ion`) and Heavy ion (`heavy-ion`); fusion, plasma and untyped legacy exhaust retain the generic painter. Physics and power delivery were outside the visual integration. Release verification and publication were tracked separately.

The full study before conversion is in commit `30d80629`, at `apps/web/src/pages/lab/studies/ion-engines-2026-10-31.astro`, with its deleted study files `apps/web/src/components/studies/IonFxAccepted023.svelte`, `IonSizeComparison.svelte`, `IonStreamStudy.svelte` and `accepted-ion-plume.ts` in the same directory.
