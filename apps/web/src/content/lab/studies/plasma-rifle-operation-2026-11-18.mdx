**Accepted · 18 November 2026.** The charge makes two outcomes readable from the fitted ship. Losing the target vents the stored plasma and pushes the ship back. Holding it through release adds a concentrated stream, a contact eruption and a distinct kick. The accepted scope is a functional unsanctioned Playground fitting.

## 01 / One approach, two outcomes

An approaching Fighter has time to start loading its rifle. By the time the channel is bright, the target may have crossed the muzzle. Can the pilot read the commitment early enough to fly through either outcome? The Playground fitting supplies the World drawing, scale, mass and slot. The proposed result is one automatic rifle cycle a pilot can recognise from the mounted light, the target and the Fighter’s motion.

**1. Charging.** Fly a target into and across the rifle’s field. What range and alignment start loading, how long does it take, and when is the shot committed? Compare idle, partly loaded and ready at flight scale. Place provisional light in the exposed channel using the charged drawing. When can the pilot first see the charge, and when does it feel ready? Measure the cost of preparation. Repeat with falling power and with a target returning mid-cycle.

**2. Discharge after target loss.** Let the same target slip clear just before release. Where does the gathered plasma go? Try the earlier paired-vent proposal. Follow both outlets through cooling, check for weak incidental damage, and watch whether sustained backward thrust reads on an off-centre mount without turning the ship. Decide when the rifle can charge again.

**3. Firing on target.** Keep the target in reach through release. Judge the central eruption, backward kick, continuing vent thrust and longer recovery against the discharge-only pass. Vary distance and obstruction to settle reach, falloff, damage to the first surface and newly exposed fragments. Can the pilot still see the escape route and recognise the next affordable shot?

### Conditions for the first comparisons

Use the [Flight scene inspector](/lab/flight-scene) with its fixed burns and scrub the same approach on either side of each transition. Keep a Fighter with a top-visible aft rifle and Service ion drives throughout. Keep a Light Ion Cannon in the opposite aft slot so its firing overlaps rifle preparation. In a matched coast pass, artificially suppress all propulsion thrust, including stabilizers, while retaining fitted mass, initial drift and weapon forces. That pass isolates the kick from the continuing vent push.

Keep automatic fire, zero idle energy draw, the weapon energy floor and existing weapon order as game constraints. Compare full-shot affordability at acquisition and at release, including a competing cannon spending energy between them. Show energy before loading, at target loss or strike, and after recovery. Cross two eligible targets to expose which one the rifle follows. Before proposing a functional Playground part, settle the energy debits and cooldown on every exit, target selection among eligible contacts, and the relation between visible emission, contact damage and ship response. Readiness must then trace those decisions into simulation, rendering, part data and the Playground contract.

<details id="026--what-the-rifle-sheds">
<summary>02.6 / What the rifle sheds</summary>

When the target slips away, the rifle still has hot plasma to shed. Picture a whale blowing water: two fast sprays open from the side ports, briefly tracing the antlers of the earlier projector. Their travel should be visible. As the rifle empties, the escaping material pushes the ship backwards for as long as the ports are venting.

**Settled for this study.** The two ports vent fumes. Each spray inherits the ship’s velocity and adds 300 u/s along its outlet direction. Venting accelerates the ship backwards at 125 u/s². The selected Wasp setup charges in 0.7 s and empties from full in 0.3 s. Exhaust damage remains open.

<details id="027--where-the-stream-opens">
<summary>02.7 / Where the stream opens</summary>

A target stays within reach long enough to fire. A narrow stream leaves the centre channel and travels a short distance before it opens. The fireball came closest, but its scattered points felt light beside the ion cannon. A roughly 70-point strike would carry over four close-range ion hits (16 each). Can a single hot core and a rolling orange edge give that weight to the instant the stream opens? A stock 85-hull wreck now takes fire from both weapons. The stream erupts on its first physical contact for 70 damage. Radius, heat and turbulence shape the fireball while the hit stays fixed. The Wasp’s weaving pass shows the other outcome: the wreck slips out of the rifle’s zone while the ion cannon keeps firing.

The selected Wasp fireball has a 58 u radius, 2× turbulence and 1.2× heat.

## 02.9 / What the rifle sees and what it harms

Can the pilot bring one wreck into firing position while a second only catches the blast or brushes the fumes? A cyan sector opens from the muzzle. Its angle sets how precisely the pilot must aim; its radius sets the furthest eligible wreck centre. At 30°, the weaving Wasp loses its target before the charge completes. Each body takes one primary hit, using its nearest material to the impact point. The three amber rings deal 75 through 10 u, 50 through 26 u, and 25 through 42 u. Each entry into the combined violet fume area deals 3 points; staying inside or crossing between overlapping sprays adds no hit. The light can extend beyond the damage zones.

**Targeting.** Ships and derelicts can trigger a charge. Debris can take a stream hit, blast damage or fume damage while the rifle is firing at another target. The trial exercised acquisition with wrecks.

**Energy.** Charging and firing each draw at one plasma engine’s rate for 0.7 s. At the current 5 power/s, a full cycle costs 7 power. Losing the target before release costs only the elapsed charging time. Release requires 13.5 power and spends 3.5 immediately. After 0.7 s of firing, or once a lost charge finishes venting, the rifle cools for 2.1 s. A target still in the cone starts the next charge.

## 03 / Ready for a Playground trial

The operating model is settled. A pilot who holds a ship or derelict in the muzzle sector through a full charge gets a concentrated stream and a contact eruption. A pilot who loses the target pays for the charge already gathered and feels the vents push the ship back. Both exits commit the rifle to recovery.

The Wasp and Fighter trials establish the two mount views, mass-dependent recoil and competition with a live ion cannon. Wrecks and fragments exercise contact, breakup and incidental damage. The repeat-cycle test held one wreck in the cone through two releases. Gauntlet already supplies live ship targets and hull-damage callbacks to the weapon scheduler. Its quarter-tick combat steps make it the check that charging, recoil and contact damage stay independent of update frequency. The 130 u reach, 30° full angle and 6 u fume radius are the starting Playground tuning.

**Integration boundary.** The fitted part already owns its artwork, mount geometry, mass and Playground eligibility. The study advanced the rifle after the game tick and painted through inspector callbacks. In play each fitted rifle needs its own state in weapon order, access to the ship’s actual power and velocity, and a flight renderer that can read muzzle, vent and impact events. The two moving spray envelopes bound collision work independently of the number of visible particles. Frame cost with several rifles firing remains unmeasured.
