    <h2>01 / Extend the pirate bearing into a contact reading</h2>
    <p>
      Gauntlet already leaves a purple triangle at the viewport edge for each
      living offscreen pirate. It provides direction without separation. The
      selected design extends that capability into one camera-relative
      information layer, so a pilot can distinguish a pirate, the Sun, an intact
      derelict and released energy while reading their bearing and distance
      graphically.
    </p>
    <p>
      The flight box is the active rectangle between the deck’s gutters.
      Following the 19 November boundary decision, both marker eligibility and
      circular placement use this box: an object whose projected centre is
      outside it receives a cue, even when the world continues behind a gutter.
      This deliberately replaces the initial brief’s full-viewport visibility
      rule and the production pirate marker’s current behaviour.
    </p>
    <p>
      Filled triangles preserve bearing presence. A concentric arc grows with
      centre-to-centre world separation; a small inward category mark supplies
      type. Colour supports that reading without relocating deck resources,
      controls, feedback, world-HUD fields or names. The invisible edge circle
      and all its ink stay inside the active box, without a visible ring,
      labels, numerical readouts, leader lines, font icons or miniature target
      artwork. The explicitly requested skull, square and circle are category
      symbols.
    </p>
    <p>
      The study selects a grammar and working configuration, not a new gameplay
      mechanic. Motion examples can reveal transitions and graphic interference;
      combat usefulness remains a hypothesis. Production ownership, contact
      enumeration and specification adoption belong to readiness and scope. No
      study result changes production intent on its own.
    </p>
    <h3>Evidence after the boundary decision</h3>
    <p>
      02.1–02.5 retain evidence for graphic form, colour and proportions. Their
      viewport-based eligibility is outside the amended question. 02.6 is the
      selected example for flight-box eligibility. Camera, simulation and
      gameplay rules remain current product fundamentals; changed presentation
      rules are selected study proposals.
    </p>

      The deck supplies resource bars and values. Those are references for
      graphical syntax, not permission to move their information. Lime already
      means brand, action/selection and power in specific roles; an
      informational contact palette needs an explicit study hypothesis rather
      than a new global token.
    </p>

        centre-based for this first delivery. These limits are explicit; the
        selected direction does not establish every combat situation.
      </p>

        a contact inside the overall viewport but outside the flight box, with

      <h3>Blockers to production delivery</h3>
      <ol>
        <li>
          <strong>Adopt intent and extent.</strong>
          The project must adopt active-box eligibility, the four contact kinds,
          per-mode availability and the selected readings in owning specs. The
          study settles its proposal, not all features’ contracts.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Settle layer ownership.</strong>
          Recommend a dedicated screen-fixed information overlay above the game
          field and below dialogs. The study’s SVG overlay demonstrates that
          separation. An existing pane is still architecturally possible; the
          project must select the owner and its order rather than quietly
          overloading World HUD or Deck.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Define the contact boundary.</strong>
          Give the production view validated type/id/position/lifecycle inputs
          and a shared projection calculation. The inspected salvage
          representation already distinguishes free cells by kind=cell; the new
          contract must state the selected inclusion and removal rules.
        </li>
