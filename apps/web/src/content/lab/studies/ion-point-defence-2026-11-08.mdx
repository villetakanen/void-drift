## A small turret that turns to meet the threat

Accepted 9 November 2026 (date from Git history). The study selected a small automatic ion turret for a weapon slot, intended as the baseline defence and eventually as equipment that never needs to be bought. It should visibly turn and align before firing without competing with flight or the ship’s character. Combat balance, energy cost and the future of the red laser remained adjacent questions.

<section id="turning">
<h2>02.1 / Form and turning</h2>

B, a round base with six rim slots and one pale index mark, was selected at 8 units diameter beside the 16-unit Light Ion Cannon. The marks turn with the turret while the base lighting stays with the ship. A plain disc (A) cannot show its own rotation; C removed the base entirely. B was expected to carry the turning without breaking the ship’s light. The comparison also offered 6- and 10-unit bases.

The fixture used production ship rendering and starter outfits, with a cannon on the next weapon-capable mount when possible; Scout has only one such mount. A deterministic target changed bearing every 1.6 seconds at 55 units, with provisional 180°/s turning. It had no shots, targeting rules, damage, flight or camera motion. Flight scale was one CSS pixel per unit.

<section id="speed">
<h2>02.2 / Turning speed</h2>

180°/s was selected: it read as slightly slow, while 360°/s snapped rather than swung. At 90°/s a ship turning away could outrun the turret. The comparison used the same 8-unit B turret and target script, with an optional fixed left–right ship turn at 2.25 rad/s (about 129°/s). It had no shot; alignment delay and ship-specific turn multipliers were outside the comparison.

<section id="shot">
<h2>02.3 / Alignment and shot</h2>

Three shots per second was selected, matching the Light Ion Cannon, over 1.5 and 6 shots per second. The selected turret turns at 180°/s, fires within 2° of the target and casts an instant blue ray to 100 units, half the cannon’s reach, stopping at the target. The fixture compressed the cannon’s light profile and transparent tip to that reach, with 65% dispersal. Half the cannon’s damage remained an assumption.

The target changed range as well as bearing; one position at 120 units was beyond reach. The fixture had no damage, energy, impact plume or hull occlusion. Its 80 ms flash followed the muzzle; dispersal stayed where fired and decayed with a 0.1-second half-life without the shared effects surface. It was not production simulation.

<section id="fitting">
<h2>02.4 / A turret beneath the hull</h2>

A hidden turret was accepted under production mount layering, without adding a hull marker. Scout hides it entirely, Wasp hides half and Fiddler’s blades hide part; the protruding barrel and ray show it firing. Fighter and Corvette carry it above the hull, where its turning is fully visible. All weapon mounts on Scout, Wasp and Fiddler are beneath the hull, so choosing another mount does not solve the obstruction.

<section id="readiness">
<h2>03 / Remaining choices</h2>

The proposal was to target the closest damageable object by distance from the turret to actual collision geometry, excluding collectible energy, rather than change the laser’s threat priority. Whether to track only within 100 units or pre-aim beyond reach remained open; the fixture pre-aimed. Damage was provisionally half the cannon’s: 8, 6, 4 and 2 in four 25-unit bands. Energy per shot and application of the shared ten-power reserve remained unset.

The supplied SVG preserves the cannon’s barrel width, colours and crossbar, shortening the barrel from 19.5 to 16.9 source units, channel from 10.4 to 8.8 and side housing from 9.0 to 7.9, on two concentric base discs. Against the cannon’s 16-unit flight length the base measures about 8.9 units; the selected 8-unit diameter scales that geometry. The study trace agreed with the SVG within its proportions.

Starter status depended on a future economy, schematic and ownership contract. Fitting the turret into fixed starter outfits and keeping or retiring the red laser were separate decisions. These did not block an alpha turret offered through the Hangar.
</section>

<section id="scope">
<h2>04 / Delivery scope</h2>

The proposed playable turret retained existing weapon-capable mounts and layering, B at 8 units, 180°/s turning, 2° alignment, three shots/s and a 100-unit ray stopped at first contact. The intended production effects were an impact plume, an 80 ms muzzle-following flash and 65% dispersal. The cannon’s 200-unit light profile was to stay unchanged. Damage, energy and tracking range required confirmation. Automatic fire added no button or movement retuning; the study proposed no change to design principles.

The scope called for validated equipment and shared geometry, closest-target simulation, a shorter ion light profile and impact, and Hangar delivery with a playable encounter. Starter outfits, red laser behaviour, mount redesign and layering changes were excluded. These study choices were proposals, not project-wide decisions.
</section>
