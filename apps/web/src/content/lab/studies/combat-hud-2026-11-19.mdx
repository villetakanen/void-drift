## 01 / Finding what lies outside the view

At close zoom, a pilot can know what they want to reach without knowing which
way to fly. The sun offers charging, but the speed-camera view can leave it
out of sight. A known derelict can be outside the view; energy released from
one can travel out of sight before the pilot collects it. A pirate can be
somewhere nearby without the pilot knowing its direction or whether it is
approaching. These supplied gameplay problems motivate the study.

The immediate information need is orientation toward relevant offscreen
objects while keeping the current camera view. Direction is common to all
four situations; whether a pirate is approaching is an additional need.
The study asks what information would help, what existing feedback already
provides, and whether a distinct HUD layer is a useful response. Its contents
and form are not selected. Other useful information may emerge during the
study, but it must earn its place separately; it is not the reason this study
began.

Every proposed cue must remain inside the active flight area, over the camera
view and likely above the world HUD. This layer is separate from the flight
deck and world HUD: neither is being redesigned, and their information is not
being moved into it. Combat HUD remains the working title. Drawing into an
existing pane versus adding a separate pane remains open, as does whether
additional information is needed at all.

### Requirements and boundaries

- **Charging:** help the pilot find the direction of the sun when it is outside
  the close speed-camera view, so they can navigate toward charging.
- **Salvage:** help the pilot locate a known derelict outside the view, and
  pursue energy released from it after that energy leaves the view.
- **Opponents:** help the pilot locate a pirate outside the view and understand
  whether it is approaching. Gauntlet supplies the current pirate context;
  pirates in Survival remain hypothetical.
- **Grounding:** these are supplied problems. Mode-specific object
  availability and existing feedback must be checked when staging examples.
  Available implementation is not evidence that the information need is met.
  Each example must state which objects the pilot knows about and why they
  matter to the intended action; no discovery or detection rule is presumed.
  Existing pirate bearings are implementation evidence, not an accepted
  requirement to retain their exact placement.
- **Containment:** all cues, labels and effects stay inside the active-area
  rectangle bounded by the gutters. The world renders beyond that rectangle;
  its boundary and the edge of the visible world are different. Neither a new
  camera clip nor a square viewport is prescribed.
- **Fixed systems:** flight, camera, charging, collection and weapon mechanics,
  and keyboard and landscape iPad capabilities remain unchanged. No flight-deck
  or world-HUD information is relocated; duplication is not presumed.
- **Open decisions:** which information is useful, which existing cues suffice,
  and whether and how a new layer should communicate the remainder. Distance,
  exact velocity, route guidance and target selection are not requirements yet.
- **Emergent candidates:** weapon acquisition, reasons for weapon silence,
  incoming fire and other ideas may be examined if they prove useful. They are
  neither excluded permanently nor requirements of the originating problem.
  Their inclusion needs its own design reasoning and decision.
- **Rendering and adoption:** pane choice, pass order and clipping remain open.
  The game-screen contract currently says no chrome covers the active area;
  an accepted proposal must resolve its relationship to that rule and name
  changes needed in the screen and render contracts before production work.

## 02.2 / What knowledge changes a pilot’s decision?

This round sets aside 02.1’s assumed contents. The working question becomes:
what combat information would help the pilot make a meaningful decision, and
is anything missing from what the game already communicates? Section 01’s
active-area and separation constraints still hold. This clarifies the inquiry
without selecting a new HUD or its contents.

**Hypothesis:** information earns consideration when two otherwise ambiguous
situations call for different actions, and knowing the difference helps the
pilot choose. Availability in simulation is not sufficient reason to display it.

Read each case as a short thought experiment: first the situation, then the
candidate fact. Consider whether that fact changes what you would try to do.
The blocks below are a reading aid, not a proposal to put cards or sentences
on the flight screen. All situations are authored; no gameplay observation or
human playtest result is claimed.

### Grounding and unresolved choices

- **Finding opponents:** `FlightDeck.svelte` already computes Gauntlet hostile
  bearings outside the full camera viewport. That is distinct from the smaller
  active-area boundary. This is existing feedback to account for, not permission
  to relocate it. Approach or separation information remains an unselected
  candidate; this example does not prescribe its measurement or disclosure.
- **Understanding your weapon:** the plasma-rifle contract specifies venting
  and cooldown after interrupted charging, and per-mount acquisition. Its charge
  light distinguishes stored charge but leaves idle and cooldown unlit. Current
  charge, phase and target identity exist in the rifle state. A player-facing
  explanation of every reason for silence is not an established shared contract.
- **Reading an opponent:** Gauntlet pirates use plasma rifles with independent
  weapon state and shared effects. Their current acquisition is mechanically
  knowable; whether the pilot should receive that knowledge explicitly is a
  design choice. Acquisition does not guarantee release or a hit.

### Finding / Find what matters beyond the view

**Assessment · 19 November 2026:** 02.2 usefully exposed missing
gameplay requirements. The concrete needs concern navigation to things outside

supplied gameplay problems, not findings from a playtest:

| Situation | Missing knowledge | Intended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The pilot knows charging is possible near the sun, but a close speed-camera view hides it. | Which direction leads toward the sun? | Return toward a charging opportunity. |
| The pilot knows a derelict is somewhere in the encounter but cannot locate it at the current zoom. | Which direction leads toward the derelict? | Find and approach it. |
| Energy released from a derelict travels out of view. | Which direction did the collectible go? | Pursue and collect it. |
| A pirate is in the area but outside the view. | Where is it, and is it approaching or moving away? | Orient toward the encounter or respond to an approaching threat. |

The supplied contexts include Survival and Derelicts for finding salvage,
and Gauntlet for pirates, with pirates in Survival a future possibility.
Exact object availability by mode must be checked when staging examples;
these contexts do not authorize adding objects or encounters to a mode.

**Design requirement:** help the pilot orient toward relevant offscreen
objects while retaining the current camera view. Direction is the common
missing information. Relative motion is an additional stated need for pirates;
distance, exact velocity, target selection and route guidance are not yet
requirements. Existing bearing code establishes available means, not that the

**Change in the inquiry:** the proposed question is now, “What information

The active-area containment and separation from the flight deck and world HUD
still hold. “Offscreen” describes the reported problem; the next example must

**Retained evidence:** 02.1 exposed premature placement; 02.2 exposed the

cases remain candidates, not the problems that prompted it. No rendering

## 02.3 / Identity, direction and changing separation

**Proposal:** offscreen information begins with what the object is and its
current direction from the player. A pirate additionally communicates whether
the gap is shrinking or growing. These are candidate meanings, not selected
icons, labels or positions. This example tests their sufficiency for the four
originating problems before exploring presentation.

The pilot already knows the sun and derelict, has seen the collectible released,
and knows a pirate is present. The diagram reveals their authored positions;
the player cannot see them. It combines the four needs for
comparison without claiming they coexist in a particular production encounter.
The objects are outside the entire rendered view, not merely in its gutters.

</div>

  <figcaption>02.3 authored information diagram. Retained before type, distance and direction were selected as the next working set.</figcaption>
</figure>

<div className="combat-hud-prose">

**Hypothesis:** identity plus a current bearing lets the pilot distinguish a
charging opportunity from salvage or an opponent and orient toward the chosen
one. Updating that bearing could help follow moving energy without promising
an interception path. The pirate comparison exposes a limit of direction
alone: the same bearing can describe a closing or separating encounter.

**Meaning to judge:** “getting closer” means separation from the player is
currently decreasing, regardless of which ship's motion causes it. It does not
mean “attacking.” Stable separation and noisy transitions remain unresolved;
no speed thresholds or prediction rules are selected in this round.

**Limits:** this diagram establishes only what the candidate information means.
It does not demonstrate readable cues during flight or prove the set is
sufficient. Multiple objects of one kind, overlapping bearings, moving energy,
and decisions about distance remain open. Existing feedback must still be
accounted for before proposing additional cues. No layout, detection mechanic,
world-HUD change or production implementation is accepted.

**Decision · 19 November 2026:** proceed on the working assumption
that the layer presents three types of information: target type, distance and
direction. Arrange these on a circle inside the active camera area, for objects
outside the viewport. This selects the basis for the next example, not a final
visual design. Changing separation is not included in this three-part example;
the original interest in whether pirates are approaching remains unresolved.

**Grounding and departures:** A uses `COLOR.countdown`, the current world-HUD
ink. B proposes sun amber from `yellowStarPalette(70)`, neutral `COLOR.text`
for derelicts, `COLOR.resourcePower` for released energy and the existing
pirate-bearing purple. These proposed target meanings are study choices, not
established design-system roles. Both use the existing pirate marker geometry
as a constant reference; its use here does not select it as the final form.

**What this can reveal:** A makes the information layer visually coherent but
cannot distinguish target types. B can distinguish types after learning its
colour mapping; whether that tradeoff belongs in this HUD remains open. Similar
colours, varied backgrounds and colour-vision differences remain limitations
of colour-only information. No claim of play readability follows from this still.

**Held fixed:** the 02.5 sky and production Wasp, 1280 × 900 authored frame,
active-area containment, four offscreen targets and their circular bearings.
No world-HUD or flight-deck content moves. No production code changes.
