---
title: "Flight scene: one screen for seeing a burn in the game"
question: "What does one shared instrument look like that plays a fixed burn through the game’s own layers, so a study can judge how a ship, its effects and its weapons look in flight?"
phase: "scope"
status: "accepted"

<section className="historical-flight-record">

Historical record · accepted study, 10 November 2026. The live examples were
retired on 25 November after capturing their current implementation. Those
captures are **reconstructions**, not contemporaneous evidence of the original
judgments. Findings and uncertainties below retain their original scope; this
retirement makes no new design decisions. The [current Flight instrument](/lab/flight-scene)
has its own contract and may differ from these historical examples.

<h2 id="problem">01 / Every study builds its own window into the game</h2>

Engine, cannon and gravity studies built separate views with different backgrounds,
effects and controls. Some shared burns; others approximated physics or hull art.
The Light Ion Cannon study had already embedded a game view in Motion’s path pane
and replayed its light when seeking backward. A shared Flight instrument would
make production simulation and ordered rendering available for judging effects.

The central tension was inspectability against fidelity: light is fading history,
sparks and camera carry state, and automatic weapons depend on targets and power.
A time slider asks for an instant that the simulation can reach only by flying there.
Gravity and power mean its paths need not match Motion’s graphs.

Held fixed: Motion’s B1–B3 engine programs, 14 seconds, a time cursor, a full-window
snapping embed and study values pane. The scene is supplied by the study; exposed values
are adjusted in the instrument. Production art, physics and render order remain the source.
At framing, the order was copied from the flight screen; later layer work shared it.
Layout, replay, views, staging, layer isolation and single-ship comparison were open.
Manual piloting, fire inputs, migrating older studies and a permanent room were outside
this exploration.

Question check, 10 November: One subject and Consequence passed; Grounded failed
because the framing overstated earlier differences, omitted the cannon example,
and confused the fixed time cursor with open replay behavior. The framing was
corrected but not retested.

<h2 id="time">02.1 / Reach a jump by flying there</h2>

**Settled, 10 November:** play and seek both belong. A seek reflies from the start
and paints the whole flight’s light. The comparison exposed instant-only, last-second
and whole-flight painting; that diagnostic choice was not proposed as a normal control.

At nine B1 instants, last-second and whole-flight painting differed in at most 7 of
2,073,600 pixels. Instant-only painting differed in 800–16,000 pixels with engines
or cannon lit, losing plume and afterglow. An end-of-burn jump took about 0.2 seconds
on one machine, with whole-flight painting adding about a seventh. These measurements
predated the layer split and bloom. Continuous play and seek were **not** compared
pixel by pixel; the measurements do not establish the usefulness of stills for judging motion.

The fixture used Derelicts rules with encounter held off, production sun, planets,
boundary, historical Canvas starfield and camera. Starter Wasp carried a Light Ion
Cannon on its first hull mount, with two placed wrecks ahead and no randomness.
The first wreck broke within two seconds, leaving the cannon without a target;
the game camera made the ship small for most of the burn.

<h2 id="camera">02.2 / Game framing and two follow views</h2>

**Settled, 10 November:** Game, Follow and Follow 4×. Game retained framing around
ship and sun and five zoom steps. Follow centered the ship at the game’s closest
zoom, 1.6. Follow 4× rendered art and light at an enlarged scale the game never
reached, making finer edges and afterglow; a judgment there still needed a Game
check. Magnifying the game’s existing pixels was compared and rejected. Changing
view replayed the burn because the effects surface was camera-bound.

<h2 id="scene">02.3 / Stage the start, targets and visible layers</h2>

**Settled, 10 November:** configuration chooses starting position and heading, and
no targets, placed wrecks or a wreck held 140 units ahead. The held target is a Lab
substitution: it follows each tick and is replaced when destroyed. Cannon, damage,
break and shards are production behavior; held-target shards expire after two
seconds to avoid burying the view. Placed wrecks retain normal shard behavior.
World, FX and LFX can each be hidden; rendering continues beneath the visibility switch.

A single reading of the running page on 10 November found B3 lost in the sun near
10s from Run start; from Close to sun, B2 near 7s and B3 near 14s. All three completed
from Far out. These observations were not repeated. The scene reports ship loss;
a configuration must choose a start its burns survive.

<h2 id="layout">02.4 / The scene is the screen</h2>

**Settled, 10 November:** a 1440 × 900, 16:10 scene fills the instrument. Burn and
time float over the lower edge, view sits top-left and values top-right. A 16:9
scene above controls and a 4:3 scene beside a permanently visible rail were compared
and rejected; the rail cost about two fifths of the picture. The retained values
menu covered the right half while open, an accepted compromise.

<h2 id="ships">02.5 / One ship</h2>

**Settled, 10 November:** one ship per instrument. Comparisons use successive
instruments rather than halving the large picture. This follows from 02.4; no
separate example was built. B1–B3 run at 60 ticks per second without randomness.
Follow zooms, held distance and shard lifetime were working values, not tuned ones.

<h2 id="readiness">03 / Readiness at closure</h2>

Production simulation and the shared ordered passes on World, FX and LFX were
reusable, as were Motion’s controls, pane and band. The study engine supplied replay,
views, staging and variable render width. Delivery still needed one validated
configuration, a shared component, study values content, a reference page and its
own tests. Lab-side staging through mutable game fields was explicitly accepted
on 10 November, guarded by instrument tests; no game staging API was requested.
Finished studies were intended to retain captures independent of later code.

The original readiness assessment left these limitations:

- Engine light looked like a ladder of stamps at Follow 4×, limiting trail judgment;
  fixing it belonged elsewhere.
- Seek painted once per tick, play once per drawn frame. Their pixels could differ.
- Bloom size against the ship after display scaling was unchecked.
- Replay cost after layer split/bloom and pages with multiple instruments were unmeasured.
- Burns could end in the sun. Follow values, target distance and shard lifetime still
  needed a configuration decision; the large values menu could obstruct frequent tuning.

The page at 1280px, visible-window playback speed, and every B2/B3 view combination
were not checked in this study. These are historical uncertainties, not fresh
measurements or claims about the current instrument.

<h2 id="scope">04 / Adoption map recorded at closure</h2>

The proposal was one configured component, a Lab reference page and a study embed,
with no design-principle change and no player-visible change. Its mapped work was:

1. Define a Zod configuration for loadout, staging, targets, working zooms and opening
   burn/view/time; move engine and 02.4 screen into shared owners with a study pane.
   Verify invalid input, deterministic replay, backward seek, held-target reach and
   differing held/placed shard lifetimes.
2. Publish the default instrument in the Lab and move its browser checks there,
   covering layers/blending, play/pause, seek, views and visibility at 1280 and 1440px.
3. Share the full-window snapping embed with Motion and document its use for studies.
4. Measure seek versus play, bloom against production, end-of-burn cost and three
   simultaneous instruments; record differences instead of silently assuming fidelity.

The mapping placed configuration first, reference page and embed next, measurements
last. It excluded the stamp defect, older-study migration, capture retirement,
manual flight, weapon-input burns and split-screen comparison. These were the
original proposal’s limits, not instructions to preserve obsolete apparatus today.
